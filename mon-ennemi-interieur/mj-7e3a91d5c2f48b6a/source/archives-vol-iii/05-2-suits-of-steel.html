## <a class="line-anchor" id="L4"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L5"></a>Suits of Steel
<a class="line-anchor" id="L6"></a>Armour is a vital part of the toolkit most Characters will acquire in order to protect themselves during the course of the adventures. The rules for armour presented in this chapter provide more options and detail to those given in **WFRP** pages 299 to 300. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L7"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L8"></a>**Looting Armour**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L9"></a>During the course of their adventures Characters are more likely to come across armour on the bodies of fallen foes and companions rather than commissioning a fitted suit for themselves. This is a great way to save money, but it does mean that many Characters will be wearing armour that was not made for them, and that will therefore be rather poorly fitted. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L10"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L11"></a>Whenever a piece of armour is acquired by a Character for whom it was not made, the GM may decide that any given piece of armour will or will not fit (if it was made for a different Species, or a person with a much larger or smaller frame, for example). If it is uncertain whether or not a particular piece might suit a Character, roll on the following table:

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L12"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L15"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L16"></a>**Suitable Fit**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L17"></a>The armour is a good fit for the looting Character and may be worn as if it were made for them. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L18"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L19"></a>**Poor Fit**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L20"></a>The armour is outsized and uncomfortable. It counts as imposing its full ENC even when worn. The armour could be adjusted as per the rules for repairing armour. In order to do this it would need to be comprehensively refitted — this can be done using the rules for repairing armour, just as if their piece in question had been reduced to 0 AP in all locations. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L21"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L22"></a>**Won’t Fit**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L23"></a>The armour is much too large or too small to be worn by the Character at all. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L24"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L25"></a>If any particular Character does not find a set of armour suitable, the GM may decide that other Characters could roll on the chart to see how well the armour might fit them. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L26"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L27"></a>**Armour Damage**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L28"></a>As blows rain down on your armour it can be damaged, possibly permanently. Whenever you are instructed to damage a piece of armour, the APs in the location are reduced by 1. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L29"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L30"></a>If this lowers the APs to 0 in that location, the armour there is rendered useless. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L31"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L32"></a>Armour is generally damaged in one of two ways. 

1. <a class="line-anchor" id="L33"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L34"></a>A special ability triggers, such as a Spell or Talent, damaging a piece of armour. 

2. <a class="line-anchor" id="L35"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L36"></a>A Critical Wound is deflected. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L37"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L38"></a>**Armour and Stealth Tests**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L39"></a>Wearing any Chainmail or Plate imposes a penalty of –10 to any Stealth Tests.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L42"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L43"></a>**Critical Deflection**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L44"></a>If a Character suffers a Critical Wound from an incoming attack on a location protected by armour, they can choose to let their armour be damaged by 1 AP to ignore the Critical Wound. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L45"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L46"></a>They still suffer all normal Wounds (and given their APs are now 1 point lower, they probably suffer an additional Wound), but they avoid the Critical Wound effects as the blow is absorbed by their now destroyed armour. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L47"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L48"></a>When it comes to Critical Wounds caused by reducing your Wounds to below zero, sacrificing armour in this way only has an effect if it prevents Wounds being reduced to below zero. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L49"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L50"></a>**Repairing Armour**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L51"></a>When it comes to damaged armour, there are two ways to approach repairing it. A Character may use the Trade Skill to repair their own armour, or that of their companions, if they have the appropriate Trade Skill, Trade Tools and, for plate armour, a Workshop. Alternatively, damaged armour may be left with an NPC artisan who possesses the Skill to repair it. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L52"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L53"></a>**Repairing Armour as an Endeavour**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L54"></a>A Character who does not have time or expertise to repair their armour during the course of an adventure could try to do so as an Endeavour during downtime. In order to do so they must have access to suitable tools (and a forge or workshop if working with plate). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L55"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L56"></a>The Character may then take a single **Average (+20) Trade** Test — repairing 1 AP of armour for every SL they score on the Test. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L57"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L58"></a>**Asking an NPC to Repair Armour**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L59"></a>If a suitable NPC smith is asked to repair armour it costs 10% of the armour’s base price per AP lost to repair it. So, if you had a Sleeved Chainmail Coat with 1 AP of damage on the Body, and 1 AP of damage on the Arms, it will cost 20% of the armour’s base cost to fix it, in this case 12/–. If a section of your armour is completely broken, it will cost you 30% of the armour's base cost to fix, and you’ll likely be waiting some time before it is completed. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L60"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L61"></a>**Repairing Armour with the Trade Skill**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L62"></a>A Character may attempt to repair a piece of armour if they have the appropriate Skill and meet other criteria depending on the piece of armour.

<a class="line-anchor" id="L63"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L66"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L67"></a>They must then carry out an **Extended Challenging (+0) Trade** Test in order to repair the armour. The Test can be carried out once every hour, and the number of SLs required in order to repair a piece of armour depends on the type of armour being repaired. This represents the amount of time it takes to restore 1 AP to one location.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L68"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L75"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L76"></a>**Armour Rules**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L77"></a>Armour is listed in the following format: 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L78"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L79"></a>**Armour Type:** Each armour is listed according to the material it is constructed from, in order of its effectiveness. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L80"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L81"></a>**Price:** The price to buy an average piece of the armour. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L82"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L83"></a>**Enc:** The Encumbrance of the armour. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L84"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L85"></a>**Availability:** The Availability of the armour. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L86"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L87"></a>**Penalty:** Any penalty for wearing the specific piece of armour in addition to Encumbrance. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L88"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L89"></a>**Locations:** The Hit Locations the armour protects. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L90"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L91"></a>**APs:** The number of Armour Points the armour provides to the protected Hit Locations. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L92"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L93"></a>**Qualities and Flaws:** Any Qualities or Flaws that the armour may possess. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L94"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L95"></a>**Armour Qualities**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L96"></a>Armour can possess the following Armour Qualities. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L97"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L98"></a>**Impenetrable**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L99"></a>The armour is especially resilient, most attacks simply cannot penetrate it. All Critical Wounds caused by attacks scoring an odd number to hit you, such as an 11 or 33, are ignored. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L100"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L101"></a>**Overcoat**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L102"></a>Certain types of armour can be worn on top of other types. Brigandine pieces and Plate Breastplates can be worn on top of either Leather or Mail armour. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L103"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L104"></a>**Reinforced**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L105"></a>Some Soft Kits are designed with sections of mail placed to compensate for chinks in a full suit of plate armour. If a kit like this is worn under plate armour, that plate armour loses the _Weakpoints_ Flaw. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L106"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L107"></a>**Visor**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L108"></a>Some helmets come with a visor that can be raised to provide a wider field of vision. If the visor is closed the helmet performs as described. If the visor is opened the helmet loses any special abilities associated with its design and gains the _Partial_ Flaw. Perception Tests are made with a –10% penalty when visors are open. It takes an Action to open or close a visor. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L109"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L110"></a>**Armour Flaws**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L111"></a>Some armours have a point of weakness, as described by Armour Flaws. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L112"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L113"></a>**Partial**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L114"></a>The armour does not cover the entire Hit Location. An opponent who rolls an even number to determine hit location, or a Critical Hit, ignores the partial armour’s APs. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L115"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L116"></a>**Requires Kit**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L117"></a>Armour with this flaw can only be worn if a Soft Kit is worn underneath it. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L118"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L119"></a>**Weakpoints**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L120"></a>The armour has small weakpoints where a blade can slip through. If your opponent has a weapon with the _Impale_ Quality and scores a Critical, any APs of your armour are ignored. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L121"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L122"></a>**Combining Armour**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L123"></a>Different grades of armour may be worn on different parts of the body, but the notion that different grades of armour may be layered on top of one another is a misconception. Often suits of plate are finished with areas of mail or leather to cover gaps and points of articulation. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L124"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L125"></a>Soft kits are woollen doublets, hose, and padding for the head designed to be worn under armour. They provide cushioning needed to prevent chafing, as well as laces and holes to attach pieces of armour to. They can be worn under any armour and must be worn under certain pieces of plate. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L126"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L127"></a>Boiled leather is constructed from thick pieces of hide. It is rigid and tough and cannot be worn under mail or the more complicated pieces of plate. Only brigandine armour or a plate breastplate can be worn over boiled leather. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L128"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L129"></a>Chainmail is made from thousands of interlocking iron rings. It is heavy and requires regular cleaning and oiling to prevent corrosion. Only brigandine armour or a plate breastplate can be worn over chainmail. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L130"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L131"></a>Brigandine consists of padded leather or fabric into which many small metal strips, or lames, have been riveted. Brigandine pieces are bulky and cannot be worn under other forms of armour. Lamellar armour, or scale mail, may look rather different to brigandine, but has the same qualities. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L132"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L133"></a>Plate armour is painstakingly crafted from sheets of iron or steel. It is tailor made and close-fitting, so may not be worn over any type of armour other than soft kits. Breastplates are an exception to this rule, and can be worn over boiled leather or chainmail. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L134"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L135"></a>_Example: Alphonse is looking for as much protection as possible, and has the coin to get it. He can wear a reinforced soft kit with aventail, granting 1 AP to every hit location. A chainmail shirt grants him 2 AP to his body. He chooses plate bracers and leggings, along with a plate breastplate and great helm, granting him 3 AP to each location. In total, this grants him 4 AP to head, legs, and arms, and 6 AP to his body. Unfortunately the complicated plate bracers, leggings, and helm do not allow for the chainmail that grants him the additional 2 AP to his body._

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L202"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L203"></a>**Helmet Designs**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L204"></a>There are a great many designs of helmet used in conjunction with plate armour, and Tilean, Bretonnian, and Imperial armourers all have their preferred designs. There is little practical difference between these designs, they are all strong, fully enclosed helms with visors which weigh much the same and provide the same sort of protection. However, in some circumstances particular designs may afford extra protection. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L205"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L206"></a>**Great Helm**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L207"></a>The Great Helm is the archetypal form of a fully enclosed metal helmet. They are considered old fashioned in Tilea and the Empire but are still common in Bretonnia. Early examples of these helmets had a square top. Later designs tend to have a conical top which lessens the impact of hammers and swords. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L208"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L209"></a>**Bascinet**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L210"></a>The Bascinet typically incorporates a conical visor, leading it to be known as a ‘pigs-snout’ helmet by many soldiers. This design provides additional protection against missile fire, as slingshots and arrows are deflected by the conical visor. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L211"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L212"></a>If missile fire strikes the wearer of a Bascinet, and originates from in front of the wearer, then the helmet provides 4 APs rather than 3. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L213"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L214"></a>**Armet**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L215"></a>The Armet is a Tilean design which fits closely on the head, being narrower around the neck. To facilitate the snug fit the helmet has an integral mechanism that must be worked to open and close it. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L216"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L217"></a>The exacting design of the Armet means that it can withstand blows that might damage other helmets. Every time the helmet might lose a point of AP, roll on the Armet Damage table.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L225"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L226"></a>**Sallet**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L227"></a>The Sallet is typified by having heavy plates projecting over the neck to the back, and being combined with separate pieces, either a gorget or bevor, to provide protection to the throat, chin, and face. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L228"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L229"></a>A wearer of a Sallet who takes a Critical Hit to the head will take 1 less Wound from the Critical Hit than they otherwise would.
