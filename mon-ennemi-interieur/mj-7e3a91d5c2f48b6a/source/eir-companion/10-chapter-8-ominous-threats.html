# <a class="line-anchor" id="L1"></a>CHAPTER 8 Ominous Threats

<a class="line-anchor" id="L2"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L3"></a>*Pages PDF 60-64*

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L4"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L5"></a>Chapter 8 Ominous Threats
<a class="line-anchor" id="L6"></a>Throughout **The Enemy Within** , Characters are faced with some of the most insidious and horrifying threats in existence. From Daemonic entities beneath the streets of Bogenhafen to towering Ratogres, the Characters have overcome it all. By now, in fact, they may have become rather assured of their combat prowess. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L7"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L8"></a>This chapter exists to completely disabuse them of such notions, and to make sure they know true peril is never too far away. From the slops of the Black Mountains to the walls of Deisdorf, threats pursue them that would chill the heart of any vaunted hero of the Empire, and cleave a path of ruin and blood through entire armies. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L9"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L10"></a>**Pride Before the Fall**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L11"></a>This is all well and good. By now the Characters are quite experienced, and deserve to feel competent, even powerful. While a lucky crossbow bolt can critically injure even the strongest warrior, threats that would have seen off the hapless souls who stumbled across Kastor Lieberung’s body in **Enemy in Shadows** would now hardly pose a threat at all to most parties. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L12"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L13"></a>This is absolutely appropriate, even essential. Agents of powerful people who seek to save the very Empire itself should feel competent enough to at least have a chance of achieving such a lofty goal. However, such a group may also become somewhat lax in their approach to the dangers of the Old World. Assured of the calibre of their carefully nurtured skills, and perhaps with a Fate point or two in tow, they may scoff at the Goblins, Beastmen, and fanatics that assail them during **Empire in Ruins** . While the final battle with Sheeragetru will test even the most skilled Characters, your players may feel that they are otherwise safe. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L14"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L15"></a>Caveat Emptor
<a class="line-anchor" id="L16"></a>The creatures listed here are particularly dangerous, and especially in the cases of the Giant Squig and Undead Colossus are more suitable as foes for armies rather than parties of adventurous vagabonds. While suggestions are made as to how to fairly use these beasts in the campaign, some caution is warranted. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L17"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L18"></a>Recall that as a GM your goal should be to ensure everyone at the table, yourself included, have a good time. This should be achieved in concert with your players, and except when putting yourself in the mind of an enemy NPC, you should not be antagonistic towards your players. Wiping out the party with an unexpected and overwhelming threat may seem briefly exciting, but it is not dramatic, and does not make for a satisfying game. Give your Characters the means to succeed, and give them room to succeed or fail as their wits decree — do not set them up to fail from the outset. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L19"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L20"></a>OMINOUS THREATS 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L21"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L22"></a>**Chilling Horror**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L23"></a>Mournguls are terrors feared above almost all other dreadful things that haunt the hinterlands of the Old World. They cling to the vast expanses of emptiness that surround many more populated regions, their shill cries echoing through mountain ranges and the chill tundra’s of the North. They are a thing neither alive nor dead, but occupying a liminal space between both. The unstable, corrupting magic of Dhar swirls about the beasts, and even the most headstrong Necromancer knows that it is almost always folly to attempt to bind a Mourngul, despite the obvious advantages controlling one would provide. Indeed Mornguls are feared by all save Ogres, who have no particularly opinion of the beasts and who seem not to be frequently preyed upon by them. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L24"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L25"></a>It is said that, in the very worst winters when the snow drifts smother whole cottages and the bitter chill reaches into even the warmest hearth, Mournguls descend from their frigid peaks and windswept plains to terrorise the villages of the Old World. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L26"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L27"></a>**Encountering a Mourngul**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L28"></a>Mournguls haunt only the coldest and most remove places of the Old World. Some are known to dwell in the highest peaks of the Black Mountains, and should an unseasonable blizzard strike the Characters may well find themselves facing on in or around Blackfire Pass. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L29"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L30"></a>Many myths claim that Mournguls are the remanent of those who slew and devoured their companions to survive while stranded in the wilds. Others say that while this may have been true of the first Mourngul, most that exist today are surely victims of the creatures who refused to die, but were infected with the longing hunger of their attacker. Whatever the case, they are truly terrible to behold, a gaping may of razor teeth, dead flesh, and frostbitten crawls that rend and devour whatever warm flesh they can find. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L31"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L32"></a>**Mournguls**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L33"></a>Horrific, half-living creatures that haunt the fright heights and wildernesses of the world, Mournguls are a fearful sight. In place of legs, a cavernous wound lies open like a gaping maw. Condemned to an eternity of insatiable hunger and bitter cold, they are a foe without mercy. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L34"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L35"></a>**Traits:** Armour 4 (9), Bite +9, Champion, Chill Grasp,  Dark Vision, Immunity (Cold), Immunity to Psychology, Magical, Stealthy, Terror 2, Vampiric, Ward 8+, Size (Large), Weapon (Frigid Claws) +13 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L36"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L37"></a>**Betrayal Incarnate**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L38"></a>The Curs’d Ettins are a species of monstrous beasts, larger that even the broadest Ogre, and possessed of a fury and hatred that is second to none. It is said that, long ago, these foul creatures were mortal Norscans who served the Ruinous Powers and profited mightily from it. However, when the day came to invade the soft southern lands, the progenitors of the Curs’d Ettins instead chose to reave the lands of their neighbours who had answered the call to arms. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L39"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L40"></a>Perhaps proving that there is loyalty even among the servants of the dark gods, a curse fell on the traitors, and the Curs’d Ettins were born. All those who have since crossed paths with the creatures have died in an display of primal blood and rage, or fled to carry tales of the dread giants to others. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L41"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L42"></a>**Encountering an Ettin**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L43"></a>The creatures know no master, save those who can bind them — often only temporarily — by magic. Should the Purple Hand in Altdorf come to truly appreciate the threat the Characters pose to their plans, then either Zuntermein or Wasmeire may well set such a beast upon them. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L44"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L45"></a>If Zuntermein, the attack will come while the Characters are out of Altdorf, perhaps traveling to Castle Reikguard or further South. If Wasmeire, the attack will come whenever he sees the opportunity — such as while the Characters pursue him after the assassination attempt. Quite how the ex-Law Lord managed to sneak such a beast into the city is another matter. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L46"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L47"></a>In either case the Curs’d Ettin is a terrible foe, and one certain to challenge most parties. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L48"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L49"></a>**' Curs D Ettins**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L50"></a>Enormous creatures of twisted flesh and hateful soul, Curs'd Ettins are the remanants of a once mighty tribe who lost the favour of the Dark Gods. They now rove the land serving no one master, but seeking to quench their endless rage in a torrent of blood and powdered bone.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L61"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L62"></a>**Prize of the Pits**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L63"></a>A Colossal Squig is a beast which, by great endurance or simple forgetfulness, never stopped growing. They are most often bred by particularly ambitious goblin Shamans, though wild examples do occasionally emerge from unknown caves after they have finished devouring their smaller kin. Though at a distance their appearance is somewhat ridiculous, up close they are anything but, devouring whole regiments of pikemen before bounding away to sow havoc behind their own lines. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L64"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L65"></a>**Encountering a Colossal Squig**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L66"></a>There is much intrigue among the Goblins of the Madcraw tribe, and perhaps one of their number — most likely Cacklespit or one of his minions — has been breeding such a beast for the day they eventually arise to their rightful place atop the rest of goblinkind. After the great flood that follows the Character’s recovery of the hammer, the barricades that held back the great beast are washed away. The great aquig emerges blinking and hungry to challenge the Characters as they make their way back towards Black Fire pass. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L67"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L68"></a>**Colossal Squigs**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L69"></a>Mindless beasts with simple desires, Colossal Squigs are nevertheless difficult creatures to control. Most serve briefly as the favoured pet of an ambitious Goblins Shaman before escaping, some after devouring their erstwhile master. They are very much like their smaller cousins, beyond the obvious fact that they are much, much larger. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L70"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L71"></a>**Traits:** Bestial, Bounce, Night Vision, Size (Enormous), Weapon (Slobbering Mouth) +11 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L72"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L73"></a>**Messy Demise:** On death, the sinew and general muscle tension that holds a Colossal Squig together collapses, and so does the creature. Anyone within 3 yards of a Colossal Squig when it expires must make a **Challenging (+0) Dodge** Test or take 2d10 wounds, modified by Toughness and Armour Poins to a minimum of 1. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L74"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L75"></a>**Lumbering Graves**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L76"></a>Akin to walking siege towers, they radiate a miasma of deathly energies that whip the winds of magic into a veritable gale wherever they go. From within the cages of iron that make up their frame, the grasping claws of undead corpses grab any who get too close. In the unlikely event that a Necrofex Colossus should be destroyed, those undead corpses still sufficiently intact to do so swarm forth from the shattered hull. Maddened by the torment of their former imprisonment, they will devour any living creature they can reach — and have frequently ended the celebrations of those responsible for slaying the Colossus in bouts of blood and screaming death.. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L77"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L78"></a>An horrific fusion of necromantic power and unnatural craftsmanship, only a handful of Necromancers in the history of the Old World have been able to create one of the abominations known as a Necrofex Colossus. Lumbering constructs of blood, bone, metal and unliving corpses, these towering monuments to the mastery of undeath are a terrifying foe. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L79"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L80"></a>**Encountering a Necrofex Colossus**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L81"></a>If you are using the optional Gravelord adventurers as part of your campaign, the Necrofex Colossus represents the pinnacle of what the Gravelord might be able to achieve. Truly the colossus is more suitable to appearing on a battlefield than as an encounter on the road, and you should keep this in mind if you wish to make use of it. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L82"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L83"></a>The Siege of Streissan (page 112) is the ideal place to deploy the abomination. As Gräber gathers his forces to attack, a lumbering figure approaches the walls, quickly revealing itself to tower above them. It begins to tear the walls to pieces, creating the perfect opening for the Gravelord's forces to advance while he attacks the University. Marius Leitdorf's forces can occupy the thing for a time, but without the Characters intervention it is sure to reduce Streissan to so much rubble and smouldering ruin. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L84"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L85"></a>**Necrofex Colossus**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L86"></a>The pinacle of Necromantic reanimation, the Necrofex colossus is a towering construction of bone, muscle, sinew, and blasphemous power. Few are the Necromancers capable of creating one, and fewer still are those who can retain control one once it has been unleashed. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L87"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L88"></a>**Traits:** Construct, Distracting, Dark Vision, Magical, Size (Monstrous), Terror 2, Undead, Weapon+13 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L89"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L90"></a>**Suffused with Death:** The necromantic magics that sustain the colossus warp the winds of magic within 80 yards of the creature. Any successful attempts to cast spells from the lores of Death or Necromancy gain +1 SL.  Such is the power of this vortex of Death that attempts to cast spells or of the Lores of Ghyran, Hysh or Azyr suffer a -10 penalty within 80 yards of the creature. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L91"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L92"></a>**Garden of Corpses:** Should the Colossus be defeated, 3d10 zombies spring forth from its remains, attacking any living thing nearby. **Optional:** Spellcaster
