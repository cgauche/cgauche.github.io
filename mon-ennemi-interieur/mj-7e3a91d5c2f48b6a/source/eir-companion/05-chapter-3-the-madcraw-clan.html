Chapter 3 the Madcraw Clan

The Madraw Clan are a major barrier to the Characters reaching their goal of recovering Ghal Maraz. Quite how major depends on how well the party can negotiate with the tribes various power-players, from the prophetic Cacklespit to his various disloyal underlings.

Goblins in Cacklespit’s position often reach far beyond their meagre grasp, and it would not at all be unfitting for him to attempt such a thing despite the advantages that Ghal Maraz would grant the Characters in the battle that would ensue from such an attempt.

Exploring the Clan

More could be made of the Tribe, however. Cacklespit’s insight has given the Goblin some insight into things far beyond the knowledge of most Goblins. While Ghal Maraz is a terrible force of destruction to the average Orc or Goblin, a cunning leader such as Cacklespit will know that such a source of power could be put to many uses. Perhaps he can direct the Characters to smite some of his more established enemies in other Orc and Goblin clans, or even have the Characters recover the weapon only for him to steal it for himself.

As written, the Madcraw Clan are all but washed away by the cleansing flood unleashed after the Hammer is recovered. If you wish to make use of some of this material, or the Colossal Squig on page 63, consider reducing the severity of the flood. The Madcraw Tunnels remain more or less intact, and whoever the Characters aided awaits their return — either Cacklespit himself, or one of this juniors who learned of his plan to seize the hammer and decided it was worth a try.

If nothing else, it will give the players an opportunity to explore the abilities of Ghal Maraz and see if it lives up to its Khazalid name of 'Skull-Splitter.'

Olde Weirde’s Incunabulum Thoughts on some most peculiar Goblins

I’ll admit to occasions where I’ve staggered back from the Dancing Dwarf after several goblets of port and, apparently, serenaded Frau Weirde with old Ostermarker folk songs. But never would I touch a stronger intoxicant, especially after meeting Otto Practmann, a Nulner merchant who recounted a horrendous episode involving a tribe of Goblins in the Black Mountains. Prachtmann was in Altdorf seeking solace in the Grand Cathedral of Sigmar. He met Professor Pfaff there, and Pfaff told him of my wide-ranging interest in the many species of the Old World and suggested he unburden his troubles to me.

Prachtmann was travelling in a trade caravan through Black Fire Pass, when one night his wagon was attacked. He hid in a large trunk of silks, but his very place of concealment was claimed as loot. He remained undiscovered until the raiding Greenskins reached their lair.

His abductors were Night Goblins, a breed of nocturnal Greenskin that wear dark robes to protect their skin from sunlight. When the trunk was opened, Prachtmann saw horrible faces looking down at him, with long noses poking from under cruel red eyes. He was hauled from the trunk into a dingy cavern where a Goblin shaman sat on a large mushroom lit by the eerie blue light of luminescent fungi. He later learned that this was Cacklespit, a shaman who rules over the tribe. Several more shamans leered from the shadows, while other Night Goblins cackled and burbled, half-chewed mushrooms falling from their slack mouths. (The stench of halitosis and earthy fungi and the sound of deep chuckling still returns to him late at night.)

A burly Goblin leapt forward to lop off Prachtmann’s head, but Cacklespit bade him stop. ‘Mansplatta,’ the big Goblin, muttered and spat, but Mansplatta did as his master ordered.

‘I seen you before,’ said Cacklespit in broken Reikspiel. ‘I seen you in me visions. You’re me ride.’

And so Otto Prachtmann of Banker’s Row, Nuln, became a mount for Cruzzik Cacklespit of the Madcraw Clan. The shaman ordered him stripped and painted with swirling red and green patterns. Prachtmann showed me a brand on his chest — a large stylised bird’s head that is the tribe’s glyph. From thenceforth, when Cacklespit wanted to leave his throne, he climbed atop Prachtmann’s shoulders and rode to his destination, putting on great airs and graces. (The physical sensation is still acute, Prachtmann had to get up and move his shoulders and straighten his back as he recalled this, sometimes kneeling down as if to allow his mount to dismount.)

It was a horrific time for the Nulner merchant and he has my greatest sympathy. On the other hand, he learned a great deal about the Madcraw Clan, which, of course, is truly fascinating to a scholar such as myself. But he reacted badly when I suggested there was an upside to his experiences. I didn’t press the matter further.

The tribe dwells in a hidden gorge somewhere east of Blackfire Pass. They worship the Greenskin deities Mork and Gork, but also have some totemic attraction to the raptors of the Black Mountains. As a spiritual leader, the shaman Cacklespit achieved his exalted position through visions and prophecies that claimed a great destiny for the tribe.

There are several distinct groups within the Madcraw Clan. Cacklespit was the shaman boss, with a small coven of subservient shamans to do his bidding. These spellcasters conjure the violent magicks inherent to Greenskins. Most were just toadies, but ‘Moongobba’ was Cacklespit’s lieutenant, and a shifty character even by Goblin standards.

The general run of the clan were Night Goblins, alternately awed and cowed by Cacklespit. An argumentative rabble, Prachtmann noticed that they enjoyed vicious pranks and unprovoked nastiness. On several occasions they tried to torment him, but Cacklespit reserved this right for himself. More than one Goblin lost a digit or eye for taunting their warboss’s mount.

Then there are the so-called ‘loonies’. These fanatics gobble varieties of mushroom to bring on intoxication and frenzy. In the Madcraw Clan, they believe this helps them emulate their leader, but it chiefly made them do great harm to themselves and others. If I were less pious, I might compare them with the flagellant bands that traipse after our own armies.

Mansplatta’s gang of toughs are the Skargobbos. Stronger and bigger than the other Goblins, they throw their weight around and bully the rest of the tribe. When the clan raids a caravan, attacks travellers or makes war with other Greenskins, Mansplatta and his mob are at the fore.

Cacklespit sounds like a fascinating character. He claims to see visions of the past and future when under the influence of a rare purple fungus. (The Purple Cap mushrooms are found only in the Northern Gorge. They give off an odour of heavy

rain.) After eating these mushrooms, he falls into a trance and babbles in many tongues — Reikspiel, Khazilid, Classical and several Prachtmann did not know. When he emerges from his delirium, the shaman paints the walls with visions of strange scenes surrounded by swirling patterns. On occasion, he summoned the tribe to recount what he saw, whipping them into a baying hysteria.

Prachtmann noticed that Moongobba coveted the Purple Cap mushrooms, but Cacklespit ensured that no other Goblin ate them. One evening at twilight, the shamans travelled to the mouth of the Northern Gorge which is the only place the Purple Caps spore. The tribe fear this region and keep away, but to make a point to his acolytes, Cacklespit rode Prachtmann into the valley and plucked a handful of Purple Caps from the wall. Back in his throne room, he ate them in front of the shamans and, to add insult to injury, shoved one into Prachtmann’s protesting mouth.

In the warm comforts of Prachtmann’s home, I listened in disbelief and sadness as I realised that the man’s mind had cracked. The visions he recounted were absurd — frogs from the sky, Sigmar wrestling a giant bird, a three-eyed king… gibberish. The poor man’s mind was permanently twisted.

When Prachtmann came to, Cacklespit was still in a trance and Moongobba held the end of the merchant’s chain. He dropped it and winked. This was some act of spite on the part of the shaman, but it was also the opportunity Prachtmann needed. He escaped into the daylight, blinking and stumbling, finding his feet eventually and fled through the mountains. Days later he was found half-dead by a Dwarf patrol, collapsed in a stream.

A fantastical tale, perhaps, but it tallies with what I know of the Night Goblins. Sigmar help us should Cacklespit and his Madcraw Goblins ever bring their insanity to the Empire.

The Madcraw Clan

Cruzzik Cacklespit keeps them in his thrall with a mixture of underhand violence and insane prophecies promising a glorious and ill-defined destiny for the tribe. But the shaman’s position is precarious, as Moongobba and Mansplatta vie for his position as Warboss.

In Empire in Ruins , the party encounters the Madcraw Clan in the Lost Gorge that leads to the resting place of Sigmar’s Hammer. This chapter presents more background and options for using the tribe in your campaign.

The Origins of the Madcraw Clan

The Goblins have little interest in how they arrived in the Lost Gorge. They sometimes tell tales about a great bird ‘sent by Mork’ that brought them to the valley, a folk memory of their origins. If the Characters eat a Purple Cap mushroom, they may experience time-bending visions that show them the origins of the tribe.

The Madcraw Clan’s ancestors were bosses from the Crookblades tribe, who dwell in the Worlds Edge Mountains. After intertribal squabbling, they fled north-west towards the Empire. In the Black Mountains they were captured by the Bloodied Nose tribe, an especially nasty band with a taste for sacrifice to appease the gods. But just as the bosses were about to have their hearts removed, a huge buzzard swept down and devoured their captors.

Convinced Mork sent the bird, the bosses followed the buzzard to its nest in the Lost Gorge. They showed their eternal gratitude by trapping the bird and eating its flesh, then settling in its home. The new Madcraw Clan took the image of the buzzard as its icon, a mean-looking bird with an open beak and projecting tongue.

The tribe grew over the years and while they didn’t prosper, they became well-established in the gorge. The Night Goblins learned to fear the north stretch of the valley, where they sensed something warped and wrong emanating from the cave. When the young shaman Cacklespit wanted to impress his fellows, he walked further north than any before.

In the Northern Gorge he found the Purple Cap mushrooms and ran home. That night, he tried one for the first time and experienced his first vision — a sight of the Madcraw Clan astride the ruins of Averheim. Within a few months, he used his mystical foresight to bring the whole tribe under his command.

Relationships

The clan are not restricted to the Lost Gorge. They range across the many mountain passes and valleys in the Black Mountains east of Black Fire Pass, looking for easy victims and other tribes as allies or enemies.

The Madcraw Clan covet the riches of merchants travelling to and from the southern Empire — which are a source of weapons, fine foods and gaudy fineries. They scout for the caravans with the weakest protection and raid under cover of darkness, using stealth and Night Vision to overwhelm the guards.

Cacklespit and his henchmen try to avoid Dwarfs and keep them away from the gorge. On occasion when Dwarf Rangers have strayed close by, the Madcraw scouts have proved cunning enough to lead them towards other Greenskins.

These other tribes are alternately trading partners and enemies. The Moonfang Tribe are a tribe of tough Hill Goblins to the south who occasionally trade or raid alongside the Madcraw clan. The Bloodied Nose have an ancestral dislike of the tribe and still hope to sacrifice its leaders in one of their bloodletting rituals. The Warboss Gnashrukk is one of Warlord Gorfang Rotgut’s Orcs. He is charged with watching the Black Fire Pass with his band. The Madcraw Clan fear him and prefer to stay out of his way.

Eating Purple Caps

These mushrooms are tainted by the magic emanating from the cave and eating them grants powerful visions of the past and future. Within d10 turns of consuming a Purple Cap the Character needs to make a test as if they had consumed a Piece or Weirdroot. If they succumb their eyesight dims and they see colourful patterns swirling through the air around them, reminiscent of Cacklespit’s paintings. Vague shapes, almost like lifeforms, swim through the air. Gradually, the Character begins to experience vivid visions.

Madcraw Characters

Cruzzik Cacklespit — the Shamanic leader of the Madcraw Goblins. Slik Moongobba — another shaman; the tall Moongobba wants to usurp Cacklespit and get the Purple Cap mushrooms for himself. Globnak Mansplatta — the tough boss of the Skargobbos. He has little patience for mystical claptrap and is desperate to lead the clan to war. These tribe members are outlined in Empire i n Ruins . The following are additional characters and types of Night Goblin in the Madcraw Clan.

Olgog Gristlefang

Olgog thinks he is deep in the jungle, tormented by blue-striped elephants and bejewelled tigers. From time to time, a skinny Goblin swims into view and provides a much needed meal. The Ogre can’t leave the jungle — whenever he reaches the edge of the trees, he finds himself back where he started.

Several months ago, the Madcraw Goblins launched an unsuccessful attack on a trade caravan. An Ogre guard pursued the fleeing Goblins into the mountains, where their fellows bombed him with toxic puffballs until he fell unconscious. Now Olgog is imprisoned in a cavern, permanently drugged by the hallucinogenic fungus growing from the walls. Cacklespit occasionally sends undesirable Goblins for Olgog’s meals — the shaman knows there’s a use for the ogre, but the visions have yet to reveal it to him.

The Green Racket

The Green Racket are five Night Goblin musicians who act as Cacklespit’s heralds and minstrels. They accompany him on his travels and sometimes play in the throne cavern for his entertainment. They make a horrible noise.

The Green Racket play an assortment of crude, discordant instruments — brass gongs made from looted shields, a battered iron horn, bagpipes fashioned from an inflated eagle’s corpse, and Dwarf skull drums. Urk Bleezug is the ugliest Goblin, who simply howls and bellows. The resulting wall of discordant noise excites the Goblins and helps Cacklespit focus his visions.

The Doom Divers

The Madcraw Clan has many fanatics, driven half-crazed by Mad Cap mushrooms and the lurid imagery of Cacklespit’s prophecies. Most fling themselves about with heavy iron balls on chains (see Empire in Ruins , page 143), but some believe they are destined to fly, powered by Mork’s will .

The Doom Divers fashion themselves wings from skins and feathers. Their favourite hobby is throwing themselves from the heights of the gorge, screeching an ear-piercing pitch, whilst trying to fly. The survivors learn to glide and have even built a catapult, powered by bird guts strung over a wooden frame. If the tribe goes to battle, the Doom Divers try to fire one another at the enemy.

Gitface

Night Goblins are spiteful trouble makers, but even they think Gitface is an irritating little sod.

Gitface likes pranks. He concocts a strong acid from bird guano mixed with fungal scrapings. Dumping this on another Goblin will strip its skin to the bone and cause endless howls of laughter from Gitface. Another favourite trick is sneaking up on a Goblin as he defecates and slicing off a part of his body with a wickedly sharp blade.

Only Gitface’s speed and agility keep him alive. He dwells alone in a small cavern high above the gorge, brewing his acid, sharpening his blade and coming up with more pranks.

Release the Squig!

While the Characters are just as likely to become the target of one of Gitface’s pranks as any of his fellows, the ingenious Night Goblin is not adverse to making use of outsiders as part of his dangerous japes. Rather than attacking the party, as described on page 34, Gitface instead attempts to recruit them to help in his most dangerous prank yet. Deep in the caves, he explains, someone’s been feeding a big old Squig (page 63), only Gitface has been lacing the food with his own enticing (to a squig) mixture of fungus and acid. Splashing anything at all with ‘Gitface’s Speshul Sauce’ will make it irresistible to the Squig, he is certain.

Gitface provides the Characters with some acid to melt the chains that keep the Squig’s cave gated shut, and then goes off to douse everyone and anyone in the his sauce. If the Characters think to ask he’ll provide them with a bottle of the stuff as well — this can only add to the ‘hilarity.’

Gitface will neglect to tell them quite how big the Squig is, and the creature keeps so far in the darkness that they’ll have no idea of its size before setting it free. The Squig will devour dozens of Goblins — more, if the Characters are liberal wiht their dose the sauce — before bounding off into the mountains to terrorize someone else.

Tactics

Further Developments

In Empire in Ruins , the Madcraw Clan are an obstacle to overcome en route to Ghal Maraz, but if you enjoy using them, they can live on in your campaign.

Empire in Ruins provides various combat tactics that the Madcraw Clan will use in battle. Here are a few more, with suggestions for when and where they’re most appropriate:

When the flood sweeps through the Lost Gorge it drowns many Goblins, but it clears a path to Black Fire Pass. Those who survive no longer feel safe in the gorge and there will be an exodus, led by whoever is in charge.

Birdstrike

(Dawn or Dusk, Lower Gorge, or Deep Ravine) The Goblins bait and catch mountain birds using food scattered across the floor of the gorge. If they see a hostile party approach, they bang gongs to drive the flock into their foes. Treat as a successful Flock of Doom Spell with an 8-yd area of effect and a duration of just one turn ( WFRP , page 245).

If Cacklespit suppressed an attempted coup, he roasts the conspirator alive and feeds them to the tribe. He attributes his survival to his prophetic powers and the whole tribe fall in line, awed by his powers. He decides that the time is right to stock up on Purple Cap mushrooms and travel north to raid Averland.

Dive Bombing

(All Day, Lower Gorge)

Three Doom Divers leap from the top of the cliffs or fire themselves from their catapult. They take two turns to reach their target. Each turn, take an Average (+20) Athletics Test for each Goblin. If it fails, it hits the wall of the gorge, crashes to the ground or its wings fall off — no longer a threat. Any Doom Diver still in play after two turns hits a randomlyselected target with +12 Damage, unless they pass a Difficult (–10) Athletics Test.

If Mansplatta deposes Cacklespit, he feels the pull of the Orcs massing in the Badlands. The Madcraw migrate south into the Border Princes and, after years of raids and plunder, they join Waaagh! Vorgaz.

If Moongobba leads the tribe, he takes a huge amount of Purple Cap mushrooms and loses his mind. On his orders, the other shamans push every Goblin into the Northern Gorge, where they indulge in an orgy of Purple Cap consumption. This drives them insane. Many die, but the lingering effect of Sheerargetru’s presence causes some survivors to mutate. Some grow small purple feathers. Others develop very pronounced purple beaks or their limbs turn into rubbery fungal growths. Moongobba urges the twisted tribe to fulfill their destiny in conquering the Empire.

Unleash Olgog!

(Daylight, Deep Ravine)

If a hostile force reaches the Deep Ravine and wipes out many Goblins, Cacklespit orders that Ulgog is released from his cave. The hallucinating Ogre stumbles from confinement, desperate for a decent feed. He charges the party — treat him as Frenzied . If any Goblin strays close enough, he’ll attack them too.

On the Road

Eclectic Cruel Blade Acid Pest

If they go north, the Madcraw Tribe travels by night in a strangle cavalcade. Cacklespit (or Moongobba) rides in a shaded howdah on Olgog’s back, while the Green Racket comes after, yowling of their shaman’s great deeds. The tribe follows on foot or in crude carts filled with dried fungi and pulled by runts. Should they meet other Greenskins, they will try to convert them to following Cacklespit (or Moongobba’s) prophecies. Anyone else they will attack, with fanatics at the fore.

(Dusk, Night and Dawn, Deep Ravine)

The Characters are a perfect target for Gitface’s pranks. At an opportune moment he hides on a ledge and throws acid bombs down on them. He is 10 yd above and counts as a Small target in Medium Cover, so any attempt to hit him with missile weapons is Very Hard (–30). At night, he attempts to sneak up and slice at them from the shadows with his acid-poisoned blade. If anyone gets the chance to fight back, he flees, laughing to himself and shouting ‘ Are you mad?’ in the Goblin language.

When they reach the first human settlement (such as Grenzstadt), they wait until nightfall to dose the wells with Mad cCp mushrooms and other intoxicants. At dusk the next night they strike, taking advantage of the many incapacitated townsfolk.
