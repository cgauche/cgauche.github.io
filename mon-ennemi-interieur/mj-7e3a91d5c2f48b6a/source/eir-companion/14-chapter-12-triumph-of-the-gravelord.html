CHAPTER 12 Triumph of the Gravelord

Pages PDF 102-118

Chapter 12 Triumph of the Gravelord

The Gravelord adventures can be used as a series of incidents that form a ‘B-plot’ to The Enemy Within campaign. With a little work, they can be more fully fleshed out and form a campaign in their own right.

Part One: The Best of Enemies can take place at any time when the party faces inevitable death at the hands of a superior foe. Having decided that he, and he alone, has the right to end the lives of his arch-enemies, Gräber saves them. With the Characters at bay, he delivers one of his grandiose speeches explaining that he will return to kill them when the time is right — when there is more of an audience to witness his victory. Screened by his Undead followers, he withdraws and the party is left to continue their quest.

The Story So Far

The Characters first encountered Hans Gräber in the Death on the Reik Companion . This aspiring necromancer was once a scholar-monk at the Sigmarite monastery near Dunkelberg, but was expelled for delving too deeply into the forbidden books of necromantic lore in the monastery’s library. Swearing revenge, Gräber devoted himself even more to the study of necromancy and began styling himself ‘the Gravelord’ in tribute to his idol, Heinrich Kemmler the Lichemaster. In Vengeance of the Gravelord , the Characters just happened to be on the same boat as the abbot of Gräber’s former monastery when the Gravelord decided to take his revenge.

Part Two: Dark Deeds in the Grey Mountains takes the form of a rumour rather than a direct encounter. While in Altdorf, the Characters hear of strange happenings far to the south near the Bretonnian border. Grognard Players might recognise the names of Frugelhofen and La Maisontaal, and a little research will lead the Characters to the legend of Heinrich Kemmler, the infamous Lichemaster. It seems that Gräber is following the trail of every famous ancient necromancer he can find.

Part Three: The (Second) Siege of Streissan occurs as the Characters attempt to escape Streissan, only to find the Gravelord has at last assembled his army and is marching on Streissan.

The next encounter with the Gravelord took place in the adventure Flying Death Skulls in the Power Behind the Throne Companion . Travelling from Altdorf to Middenheim, the Characters encountered a village terrorised by terrifying flying skulls. In solving the mystery, they met Gräber in person for the first time and learned that the attacks were revenge for another slight in his past.

Part Four: What Now? Discusses some options for continuing the adventure after Empire in Ruins , should the Gravelord survive.

In The Horned Rat Companion , Gräber happened to be crossing the Middle Mountains at the same time as the Characters, although for a completely different reason. Nonetheless, he became convinced that the Characters are Imperial agents of some sort, sent to stop him — for in his own mind, there can be no greater threat to the Empire than himself. In Return of the Gravelord , the Characters heards hints and rumours throughout the adventure before Gräber’s Undead forces attacked the balloon they used to travel back to Middenheim after thwarting the Skaven plot.

Running This Adventure

This adventure falls into five parts, which take place at various points during Empire in Ruins . Although each encounter happens entirely by coincidence — as far as the Characters are concerned, at least — Gräber becomes more and more convinced that the party is bent on thwarting him. After all, what greater threat could the Empire possibly face than the greatest necromancer the world has ever known?

The Gravelord Defeated

While it is more satisfying if Gräber escapes each encounter with his skin (and his considerable ego) intact, you may find that impossible to pull off without obviously cheating. If the Characters do something brilliant and Gräber has no plausible way to escape death, let them have their victory — and then surprise them by bringing him back for the next encounter. There are various ways to pull this off:

Fate Points: Fate points are one of the things that makes the Characters special, and not every NPC deserves them. However, they can be a great way of distinguishing a recurring nemesis NPC — a true arch-enemy — from the common herd. Gräber is persistent enough to merit a few Fate points of his own, and with a little ingenuity it may be possible to devise a plausible reason for him to survive apparent death at the hands of the Characters. His Undead followers may have standing instructions to take his body to safety if he falls, for example. If the Characters do not have any means of flying, the Winged Nightmare or the Carrion could take their master’s body out of reach fairly quickly, allowing Gräber to rest and heal before he appears again.

But He Won’t Lie Down: Behind his ridiculous voice and his rampant egomania, Gräber is a skilled necromancer. During his travels in the Middle Mountains on the trail of Dieter Helsnicht, Melkhior the Ancient, and Zacharias the Everliving, he could quite plausibly have uncovered some obscure necromantic ritual that allows him to survive death. The next time the Characters encounter the Gravelord, he could be Undead himself. Thanks to the ritual, he keeps all of his memories, Skills, Talents, spells, and other abilities, and gains the Undead Trait plus any other Traits you find interesting: Fear at level 2–4, Dark Vision , Painless , and even Ethereal are all possibilities.

You Look Different: Many Ghosts owe their origin to obsessive thoughts of vengeance, and disembodied spirits have been known to possess the bodies of the living, or to animate the bodies of the dead. On their next encounter, the Characters might not recognise Gräber right away, but his new body still speaks with a nasal whine and his speeches are just as bombastic as before.

Part One: the Best of Enemies

This encounter takes place during Chapter 4 of Empire in Ruins , as the Characters are on the road from Middenheim to Shining Rock, or afterwards, on the journey south to Altdorf. If you would like to weave the Gravelord subplot into your game, it is best to have the graf order the Characters to scout the road ahead, leaving them isolated.

As a result of their previous encounters, Gräber has decided that the Characters are his arch-enemies and that he must be the one to defeat them. Therefore, he must prevent anyone else from killing them, and according to his inflated sense of drama, he cannot simply kill them once they have been weakened by some

other enemy. The final conflict must come at a suitable time and place, with a large audience to witness his triumph. Therefore, this unlikely rescuer is on hand to save the Characters when a fight seems all but lost.

A likely moment would be when the Characters are scouting ahead of the main part of the caravan, well out of sight of any assistance. As the Characters make their way south, they are set upon by a group of Religious Fanatics, Nordlander Rebels, or another group with a bone to pick with the Characters or the graf of Middenheim and his servants. See Dramatis Personae on page 18 of Empire in Ruins for stat profiles.

The Undead

Before too long, all of the attackers are dead and many of them have been raised as Undead. There is a momentary lull as the Characters and the Undead face each other, and then the whining voice is heard again. Screened from sight by rank upon rank of Skeletons and Zombies, Gräber delivers one of his bombastic speeches. As he did in Altdorf (see Vengeance of the Gravelord in the Death on the Reik Companion ), he uses the spell Murmured Whisper to make his voice appear to come from the Skeleton or Zombie closest to the Characters.

Profiles for an assortment of Undead creatures are provided at the end of this chapter, and more can be found in Chapter 12 of the WFRP rulebook. The exact makeup of Gräber’s Undead force is your decision: it should consist mainly of Skeletons and Zombies, with a handful of ‘special’ creatures such as the Flying Death Skulls (that the Characters have encountered before), the Carrion, and the Winged Nightmare, if they survived previous encounters. The overall strength of Gräber’s force should be sufficient not only to rescue the Characters, but also to make it clear that they are at the Gravelord’s mercy when the battle is over.

‘Foolish mortals! As the price of your constant interference, your puny lives are forfeit to the Gravelord!’

Staging the Encounter

The Characters may take this to mean that Gräber intends to wipe them out, but the massed Undead make no move to attack. Instead, the whining voice continues.

The attack should come as the Characters are close to being overwhelmed. Perhaps enemy reinforcements have just arrived, perhaps the Characters have taken a Critical Wound or two, or the woods have come alive with an endless stream of arrows or gunfire aimed their way. Whatever the case, the Characters should seem to be facing certain defeat, with no help in sight. The Undead arrive at the very moment when the Players begin to believe that all is lost. Suddenly there is a commotion in the rear of the enemy forces, although the Characters cannot immediately tell what is happening. After a round or two, the Characters catch glimpses of bone and rotting flesh as their enemies fall. Above the din of battle, they hear snatches of blasphemous incantations in a whining, nasal voice that is achingly familiar. Fallen enemies stand up and attack their former comrades.

‘These wretched creatures dared to usurp the Gravelord’s right to vengeance, and they died for their presumption!’

Still, the Undead make no move to attack.

‘The time of your doom is not yet upon you, so go on your way — but know that the Gravelord will return to claim you!’

The front ranks of Undead stay in place while Gräber and his other minions withdraw. The Carrion rise into the air, along with Gräber on his Flying Nightmare, surrounded by a pack of Flying Death Skulls. Only when they have disappeared beyond the treetops do the other Undead slowly retreat.

Attack!

That, at least, is how Gräber plans to have the encounter play out. He expects the Characters to be so awed by his appearance, and so confused by the fact that he has saved them, that they will be a docile and passive audience for his speech. As we have seen before, Gräber’s plans usually favour the theatrical over the practical, and he consistently overestimates the psychological effect he has on the Characters.

Should the Characters have a few choice words for Gräber, allow him to become flustered in his response. The mask slips for a moment, and the petulant young man insists they take him seriously. If the Characters persist, he leaves in a huff, reminding them that his vengeance is close at hand. Of course, don’t forget he has numberless Undead at his beck and call, and is not afraid to use them. He merely hopes for a more theatrical end for his greatest foes, one worthy of being made into a tale told down through the ages. A terrific storm would be ideal, along with a few lightning strikes to set the tone. A sizable audience of suitably terrified witnesses would also be ideal. Is that so much to ask?

Of course, rather than simply stand, slack-jawed and fearful, as Gräber plays out his scene, the Characters are also likely to attack. Gräber has made sure that he is out of sight and is using magic to make his voice come from one of the Undead in the front rank.

Spotting him through the massed ranks of Undead is not easy. Gräber responds to any attempts at violence by ordering his Undead to seize the Characters.

Each Character is subjected to 1d10 grapple attacks ( WFRP , page 163) from the Skeletons and Zombies,. Gräber supplements their efforts as necessary with immobilizing magic such as Entangle and Push . A successfully grappled Character is stripped of all weapons, which are left in a heap a short way away. Once all the Characters have been subdued, Gräber delivers the rest of his speech, throwing in a few extra lines about the futility of attempting to resist the will of the Gravelord.

Pursuit

Having been rescued, threatened, and quite possibly embarrassed by Gräber and his Undead followers, there is every chance the Characters decide to follow them, whittling down their numbers with harassing attacks and ultimately defeating the Undead horde. This can make a good ongoing thread for the Gravelord sub-plot, and you can adjust the main adventure as necessary to ensure that this does not take the Characters too far out of their way — or that they arrive at some major plot points too late, and have to improvise in order to catch up!

Alternatively, Gräber could simply use obscuring magic to give the party the slip or order his Skeletons and Zombies to fight a delaying action while he and his more valuable Undead escape.

Part Two: Dark Deeds in the Grey Mountains

Chapter 6 of Empire in Ruins sees the Characters back in Altdorf as various factions struggle for power. Worrying news and disquieting rumours come in from all corners of the Empire, and the scheming nobles make as much political capital as they can from each one. A few rumours from the southern Reikland are being used to undermine Emperor Karl-Franz, although the Characters may recognise them as part of a disturbing pattern.

Mixed in with the rumours provided in Empire in Ruins , the Characters may hear those given below. The Players may not recognise their significance right away, and they can be omitted if you think they won’t add to the Players’ experience or, worse, might confuse and misdirect them. However, they are the signs of where Gräber is and clues as to what he is doing. You might remind the Players of them by some things they read or hear in the monastery during Part Four, or one of the later encounters may include some clue that makes the Players smite their foreheads in sudden realisation.

Gräber’s Progress

Having travelled through the Forest of Shadows on the trails of Dieter Helsnicht, Melkhior the Ancient, and Zacharias the Everliving, Gräber has turned south to enact the next phase of his grandiose plan. In the Grey Mountains, he intends to visit the Mound of Krell and all the other significant sites in the career of his hero, Heinrich Kemmler.

His ultimate plan is to find Kemmler’s remains, which he believes are buried somewhere close to the Bretonnian monastery of La Maisontaal, and reanimate them as a servant and advisor. Along the way, he will crush all who resisted the Lichemaster, especially the insolent monks of Taal.

For Grognards: the Lichemaster

The name of Heinrich Kemmler, the Lichemaster, has a long history in Warhammer Fantasy lore, dating back even before the first edition of WFRP .

Terror of the Lichemaster was a battle pack for the second edition of the Warhammer: The Game of Fantasy Battles miniatures game. It saw Heinrich Kemmler leading a growing Undead force against a farm, a mine, and the Imperial village of Frugelhofen. A fourth battle, ‘Vengeance of the Lichemaster’, appeared in the Third Citadel Journal and saw Kemmler attacking the Bretonnian monastery of La Maisontaal in a three-way battle that also involved a Skaven force. Three years later, this story was adapted for roleplaying in the adventure Lichemaster for the first edition of Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay.

Since then, Kemmler has been a regular in army books, a powerful necromancer available as a leader or hero for an Undead army. His legend has been considerably expanded in those sources, and he remains one of the most iconic villains in Warhammer Fantasy.

The Rumours

The Characters hear the rumours in the order given below. The earliest rumours will have reached the court some days before the Characters and may be old news by the time they arrive at the capital. If so, they might only come up if the Characters hear one of the later rumours and decide to follow up.

  • Among the reports that have come in from Ubersreik is news that contact has been lost with a Dwarven mine to the southwest of the town, within the sphere of KarakAzgaraz. The head miner, one Gimbrin Finehelm, goes to Ubersreik quite regularly to sell his ore and buy supplies, and given the general situation in the Empire — and the rumours of renewed Greenskin activity in the mountains — Ubersreik’s council is becoming concerned. There is talk of hiring mercenaries or adventurers to scout the mountains, although Lady Nacht, the Emperor’s representative, has yet to authorize such a course.

  • Gräber has struck the mine just as Kemmler did years ago, punishing the Dwarfs for daring to resist his hero. When the Characters encounter him next, a few of his Zombies may be rotting Dwarfs. If any Character has a previous acquaintance with Lady Nacht, for example, from the Starter Set or Rough Nights and Hard Days , this rumour may come directly from her, with a request that the Characters write to her with any further information they discover.

Handling the Rumours

The rumours in this section are only intended to be background colour: things that the Characters hear while in Altdorf that keep Gräber in their minds and support the encounters to come. However, there is always the risk that the Players may regard them as a call to action and decide to head into the Grey Mountains before the main plot requires them to. If this looks as though it is going to happen, you have several options.

Let Them Go: This will take a little work — matters in Altdorf are certainly pressing — but if you wish, you may allow the Characters leave to investigate these rumours. Quintus Fassbinder (see Empire in Ruins , page 106) is a key source of information in sending the Characters to Blackfire Pass in search of Ghal Maraz. Having Quintus tell the Characters that it will take some weeks before he is ready to present his evidence to them gives the Characters some leeway to chase down the Gravelord. While the political situation is dire, it is clear that some time will be required for Middenheim and Altdorf to muster the forces for outright warfare. If you wish, Quintus might later seek the Characters out in the south of the Reikland, taking the rare (for him) step of leaving the capital.

Should they leave, the Characters can spend some time traipsing around the Grey Mountains, trying to pick up Gräber’s trail and getting into all kinds of scrapes in the process. The Horned Rat

  • A great storm damaged the village of Frugelhofen, causing many deaths. It seemed to be centered on the Frugelhorn mountain. This rumour is a concatenation of three separate

  • events, and different versions heard from different sources may give some clue about this by emphasising one event over another. The lightning that was seen striking the Frugelhorn was a side effect of the necromantic magics Gräber employed to open Krell’s mound and raise some of the casualties from past battles in the area, strengthening his forces. He is particularly keen to have the service of those who died fighting for and against the Lichemaster in his legendary campaign. The village of Frugelhofen was more than damaged, and so was Bogel’s Farm nearby. No one at the farm survived to tell the tale, but a few people escaped from Frugelhofen before it was entirely destroyed. Over the days after the Characters first hear this rumour, more precise reports come in from Lady Nacht and others in the area. The Characters can learn through them that the village and several outlying farms were destroyed. They may also hear that the attackers were Undead, led by a necromancer who made bombastic speeches in an irritating, nasal voice.

  • Someone has opened Krell’s mound near Ubersreik. It was always a place of ill omen, and the locals are worried about another Undead plague. A powerful Wight king and former Champion of Chaos, Krell, was unearthed by Kemmler at the beginning of his reign of terror.

Companion includes some monsters and other ideas to enliven mountain journeys. During their journey, the Characters can learn a few things about Kemmler and perhaps even begin to understand Gräber’s obsession with him. Eventually, an opportunity to rejoin the main plotline should present itself — either Quintus arriving unexpectedly with the evidence of Blackfire pass, or , if all else fails, a messenger carrying orders from Graf Boris or Baron Heinrich can put the party back on the right track. Remember that Baron Heinrich will have access to the War Balloon after it is repaired of any damage and could quickly snatch the Characters up and deposit them again somewhere more conducive to the plot.

Not So Fast: By this point in the campaign, the Characters are no longer entirely free agents. They may not be formally in service to a particular noble or house, but they have developed a close relationship with Graf Boris Todbringer, Baron Heinrich, ‘Princess’ Katarina, and quite possibly other Imperial dignitaries as well. Not having encountered Gräber themselves, these worthies will not be inclined to attach as much importance to these rumours as the Characters, and in these tumultuous times they probably have other things for the Characters to do. Therefore, it is not too unlikely that they send the party on a different mission, which keeps them closer to the main plot of Empire in Ruins .

We’ll Look Into It: If the Players are likely to feel railroaded by some noble patron ordering them to ignore the rumours, but if the time is not right for them to take off into the mountains on a wild goose chase, you can use a blend of the previous two options. The party’s noble patron takes their concerns seriously but stresses that they are too valuable, and present circumstances too fraught, for them to simply wander off chasing rumours. Instead, their patron hires another party of adventurers, or detaches a few specialist troops from the army, to follow up on the rumours and report back. The Characters are given the opportunity to brief these investigators before they set off, but hear nothing of them again. For added dramatic impact, the Characters may spot one or two of these investigators — now Zombies — among Gräber’s followers in a future encounter.

The Legend of the Lichemaster

The dread name of the Lichemaster strikes fear into hearts across the Old World, and the tales told of his exploits are many. Here is a brief summary of the information the Characters may be able to glean through research and rumour.

  • A century ago, Heinrich Kemmler was an ambitious young necromancer. He travelled in the Lands of the Dead — even, it is said, making the Dark Pilgrimage to Nagashizzar — and returned with a knowledge of necromancy second only to that of Nagash himself.

  • He gathered a dozen lesser necromancers around him, but many were subverted by the Necrarch Vampire Brachnar the Damned. After a series of wars across Estalia, Bretonnia, and the southern Reikland, Brachnar was defeated.

  • While searching for one of the Nine Books of Nagash, Kemmler was imprisoned by the Liche Crovan, but defeated him in a magical duel. After this, Kemmler began to style himself ‘the Lichemaster.’ Afterwards he ravaged northern Tilea and Bretonnia for almost twenty years, before being defeated by Bretonnian forces led by King Theobald II. Fleeing from pursuit, he was ambushed by a resurrected Brachnar and reduced to a half-mad, wandering beggar. In this pitiful state he wandered the Grey Mountains and the Vaults for a decade. At one time he sought refuge in the Abbey of La Maisontaal, but the abbot recognised him and once again he was forced to flee.

  • High in the Vaults, Kemmler happened upon the ancient burial mound of Krell (see The Legend of Krell box), and there he made an unholy pact with an unknown power. Kemmler’s power was restored and Krell was free from his magical imprisonment to act as a commander of the Lichemaster’s forces. After strengthening his army by slaughtering the village of Frugelhofen and some outlying farms and mines, Kemmler assaulted La Maisontaal in 2491. Betrayed by Skaven allies, he was defeated by Duke Tancred II of Quenelles.

The Legend of Krell

Krell was a mighty Chaos Champion in the days before the birth of Sigmar, the ruler of a barbarian tribe that had been corrupted by the Chaos God Khorne. Krell carved out an empire among the tribes before turning against the Dwarfs to the south. He allied with the Night Goblins that stormed the Dwarf strongholds of Karak Ungor and Karak Varn, but was eventually slain assaulting the Dwarfhold of Karak Kadrin. His followers carried away his body and buried it in a barrow tomb in the Grey Mountains.

Nearly 1,500 years later, Nagash came upon the tomb when he was searching for the Crown of Sorcery. He raised this mighty hero to fight with him against Sigmar at the Battle of the River Reik. The Undead army was all but destroyed after Sigmar felled Nagash, but Krell and his Grave Guard cut their way through the Dwarf lines and escaped to cut a bloody swathe across the land. Krell was finally cornered and defeated at the Battle of Glacier Lake. His body was imprisoned in a magically constructed tomb.

Centuries later, Heinrich Kemmler came upon Krell’s tomb and freed the ancient Wight. After Kemmler’s attack on La Maisontaal, he and Krell were hunted down by Duke Tancred II of Quenelles and Krell was slain. His remains were brought back to the mound where Sigmar had buried him, and its magical defences were strengthened.

  • The Abbey of La Maisontaal, on the Bretonnian side of the Grey Mountains, was attacked by an Undead force and a sealed vault was opened. The bones of Heinrich Kemmler were removed.

  • Gräber has attacked the abbey just as Kemmler did in 2491, consciously recreating Kemmler’s career.

  • Observers posted on the borders of the Elf wood of Athel Loren report that a great Undead army is ravaging the forest. Some recall the so-called Winter of Woe in 2497.

  • Gräber is continuing to recreate Kemmler’s career, moving into the forest and using the ancient Human burial mounds there to raise a new Undead horde.

  • A rumour is circulating among the northern armies that Karl-Franz and the Reikland have stooped to necromancy to strengthen their forces, and may plan to create an Undead empire. Some insolent northerners openly claim that Karl-Franz is actually a Vampire, a slave to the Von Carsteins of Sylvania.

  • The baseless rumour is indeed circulating, but only among the most violently anti-Altdorf northerners.

  • Four years later, Kemmler slew the Duke at the Battle of Montfort Bridge. He then turned eastward into Athel Loren, plundering the ancient Human barrows there and animating the Barrow Kings to ravage the forest of the Elves. At the Battles of the Cairns in 2497, Kemmler was defeated. Krell was slain, and so, some say, was the Lichemaster himself.

Part Three: the (Second) Siege of Streissan

During Empire in Ruins , the Characters have the opportunity to assist in ‘freeing’ Streissan and it’s fall into the hands of Marius Leitdorf. Occasionally affable, often inscrutable, and always unpredictable, Leitdorf is no lasting friend to the Characters. If he learns they are carrying Ghal Maraz, he will do everything in his power to take it, and the Characters will be left attempting to flee the town after their second trip there, possibly with Master Albertrus Nyman and his family in tow ( Empire in Ruins , page 166.)

To complicate matters even further, this is precisely when the Gravelord decides to strike for the final time. Gräber has come to his former home to take his revenge and ransack the library for the knowledge he needs to resurrect his idol, Heinrich Kemmler.

Having completed his long pilgrimage in the footsteps of Kemmler, he heard some evidence that the University of Streissan might hold copies of some particularly ancient books on the intertwining of Necromancy and Chaos. Though this was far from certain to be the case, the Characters’ presence at the first siege of Streissan has, in Gräber’s mind, cemented the rumour as absolute fact. After all, the Characters are his greatest nemeses, and so must have gone there to vex him. The fact that he comes across them again, having returned to the town, only makes him more certain they are there to hide from him the very knowledge he seeks.

The town, ravaged by war, is the perfect backdrop for his final victory over the Characters and the resurrection of the great Heinrich Kemmler, who at least in Gräber’s mind, will recognise the Gravelord as an equal at last

The Alarm

Events kick off as the Characters, having found Master Albertus Nyman, are departing the town. If they have avoided Leitdorf ’s interest, it is merely unnerving. If they are actively fleeing his forces, they might already feel on edge. As they approach the gates, a bell peals out over the town. The deserted streets remain so for a moment, but as the bell rings on, troops begin to rush to the walls, and private homes open doors and windows so that those within can assess the situation. The sound is one of the three warning bells of Streissan, a mighty dull iron monument that hangs above the town’s temple of Morr — it is only rung when there is threat of Undead assaulting the town. Most have never heard its tone outside of a yearly ceremony, and it is enough of a novelty that even the worried citizens of Streissan take an interest.

Upon reaching the walls, it is clear that escape has become (more) difficult. A host of soldiers is gathering around the town, still a ways off but numbering in the thousands. They are heading to lines occupied by Alptraum’s soldiers some days or weeks prior, and threaten to surround the town in short order. The host carries tall banners, black and bearing, of course, a great skull. Those with a telescope can see that they are mostly Skeletons and Zombies, though Flying Death Skulls, Carrion, and other fell things fly above the oncoming army.

If the Characters are attempting to leave by the city gates, they are turned back, fanciful talk of their having Ghal Maraz forgotten for the moment. Any attempt to sneak out results in the Characters fumbling into the hands, or claws, of Gräber’s forces. You should give the Characters an opportunity to retreat back to the town, where ladders may be thrown down for them — either to assist in their rescue, or to seize the Hammer if the troops are aware of it.

The army is an Undead force commanded by Gräber. Currently led by Krell, its orders are to approach openly and draw attention away from what Gräber plans to do next: sneak into the university.

An Interesting Approach

Any Character who makes a successful Challenging (+0) Lore (Undead) Test notices the following:

1 SL: A couple of Carrion are hovering over the vanguard, keeping pace with it rather than ranging ahead. If the Character has encountered Gräber before, they may be expecting to see him riding a Flying Nightmare, but it is nowhere in sight.

2+ SL: There do not seem to be any ethereal Undead anywhere in the force.

While it is obvious to anyone that the army is making no attempt to approach stealthily, any Character who makes a successful Challenging (+0) Lore (Warfare) Test notices that it seems to be doing everything it can to announce its arrival. Drums are booming, banners are flying, and the ranks seem spread out a little wider than usual to make the force seem larger than it is.

What the Characters make of this information is up to the Players. The fact is that Gräber wants all eyes on the approaching army so that he and his ethereal detachment can enter the town unnoticed, taking the inhabitants by surprise.

Preparations

The Characters have a few minutes to observe Streissan’s preparations to meet the attack. In response to the alarm bell, those forces still in good order collect weapons and take up positions on the wall. Non-combatants — the old, the sick, and the less able — have all long been confined to their homes, but nevertheless a few boisterous elderly folk attempt to join the defence. Word has quickly spread that the enemy are Undead and despite days of persecution at the hands of Marius Leitdorf ’s forces, the threat of being eaten alive by a shambling horde of monstrosities does much towards unifying the town’s citizens. Priests of Morr and Sigmar, who had avoided taking a political stance on the siege and takeover of Streissan, emerge to bolster morale. Many begin to pray, calling on Sigmar to grant Miracles and Blessings that strengthen the town’s walls and gates against the approaching Undead. Pigeons are released, bound for Averheim, Nuln, and Altdorf with news of the attack. With weapons ready and eyes fixed on the foe, the defenders of Streissan wait.

The Hammer and the Priest

If Leitdorf is aware of the Hammer and his forces become aware of the Characters’ presence during preparations, Leitdorf offers them a final opportunity to give him the weapon willingly or be killed. Father Olaf, an elderly priest of Sigmar, inserts himself into this standoff asking to see the Hammer. If the Characters show it to him, the old Sigmarite instantly falls to the ground before it. After a powerful moment he arises, the fire of a younger man alight in his eyes. Father Olaf chastises Leitdorf, telling him:

‘Those that bear the Hammer of Sigmar are his Chosen Few, and to block their passage is to spit upon the Heldenhammer himself.’

The old priest’s voice carries unnaturally far and with a shuddering presence, enough to cow any immediate threat of violence from anyone save Leitdorf himself. Drawing his blade, he looks set to act, before his horse, Daisy, strides towards Father Olaf and begins to eat his hat. The awe and tension of the moment evaporates as Leitdorf laughs aloud and sheaths his blade.

He fully intends to seize the Characters and take Ghal Maraz before they leave the town, but for now he lets them be.

The Old School Spirits

Just as the Characters start to think about what they should be doing, a terrible sound comes from the university, carrying over the din of preparations. A terrible scream, inhuman and chilling to the very bone, echoes over Streissan. In the silence that follows, far more Human screams are heard faintly coming from the university — the Gravelord has arrived.

If the Characters head to the university, they see a group of ethereal Undead creatures passing through the walls of the buildings that make up the campus. There is one Cairn Wraith ( WFRP , page 329) for each Character (you can alter the numbers as needed if the party is particularly strong or weak), plus one Tomb Banshee ( WFRP , page 330) and Gräber himself (page 117), who has cast Spirit Walk to make himself Ethereal.

If the Characters do not go to the university, it quickly becomes clear that they should. The Undead outside the city launch feints and minor attacks, holding back the great bulk of their forces, while survivors of the university attack swarm the walls, attempting to get out of the city rather than remain with the ghosts inside. If the Characters still refuse to head to the university, worry not — Gräber will come for them soon enough.

Gräber’s Plan

Gräber is using his army’s approach as a diversion to cover the incursion of his ethereal force. Once in the university, he plans to slaughter the defenders and send his Tomb Banshee and Hexwraiths (see page 118) to surprise the city’s defenders from within, just as the army outside is attacking. Meanwhile, Gräber himself plans to ransack the library for the forbidden books he is sure are kept there. While he will discover some chronicles that tell of Sigmar’s battle against Krell and the career of Heinrich Kemmler, he does not find the lores of necromantic knowledge he seeks. Gräber soon realises that they are no longer present — a chancellor of the university long ago burned the foul things, which Nyman can confirm if the Characters learn of Gräber’s plans and think to ask him. Gräber, for his part, assumes the Characters took them when they were in the city earlier and swiftly turns his attention, and his wrath, on them.

Contact with the Enemy

Gräber is not expecting the Characters to be at the university — he had not realised they returned to the town after their earlier visit. Their presence will complicate his position considerably and convince him even further that they are an elite force of witch hunters sent by the Emperor himself to hunt him down. Gräber’s reaction is a mixture of frustration at the obstacle the party represents and satisfaction that his arch-nemeses have appeared once more, confirming his own opinion of his importance.

Our Weapons Are Useless!

Creatures with the Ethereal Trait ( WFRP , page 339) can be trouble, as some parties simply have no way to deal with them. Only magical attacks can damage them, and while the Characters have had some opportunities throughout The Enemy Within to acquire such weapons, they are only really guaranteed to possess Ghal Maraz at this point. Leaving a single Character to deal with all of these creatures may be a tall order. Miracles and magic also work against Ethereal creatures, but if the party does not have any other way to deal with the creatures, you may wish to change up the encounter. A priest of Sigmar or Morr may be on hand to work a Miracle or Blessing, such as Blessing of Righteousness ( WFRP , page 226) , that will allow a Character to harm the spirits, or they may be fewer in number. Priests of Morr can also create special oils that allow a weapon to harm spirits, though these are rare and expensive to create, so any priest will only have a little to hand. Alternatively, the ward which protects the restricted section may temporarily make all the spirits present sufficiently manifest for them to be harmed by mundane weapons. Such an effect would be brief, perhaps 2–3 rounds, but would give the Characters some time to strike back against the dead.).

Ghostsmear

Though Priests of Morr refer to it reverentially as Morr’s Blessed Oil, most who have had cause to use the shimmering tincture in battling the undead refer to it as ‘Ghostsmear’ or simply ‘The Good Stuff.’ Spirits, spectres, ghosts, banshees and other ethereal undead that would normally only be harmed by magical weapons can be damaged by a weapon coated in Ghostsmear. One application lasts an hour under ideal conditions, but may be washed from the blade sooner if it is raining heavily or the blade becomes submerged. Additionally, if the weapon is used to injure a living creature while covered in Ghostsmear, the effect is instantly dismissed as the infusion of living blood renders the oil inert.

Ghostsmear is never offered for sale, but only created at need by devout priests of Morr. It requires 3 Gold Crowns worth of ingredients and a night of fevered prayer to create. Ghostsmear can be stored for one year before losing its effects.’

However, the Characters are likely quite powerful by now, and have had plenty of warning that Gräber is a threat to them. If they failed to prepare properly, you may simply wish to let the spirits do some serious harm to them. This is WFRP , after all, and Fate points exist for a reason!

To Gräber, there could be no greater affirmation of his destiny soon reaching its zenith than the presence of his greatest enemies here, at the mercy of the greatest army he has ever commanded.

Before entering combat, Gräber casts a number of protective spells on himself, in addition to the one that makes him Ethereal . These are Aethyric Armour (which he can extend as described on WFRP page 242), Distracting , and Terrifying . All four of these spells have a Duration of Willpower Bonus Rounds, and will expire four rounds after combat is joined.

Gräber spends the first round making one of his bombastic speeches, and as usual its dramatic impact is somewhat lessened by his small stature and whining, nasal voice. The Tomb Banshee hovers by his side as he speaks, and the Hexwraiths (page 118) form a screen in front of him, ready to fight anyone who tries to approach.

‘So, we meet again! The Gravelord commends your persistence, but this time there will be no mercy! Prepare to die, and serve the Gravelord forever! Attack, my children!’

At his command, the Tomb Banshee begins a Ghostly Howl and one Hexwraith moves to fight each Character. Any ‘spare’ Hexwraiths begin slaughtering anyone else about or gang up on the most dangerous Character, especially any Gräber knows to be capable of spellcasting or commanding Miracles.

If confronted at the university, Gräber makes his way to the library, an imposing old building of stone with a much less imposing thatched roof. To continue his search (passing through the shelves in his Ethereal state), he heads for the Restricted Collection. Any Characters who try to follow him have to negotiate the walls, locked doors, and a maze of shelves, which means that he will probably reach the door to the Restricted Collection first. Though he will not find what he is searching for there, the section does contain many dangerous books, and a previous chancellor hired a wizard to set several wards upon it. The wards dispel Gräber’s Spirit Walk , and he will have to deal with the inconvenience of corporeality.

If the Characters are elsewhere, such as hiding out in the Nyman basement or helping defend the walls, then Gräber eventually finds them and attacks them directly. He pauses first to compliment their cunning in retrieving the books he seeks before he could get there, which doubtless leaves them confused..

The Attack Begins

Regardless of whether or not the Characters are at the university, once Gräber reaches the Restricted Section his Spirit Walk spell comes to an end and he becomes fully material. At the same time, screams and crashes ring out from the town walls as the main Undead force attacks. If the Characters are at the walls, they may sensibly wish to help defend them. While the Skeletons and Zombies have some trouble scaling the walls, their numbers quickly add up. The Undead throw up crude ladders of lashed-together bone, and the nimbler Skeletons attempt to assault anyone defending the walls. Flying Death Skulls assault defenders from the air, along with other flying Undead.

The Shelves

During any fight in the library, there is a very good chance that a Player will think of tipping the bookcases over to stop Gräber reaching the restricted section, to crush or escape from some Undead attacker, or for some other reason.

The bookcases in the university’s library are huge and heavy in proportion. Double-faced and made of oak planks an inch thick, they stand 8 feet high, 12 feet long, and 2 feet deep. Each one holds books totalling several hundred pounds in weight. This weight makes them hard to tip, at least initially.

To get a shelf unit to a tipping point requires a Very Hard (-30) Strength Test. Up to eight Characters and NPCs can co-operate in pushing one shelf over (see Assistance on page 155 of the WFRP rulebook). As the bookcase tips, books will begin to slide from the shelves on the side opposite the Characters pushing it. They will rain down on anyone beneath, causing a Damage 3d10 hit on any creature in the way who fails a Difficult (-10) Dodge Test — though of course Ethereal creatures are immune. A Critical Success on the Strength Test leads to a domino effect. Instead of ending up leaning against its neighbour, the falling bookcase knocks that one over too, and so on until a bookcase either hits a wall or falls into an empty space.!

Leitdorf ’s troops were unprepared — some are drunk, others injured from the battle. The defence is desperate. There should be some kind of Undead for each Character to attack every round, though in the cut and thrust of mass combat, it is difficult to make any real progress. The presence of Ghal Maraz, if it is drawn, bolsters the defenders, and any section of wall where the bearer of the great hammer stands is defended bitterly and with success.

After he is finished in the university, or when things start to go badly for him if the Characters are there, Gräber tries to cast a Marsh Lights spell ( WFRP , page 241) and send the lights out through the main window. This is a pre-arranged signal to his Undead force, and a barrage of Screaming Skulls ( WFRP , page 257) strikes the window on the next round, shattering the glass and offering Gräber a potential escape route.

Combat

By this stage, battle has been joined between the defenders of Streissan and the attacking Undead. You can resolve this battle in a number of different ways; three possibilities are offered below:

Flee!: The Characters may well attempt to flee the town, leaving it to its fate. If they have found Nyman, the Characters have all the information they require. If not, they will have to live without being able to renew Ghal Maraz — see Empire in Ruins page 176.

The docks are a likely path to freedom. Though they are under siege by Undead emerging from the river, Gräber is no tactician and underestimated the flow of the river. Many of his forces are carried away downstream, likely to confuse and terrify some fisherfolk near Nuln in the near future. The Characters may grab a boat or raft here with relative ease, seeing off the few waterlogged Undead who cling to it, and flee. This is not the most heroic response, but it is certainly the safest. Leitdorf will eventually hold Streissan, but at a terrible loss, and his convincing victory and claim to the seat of Elector is again thrown into doubt. More civil strife will result, and the Gravelord will survive to trouble the Empire further. Despite his theatrical bluster, Gräber clearly has a gift for Necromancy, and though the Characters may have laughed at him in the past, they are unlikely to be quite so full of mirth should they cross his path again.

contains The Mass Combat Rules: Archives of the Empire II rules for mass combat. They can be used to cover the party’s experience of the battle, though you should do this only if the Characters enjoy larger combats. Elsewhere in Empire in Ruins the Characters find themselves at another siege if they attempt to renew Ghal Maraz, and again at Diesdorf if you use the additional material The Siege of Diesdorf in this book, all of which could use the mass combat rules. Be aware that the Characters may well become fatigued from finding themselves in the midst of such battles, though others might find it a suitably epic experience as the campaign nears its end. You should play this by ear, and attempt to cater to your Players’ preferences.

Defend the Walls!: The walls of Streissan are assaulted from all directions, and some areas risk being quickly overrun if the Characters don’t pitch in. Defenders sound horns from distressed areas; if the Characters aren’t aware of what this means a nearby soldier quickly fills them in — someone is almost overrun. If the Characters respond to this call, they find 1d10 beleaguered soldiers holding out against a group of Undead that have scaled or breached the walls.

You can use the table provided, or select the numbers yourself. If any Character makes a successful Challenging (+0) Leadership Test, the soldiers obey their orders and any survivors continue to follow them once the combat is resolved. This Test becomes Average (+20) if the Character wields Ghal Maraz. The Characters might be able to gather a force around them to retake the walls.

Ending the Attack

Gräber’s Undead force breaks off its attack on the monastery as soon as one of the following conditions is fulfilled:

The Gravelord’s Army

Gräber is killed; see The Gravelord Falls below.

A definitive list of Gräber’s Undead army is not given here. This is deliberate.

The Character bearing Ghal Maraz rolls an Astounding Success (6+ SL) on a Challenging (+0) Leadership Test to command the troops defending the town; see Heroes of the Day below.

As GM, you must decide in advance how you are going to run this incident. Depending on the choice you make, a hard-and-fast army list may be unnecessary. It can also be damaging to the campaign experience.

The Characters appear to be utterly doomed and need rescue; see The Wrath of Sigmar below.

While the party is certainly stronger by now than they were at the start of the campaign, their actual combat strength could vary widely, according to the Careers and Skills they have taken, the equipment they have bought or otherwise acquired, and various other factors. Any attempt to provide a ‘one size fits all’ army list would be doomed to failure: either in the form of a demoralising ‘total party kill’ or as an enemy scarcely worthy of the name. Only you know your Players and their Characters, and only you can decide what would make a suitably challenging foe.

As the GM, you can also end the attack if anything else happens that makes it appropriate in dramatic terms for the Undead to retreat. The following paragraphs may provide some inspiration for how to bring this about.

Choose the creatures that make the most sense, add any that you think would make things more interesting for the Characters, and be prepared to adjust numbers on the fly if things turn out to be too easy or too hard.

A Strategic Retreat

Gräber, as ever, underestimates his opposition. He certainly did not expect the Characters to be carrying the Ghal Maraz. Once he realises the books he sought are not here, and once he has convinced himself that the Characters do not have them, he retreats. Whatever troops he has available continue to attack the Characters, attempting to distract them, and the troops attacking the walls continue their assault, though their strength is diminished once the Gravelord has fled.

The Characters can try to pursue Gräber — they are very likely to want to put a final end to him. He flees into the nearest open area, and after two rounds, his Winged Nightmare swoops down and picks him up, its talons barely grazing the battlements and rooftops as it struggles into the air. Under cover of a barrage of missile fire from the Undead army, he abandons the attack, leaving masses of Skeletons and Zombies to fight a rearguard as the army withdraws.

If Gräber is killed in the course of his escape attempt, refer to The Gravelord Falls below.

The Gravelord Falls

at Brass Keep. Priests of Morr gather up the Gravelord’s mortal remains and subject them to a series of divine rituals that ensure that Gräber is utterly destroyed, in body and soul, forever. The Characters can escape the town and are free to carry on with Empire in Ruins .

Gräber has been a thorn in the party’s side for some time now, and few things would be more satisfying to the Players than to bring him down — especially here, at the zenith of his power, where none can possibly deny the threat he poses. It smacks of poetic justice, and the Characters’ role in his defeat goes a long way to ensuring their safe exit from the town, as Marius Leitdorf should almost certainly be aware of the presence of Ghal Maraz by the battle’s end, if he wasn’t before. He seeks to claim it, but if the Characters slew the dread necromancer who assaulted Streissan in full view of his soldiers, Leitdorf has no option other than to let them go.

A Cunning Plan: Gräber has also been frustrated by his various encounters with the Characters, and his natural overconfidence has been tempered somewhat. While scouring the vaults for traces of Krell and Kemmler, he recovered a rare Nehekaran scarab amulet, which he wears out of sight inside his clothing. Upon his death, the scarab frees his spirit as a ghost, crumbling to dust as it does so, and he either escapes or the Characters might manage to chase the Ghost down and destroy it. If he escapes, Gräber is Ethereal in the next encounter. If the Ghost is destroyed, refer to Victory! above.

The Characters may have killed Gräber, or come close to doing so, in one of the previous encounters. Their best chance was in the village of Pritzstock during the events of Flying Death Skulls ( Power Behind the Throne Companion ). If so, the Players might be enormously frustrated by his return and eager to put a permanent end to this dangerous — and profoundly irritating — foe. If Gräber falls at Streissan, you have a choice:

Heroes of the Day

While the Characters may well be enemies of Marius Leitdorf and pursued by his troops, all that is put aside during the Undead attack. The troops are weakened by their occupation of the town, and the possibility of an Undead horde was far from anyone’s mind. They are prepared to defend the walls, as a counterattack by the Alptraums was always a possibility, but had thought any counter-attack would take weeks to muster. The Characters are likely skilled combatants and certainly have experience fighting the Gravelord and his undead. They also bear Ghal Maraz, which will likely become obvious during the battle.

Victory!: The Characters have prevailed. Gräber is dead — truly dead this time — and bereft of their master, his Undead followers crumble to dust and blow away on the breeze. This is particularly appropriate if the killing blow was struck by Ghal Maraz, as it is an ancient enemy to Necromancers, having long ago been used by Sigmar to overcome the Necromancer Morath

If the Characters find themselves fighting on the walls, and a Character scores an Astounding Success on a Challenging (+0) Leadership Test, the troops begin to rally to them. Much of Leitdorf ’s leadership takes a long time to get organised, and the sight of an inspiring Character, shouting words of encouragement and striking down Undead left and right is an inspiring one. The nearby forces swiftly rally to the Character, pushing the Undead back all around them. If the Father Olaf made an appearance earlier to mediate for the Characters with Leitdorf, he appears again now, invoking Sigmar and calling all to resist the Undead. Leitdorf ’s forces temporarily gain the Hatred (Undead) and Holy Hatred Talents ( WFRP , page 138) at maximum level until the battle is over, but it will likely be more than enough to describe the narrative as the rallied troops overwhelm the Undead attackers. The creatures are cast back from the walls and pursued mercilessly into the plains beyond.

A brief time later, the surviving Undead pull back (if Gräber is alive) or turn to dust, should he fall in the battle (See The Gravelord Falls , above).

The Wrath of Sigmar

If, and only if, the Characters are completely overwhelmed and likely to perish, you may consider having Father Olaf return for a final strike against the Gravelord. Wielding an old carpenter’s hammer, glowing brightly with holy might, he emerges from the melee to strike at Gräber. The blow lands true, but the Gravelord is only staggered. Gräber turns and with a blast of necromantic energy casts Drain , withering the old priest to a dried husk in moments. Still, he is staggered, and the hammer’s blow had a touch of the divine about it. Unwilling to risk himself further, the Gravelord flees the battlefield (see A Strategic Retreat , above).

This should be an embarrassment for the Characters, as Gräber once again escapes their grasp. Not being the heroes of the hour, they still have to deal with escaping Leitdorf ’s grasp.

The Aftermath

The Undead are in full retreat, and if Gräber has fallen many of them will have crumbled to dust. The soldiers begin the task of cleaning up, while priests of Morr prepare the dead for burial with more care than usual, should the Undead influence have spread to their bodies. What remains of the Undead is burned in a great pyre that lasts for days, and chokes the sky above Striessan even further.

Part Four: What Now?

After this battle, if he still lives, the Characters may decide that Gräber needs to be stopped once and for all, distracting them from the main plot of Empire in Ruins . The Pursuit (page 105) section gives some options for making sure that Gräber is able to give the Characters the slip so they can carry on with the adventure. The section in Part Two, Handling the Rumours , offers some more ideas, including the option of letting the Characters follow Gräber.

Alternatively, you may decide that the Characters have had enough of Gräber by now — he certainly is annoying — and simply let him die and stay dead. This is an especially good choice if the Characters were particularly heroic in defeating him — there is still much adventure to come, and the Characters may well be humbled yet on their return to Altdorf. Let them have the moment, granting them Leitdorf ’s mercurial respect for just long enough to see them safely from Streissan and back upon the path of Empire in Ruins .

Npcs and Monsters

Hans Gräber, ‘the Gravelord’

‘The Gravelord’ is a scrawny Human with black, straight hair in a disheveled pudding-bowl cut; pale blue eyes; and a pointed, turned-up nose. With his small size and whining voice, Gräber looks and sounds ten years younger than his true age, and this bothers him immensely. Violently sensitive to slights both real and imagined, he has turned his almost superhuman propensity for learning and his overwrought imagination into a means of revenge against everyone who has ever slighted him, everyone who ever will — in fact, everyone. By means of his ‘Gravelord’ persona — an attempt to emulate his hero Heinrich Kemmler, ‘the Lichemaster’ — and his carefully planned, theatrical attacks, Gräber seeks to spread fear and earn respect, humiliating all who cross him in revenge for all the humiliation that he feels the world has visited upon him. While vain and rather ridiculous, he is far from stupid, and although he would hate to admit it — especially to himself — his ability to be underestimated is one of his more powerful weapons.
