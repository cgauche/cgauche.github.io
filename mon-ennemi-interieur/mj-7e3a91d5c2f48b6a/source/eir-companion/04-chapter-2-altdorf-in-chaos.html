# <a class="line-anchor" id="L1"></a>CHAPTER 2 Altdorf in Chaos

<a class="line-anchor" id="L2"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L3"></a>*Pages PDF 15-26*

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L4"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L5"></a>Chapter 2 Altdorf in Chaos
## <a class="line-anchor" id="L6"></a>**Changing Times**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L7"></a>Sheeragetru has had one eye upon Altdorf for an age, knowing by the twisted threads of fate that this festering monument to human hubris would be its place of glorious liberation. Sheeragetru would have it no other way, for it will bring great pleasure to remake the spiritual home of Sigmar, who imprisoned the daemon so long ago. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L8"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L9"></a>The maddening gaze of a Lord of Change has influenced the city down through the years, with many of Altdorf ’s more sensitive citizens feeling the presence of the daemon close by. In recent months this unsettling influence has moved from the dreams of artists and poets into physical reality, and as the 8th of Brauzeit draws closer the scale and frequency of unusual phenomena will only increase. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L10"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L11"></a>This chapter provides a selection of unusual events that players might experience during their time in Altdorf. Some are unsettling, some are informative, a few are quite dangerous, and a few simply bizarre. These events serve to underline the reality-shaking events that may come should the Characters fail, and can be deployed any time they are in the city. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L12"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L13"></a>These events may also be used anywhere the influence of Chaos might be felt, and several would make good jumping off points for other adventures. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L14"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L15"></a>**The Prophecy**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L16"></a>By this stage in the campaign, it is clear that the Empire’s veneer of unity, order, and civilisation is starting to crack. Even so, there are plenty of people who still believe in the Empire and refuse to acknowledge that anything is wrong. They ignore the growing unrest, the worrying news from all parts of the Empire, and the stories of Ratmen and Chaos-worshippers, and go to considerable lengths to explain such things away as perfectly normal events blown out of proportion, or deride them as ‘falsities and fearmongering’ promulgated by treasonous sorts with their own ineffable and unworthy agenda. Some even cast doubt upon the sanity of anyone who fears for the Empire’s future. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L17"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L18"></a>The Empire’s street-corner prophets are increasing in number and their rhetoric is becoming more deranged with every passing day, which makes it easy for the skeptics to write off anyone else’s concerns. These individuals are especially common in Altdorf at present, where they are spreading an extensive prophecy. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L19"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L20"></a>**Hearing the Prophecy**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L21"></a>It is unlikely the Characters will ever hear the entire prophecy all at one time. Instead, they might hear a piece here and a piece there. With a little imagination, it is possible to find many different ways for the prophecy to haunt the Characters and play on their nerves. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L22"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L23"></a>Street-corner prophets may include part of the prophecy in their ravings, only to be hauled away by the authorities for causing a disturbance before they deliver more than a couple of lines. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L24"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L25"></a>Fragments may be daubed on walls, or found on scraps of torn parchment in the homes of suspects and cultists. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L26"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L27"></a>Garbled lines might haunt a Character’s troubled dreams. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L28"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L29"></a>An overheard conversation might include a few words, perhaps in a voice far different from the voices of the speakers. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L30"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L31"></a>A dying mutant might gasp out a few more before expiring, or they might echo strangely from the void as a slain Daemon returns to the Realm of Chaos. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L32"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L33"></a>The Characters happen upon a ragged figure squatting in the street. They drop a handful of bones on the ground, stare at them, scrawl some symbols in the dust, and repeat the process. As the Characters approach, their face twists in a spasm of horror as they launch themself at a Character of your choice. They clutch the Character by the shoulders and scream the prophecy into their face before running . 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L34"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L35"></a>wildly off into the passing crowds 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L36"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L37"></a>In the midst of conducting a ritual, a priest might suddenly develop a glassy stare and intone a phrase or two in a voice of unnatural depth and resonance before collapsing. When the priest comes to their senses, they remember nothing other than feeling immensely cold. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L38"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L39"></a>Stranger Prophecies
## <a class="line-anchor" id="L40"></a>**Familiar Fortellings**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L41"></a>_‘I see Darkness Gathering as the Last House of Joy Falls — beware, for Shadows Over Bögenhafen stir! Then Beloved Morr, resplendent in Vestments of Green, stands astride Sigmar’s Great River. Yea, I see Death on the Reik and I despair! For then the Stained Hand guides the Once Mighty Lord, and this Power Behind the Throne curses us all. Lo, the Horned Rat then claims the Broken King atop his Throne of Lies, and the White Walls Fall, leaving our Empire in Ruins! Tremble in fear, ye mighty, for the End Times have come.’_ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L42"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L43"></a>The Characters remember this scene from their earliest visit to Altdorf during **The Enemy Within** (page 36). They may be surprised to hear the same words spoken again from different mouths; it is useful to remind them of this particular prophecy at this point, should it have spilled their minds. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L44"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L45"></a>**Gideon’s Guile**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L46"></a>_'A man bearing a false name travels the Empire, and many welcome him who should not. A hand strikes in the darkness, purple-shadowed. A crown drips blood, red with gore. A castle falls, that fell generations ago. Rats gnaw, and steal, and pray to the moon. A man’s face is stolen many, many times. The wolf eats the hammer, and chokes on its prey. The beasts in the forest awake and are hungry. Those outside will come inside, and those within shall surely perish if they do. Yet there are those inside who aid those outside, and many enemies are within!’_ 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L47"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L48"></a>**The End Times**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L49"></a>_‘Trust not your neighbour, for he may not be your neighbour! Fear the stranger, for stranger things are coming! Take the hammer from the wolf’s jaws and repair the walls and roof, for the storm is coming! Hold the hammer fast, keep the wolf close, and fight the darkness together!_ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L50"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L51"></a>_The Changer of the Ways doth sit upon the Imperial throne! The Enemy is Within!’_ 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L52"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L53"></a>For Grognards: a Portent of Doom
<a class="line-anchor" id="L54"></a>Veterans of the first edition **Enemy Within** campaign may have heard a prophecy very much like this one when they visited Middenheim in **Power Behind the Throne** . In this version, a few details have been changed in the interests of consistency with the Director’s Cut version. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L55"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L56"></a>You can change some of these details back if it will entertain the Players — or, better yet, confuse and worry them. If you feel the Characters have influenced things sufficiently, try to choose some words that reflect their own past choices and achievements. In particular, if they are wrestling with any particular issue, who to support for the claim to the Imperial Throne, the fate of Ghal Maraz and so on, try to pepper in some appropriately vague pronouncements. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L57"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L58"></a>Here are a few substitute (or additional) lines that refer to the first-edition campaign: 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L59"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L60"></a>‘I see a great withering and putrefaction to the north, its miasma blowing down on the north wind to choke us all. Truly, Something is Rotten in Kislev!’ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L61"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L62"></a>‘I see fire and slaughter sweeping the land! I see cities burning, and rivers afire! I see the Empire in Flames!’ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L63"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L64"></a>‘I see graves yawning wide, and corpses marching! The Restless Dead shall enlighten us!’ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L65"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L66"></a>Players who experienced the third-edition **Enemy Within** campaign might be treated to the following additional lines: 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L67"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L68"></a>‘Beneath a black cowl are three faces, and no one knows the truth!’ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L69"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L70"></a>‘Soldier, wizard, noble, hood — who is bad and who is good?’ (this doggerel couplet might be effective if the Characters hear group of children chanting it in the course of some game) 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L71"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L72"></a>‘The Enemy Below dims the sun! The beasts are loosed, and all is confusion!’ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L73"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L74"></a>‘All march against the Enemy Without, but the city shall fall at the sound of a bell!’ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L75"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L76"></a>‘Let the Hammer strike the Bell before the Bell strikes the hour, or Altdorf is lost!’ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L77"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L78"></a>‘The Enemy Within is the Enemy Beyond, and madness shall bring victory!’ 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L79"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L80"></a>**Wilder Magic**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L81"></a>Magic and Chaos are closely intertwined — indeed, some go so far as to claim that they are identical. Be that as it may, magic can behave in unexpected ways when a spellcaster is in close proximity to a potent source of Chaos. While in Altdorf, or in any of the other nine locations that fall under the eye of Tzeentch after Ghal Maraz has been retrieved, you may substitute the table on page 18 for the Miscast Tables in the WFRP rulebook. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L82"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L83"></a>All of this exciting and particularly mutable magical activity has not gone unnoticed. As well as terrifying wizards throughout the city, a pair of daemons have been drawn to Altdorf as gibbering moths to a flame. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L84"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L85"></a>**The Blue Scribes of Tzeentch**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L86"></a>These Daemons look very much like Blue Horrors, but are much more intelligent and powerful. It is said that Tzeentch created the two Daemons personally, named them P’tarix and Xirat’p, and tasked them with learning every spell in existence. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L87"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L88"></a>The Blue Scribes ride a Disc of Tzeentch through realms mortal and eternal, P’tarix scrawling frantically with a quill crafted from a Lord of Change’s pin-feather while Xirat’p reads the written words to check for mistakes, and in doing so, unleashing the spell’s power on any creature unfortunate enough to be nearby. The Characters, and especially any wizards, may notice them skulking about the city. This causes a great terror in any who witness them, but unusually most seem to forget about the horrors as soon as they are out of sight. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L89"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L90"></a>If left alone, they ignore mortals and, provided they are not studying spells when encountered, they are not especially dangerous. If they are threatened, Xirat’p starts reading at random from the accumulated scrolls, casting a random spell each round. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L91"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L92"></a>No profile is provided here, as the pair are inteded to reflect the magical chaos of Altdorf (and annoy any spell casters) rather than attack the Characters outright. However, if required you may treat the pair as Blue Horrors, with a shared Trait of _Fly (60)_ , and the _Random Caster_ and _Spell Syphon_ Traits as shown below. For more on the Blue Scribes see **Winds of Magic** .

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L141"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L142"></a>**A Touch of Chaos**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L143"></a>There are few events as immediately indicative of the presence of Chaotic influence that then the manifestation of Daemons on a city street. As the day of reckoning draws closer this very thing happens with increasing frequency. Event as Colleges of Magic rush to investigate, more subtle works of Chaos exert their influence on the citizens of Altdorf. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L144"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L145"></a>**Marsie Wagnershoff Child of Chaos**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L146"></a>Marsie is a baker’s daughter from Diesdorf, and  has been in Altdorf for a few weeks now, having come seeking to better her lot. Things have not gone at all according to plan. Her mutation only struck in the last few days, but it has since quite understandably consumed her entire life. An horrific experience, her brain now gives spontaneous rise to a swarm of maggotty worms. They have eaten their way through her skull from the inside, devouring her hair, and have since squirmed ceaselessly about her scalp. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L147"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L148"></a>She has brushed, washed, and scraped them away, but always they return within minutes, painfully burrowing out from her skin. Marsie is otherwise an innocent soul, and only wants to somehow survive and escape the city.  She hopes to find a cure for her condition amongst the wise Hedgefolk of the countryside. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L149"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L150"></a>**The Body Thief**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L151"></a>As the Characters pass by, a townsperson or other minor NPC falls to the ground, clutching their head and screaming in agony. The thrashing body is hard to examine (–20 penalty to all related Tests), but shows no signs of any wounds or disease — until 1d10 rounds later, when the unfortunate’s skin begins to split and peel back. ## <a class="line-anchor" id="L160"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L161"></a>**New Trait: Induce Frenzy (Mutation)**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L162"></a>If Marsie touches anyone and takes an Action to will it, the **–** target must make a **Hard ( 20) Willpower Test** or enter Frenzy ( **WFRP** , page 190). She can pick the Frenzied Character’s first target from any creature within 4 yards, but she has no further control over the course of the Frenzy. The Frenzy ends when the Character falls to 1 or fewer Wounds.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L179"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L180"></a>**Flamers of Tzeentch**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L181"></a>Flamers of Tzeentch are odd creatures, proof if it were needed that the Changer of Ways is capable of truly bizarre creations. More akin to an ambulatory fungus than any mortal creature, the Flamers of Tzeentch propel themselves by emitting bilious gouts of gas from multitudinous, ever shifting orifices. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L182"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L183"></a>New Creature Trait: Breath Rating (Warpfire)
<a class="line-anchor" id="L184"></a>Activated and resolved as any other Breath attack (WFRP page 338), Warpfire is the stuff of raw Chaos, and infused with the fickle power of Tzeentch. If a creature fails to dodge this attack, they must immediately make a **Challenging (+0) Endurance** Test. Pass or fail, this counts as a Moderate Exposure to Corruption (WFRP page 182). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L185"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L186"></a>Despite their unwieldy appearance they are capable of bursts of rapid movement, and those who have had cause to do battle with them are thankful the things are less common than the pink and blue Horrors that seem a favoured daemon of Tzeentch. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L187"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L188"></a>Though the creatures bulk is not inconsiderable, their main form of attack is the emission of great gouts of many hued flame. This is not fire in the traditional sense, but instead an intense warpfire, the raw stuff of Chaos itself. It is a fickle power, often benefitting the targets, but it brings with it the warping curse of corruption, and so Tzeentch is pleased with it in any case. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L189"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L190"></a>A Flamer of Tzeentch would be a terrible thing indeed to appear in Altdorf, a singular sign that these are strange days indeed. All who seen it would be questioned intently, and any touched by its flames would be suspect. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L191"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L192"></a>If a creature fails this Test, they take damage from the attack as normal, and additionally gain 1 Ablaze condition. As long as they have this condition, they must test for a Minor Exposure to Corruption each round. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L193"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L194"></a>If the creature passes the Endurance Test, they take no damage. Instead, the fickle powers of Chaos grant that creature the Regenerate Trait for 1d10 rounds. However, wounds healed by this Regenerate Trait do not heal differently. Wounds seal shut, but the new flesh looks different, with birth marks or tattoos that were never there before. Ruined eyes return a different colour, and restored limbs appear longer, shorter, or more muscular than before. These changes have no game effect, but should serve to unsettle the Character and underline the warping power of Chaos. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L195"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L196"></a>**Flamers of Tzeentch**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L197"></a>Fungoid entities of strange and shifting form, the Flamers of Tzeentch are named for their main means of attack — emitting great gouts of flame that burn or change all that they touch. They are a feared foe, and even those who survive an encounter with them are left changed by the experience. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L198"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L199"></a>**Traits:** Armour 1, Breath (Warpfire), Corruption (Moderate), Daemonic 9+, Fear 2, Distracting, Night Vision, Unstable, Weapon +8 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L200"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L201"></a>**Font of Flame:** If, at the beginning of its turn, a Flamer of Tzeentch does not have enough Advantage to use its Breath Trait, roll a d10. On a  roll of 8 or more it may do so anyway. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L202"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L203"></a>**Events and Encounters**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L204"></a>The influence of a Lord of Change is affecting the minds of Altdorfers as well as their physical surroundings. Here are some ideas for events and encounters — they may become more extreme as time goes on. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L205"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L206"></a>**Restless Locals**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L207"></a>Even before things begin to happen that defy the laws of nature and common sense, the minds of the townsfolk are opening to the potential for change. Ambitions and resentments are more keenly felt, and agitators stir the populace to unrest and even to violence. Riots break out in the streets, for any reason or for no apparent reason at all, with mobs marching in various parts of the city, attacking anyone who gets in their way and sometimes looting the premises along their route. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L208"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L209"></a>These mobs are lead by individuals who have fallen under the sway of Tzeentch, and find themselves drawn to the Characters. While the mob at large might be reasoned with, the Agitators who lead them are much more difficult to intimidate.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L226"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L227"></a>**Frenzied Altdorfers**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L228"></a>The Characters are passing through one of Altdorf ’s many markets when a shopper suddenly seizes a leg of mutton as an improvised weapon and launches into a frothing attack. There is no warning: the Characters cannot avoid being Surprised. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L229"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L230"></a>On every subsequent round, another ordinary citizen joins in the murderous frenzy. While in theory the Characters could be overwhelmed, in practice they are almost certain to incapacitate the attackers as quickly as they are created. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L231"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L232"></a>The Frenzied townsfolk and servants have little chance of doing any damage to the veteran Characters. The main problem is that these people are just ordinary city folk, and cutting them down like Daemons is not something that heroic adventurers should be doing.

<a class="line-anchor" id="L238"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L239"></a>The Altdorfers have no relevant Skills or Talents and do not wear armour. They are Frenzied, and they attack with improvised weapons doing SB – 4 damage. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L240"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L241"></a>While the Characters may assume that the first attacker was simply hiding their rage, make sure that, in the next round, they see a young citizen who had already started to flee the scene stop, turn with a murderous look in their eyes, and leap to the attack. Characters who make **Challenging (+0) Perception** Tests in subsequent rounds see the same thing happen again, and again, and again. The Characters should realise that something is driving the honest citizens of Altdorf into a murderous frenzy. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L242"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L243"></a>The Frenzy automatically expires if an attacker drops to 1 or fewer Wounds. Reason suddenly returns, and, if they can still move, they try to flee. The Characters may also notice this. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L244"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L245"></a>Smart Characters may start looking for the cause of this trouble. This requires a **Hard (–20) Perception** Test. If it succeeds, the Character spots a figure passing close to two citizens just as they enter Frenzy. She is wearing nondescript clothes and a deep hood covers her head. The woman does not realise that she has been spotted until the Characters make a move. If they succeed in grabbing her, they can see the reason for the hood: the woman has worms in place of hair. Any Character touching her in a round when she is conscious and takes no other action is targeted by her Induce Frenzy power (see box). The woman starts with whichever Character is holding her. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L246"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L247"></a>**Unstable Foundations**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L248"></a>The Characters are passing by a house or a small shop that clearly belongs to a wizard. Exactly what makes this clear is up to you: the house may have a sign hanging outside that advertises magical services, it may belong to an NPC wizard with whom they have become acquainted, or it may be ringed around with mystical symbols carved or painted on the walls, for example. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L249"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L250"></a>As the Characters come close to the building, its outline begins to waver. It becomes translucent, then transparent, and within a few seconds there is an audible _pop_ as it passes through a rift in reality and into the Realm of Chaos. While the rift only exists for a second or two, and one who looks directly at it must make a **Terror 4** Test because of the things they see within. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L251"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L252"></a>A successful **Challenging (+0) Lore (Chaos or Magic)** Test suggests that the building became magically unstable, and was sucked into the Realm of Chaos as a result. But surely, that only happens to Daemons… 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L253"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L254"></a>**Signs of the Times**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L255"></a>Like any settlement of any size, Altdorf is full of signs. Not all townsfolk can read, so a sign with an image of the goods a shop sells, or of an inn’s name, is far more useful than a written name. In times such as these, though, the letters might be preferable, since images have a tendency to come to life. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L256"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L257"></a>A chicken on a weathervane might flutter down to cause havoc in the streets, for example. A dragon might pull itself out of an inn sign to spread fiery panic, or a statue might climb down from its plinth and lay about itself with stony weapons. The possibilities are endless. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L258"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L259"></a>The creatures might stay the same size as their painted or sculpted originals, or they might grow (or shrink, as appropriate) to their natural size. Their profiles are the same as for the original creatures, with the following adjustments: 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L260"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L261"></a>**Characteristics:** Intelligence, Willpower, and Fellowship are all 0. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L262"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L263"></a>**Traits:** Add Construct, Corruption (Minor), Immunity (Criticals), Immunity to Psychology, Unstable. Animated statues add Armour 3 (metal) or Armour 4 (stone). 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L264"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L265"></a>**Hit and Run**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L266"></a>A coach comes rattling down the street with no horses pulling it. It runs down anyone in its path, which counts as a Charge and Stomp from an Enormous creature. It can only be stopped by a successful **Very Hard (–30) Strength** Test which achieves 5 SLs in a single round. Up to six Characters can cooperate on the test (see _Assistance_ , **WFRP** , page 155). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L267"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L268"></a>In addition to its Stomp attack, the coach lashes out with its two poles. Each one counts as a club (Hand Weapon) with Long Reach and SB + 6 damage. The **Enemy in Shadows Companion** includes hit locations for coaches and other vehicles.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L287"></a>**Moving Day**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L288"></a>Amid a great commotion, a stream of animated furniture comes running down the street, chased by its uncomprehending but determined owner. Long legged side-tables run like deer; chairs run or waddle depending on their construction; a bed tramples its way through the throng like a charging bull; and all around scamper low side-tables, footstools, and other small items. If not stopped, the furniture will throw itself into the Reik and swim for the far bank, seeking to return to the forest where its wood once grew as trees. Someone skilled in the Jade Wind may be able to use Charm Animal to calm the frolicking furniture, but otherwise they careen freely through the streets. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L289"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L290"></a>This is obviously rather surreal, and you may play this scene for laughs if you wish. Of course, to the denizens of Altdorf it is anything but funny. Unnatural occurances are rarely benign and, even should the furniture make it out of the city, those who previously owned the fitful furnishings are likely to face investigation as potential witches themselves. If you wish to play the scene more for horror than hearty laughs, have a four poster bed trample some guards to a bloody pulp on its way to the river, or some floating drapery strangle a bright wizard who attempted to set it aflame. Remember that it is quite possible to leave the Players in fits of laughter while threatening the very lives of their Characters.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L344"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L345"></a>**Unnatural Weather**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L346"></a>As far back as history records, strange weather events have been associated with evil portents. The **Enemy in Shadows Companion** includes a simple system for handling weather, but given the increasingly Chaotic nature of events in Altdorf, you could also decide to change the weather however you want, whenever you want, especially if the Players are slowing down and need a new challenge to galvanise them. You might roll some dice just for effect, or you might simply announce that the weather change has happened. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L347"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L348"></a>In order to keep things controllable, it is recommended that a Chaotic weather effect is fairly localised: no more than, say, 100 yards across. But if you’re up to the challenge of having it affect the whole city, feel free! 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L349"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L350"></a>Here are some suggestions for Chaotic weather effects, but feel free to use your imagination and invent more. Remember what the Characters have been through on the earlier parts of the campaign: if they find themselves pelted with something that stirs a memory or strikes a chord — whether it be sausages or kittens — the experience will be all the more memorable! 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L351"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L352"></a>**Rain:** Frogs, fish, blood, pus, beer. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L353"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L354"></a>**Hail:** Pebbles, teeth, hazelnuts, pennies, diamonds, Warpstone. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L355"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L356"></a>**Fog:** Incense, Moonflower vapours, liquefied Ranald’s Delight, sleeping gas ( **Challenging (+0) Endurance** Test or gain _Unconscious_ Condition). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L357"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L358"></a>**Thunder:** Laughter, a line from the prophecy (see page 17), screams, clashing weapons, animal sounds, music. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L359"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L360"></a>**Lightning:** Turns anything it strikes to ice, stone, or some other substance, mimics a spell effect, Pink Horrors or other small Daemons appear where it strikes.
