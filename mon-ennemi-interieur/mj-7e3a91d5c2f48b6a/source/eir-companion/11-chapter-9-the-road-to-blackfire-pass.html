Chapter 9 the Road to Blackfire Pass

Though several optional encounters for the Characters are presented throughout Empire in Ruins, GMs who wish to extend their campaign may find it useful to have on hand on additional NPCs to use while the Characters are travelling to or returning from Blackfire Pass. Two of these are presented here, the deserter Gregor Smiesdorf, and The Wandering Scholar, a surprisingly well informed traveller who may be able to fill in any blanks faced by players who are struggling to see how everything fits together.

Leaving It All Behind

Gregor Smiesdorf joined the State Troops not out of any particular sense of patriotism. He rather took the Emperor’s Schilling for the simple reason that he was in grave need of a schilling or two. The winter of 2504 was particularly hard, and being the fourth son of a poor farmer Gregor was first out the door when stores ran low. He signed up with the State Troops, who provided a warm bed and decent food, if not much by way of actual safety.

Gregor served loyally for many years, and was involved in battles with beastment, orcs, and worse. His most oft-repeated tale is that he helped Markus Wulfhart, the Imperial Huntsmarshal, slay Malathrax the Might, a Doomfire dragon that had plagued the Reikland for some years. This is truein a roundabout way — Gregor was with a unit of pike that swept the flanks for beastmen ambushers while Wulfhart and his monster hunters actually slew the beast.

Still, in all his service Gregor has never had to draw a blade against his fellow citizens of the Empire, something which he finds incredibly distressing. When word came of the rising tensions, and civil ware began to seem inevitable, Gregor fled the army.

If questioned Characters will find Gregor to be an excellent source of information on the comings and goings of beastmen near Diesdorf, and he will be able to give them a rough disposition of the forces of Drexol the Extinction, though he is not aware of Drexol himself. He is a capable fighter, and if offered payment, or even a good meal, he will assist in the defence of the shrine.

Since deserting, he has mostly made his home in the woods near Diesdorf, not terribly far from the Shrine of Wolfshügel. He is a practiced woodsman, and more than capable of staying ahead of the Beastmen who roam the forest. He finds this perilous existence preferable to facing his fellows in combat, though he fully expects it eventually the forests to be the end of him. Though only a few months have passed he is already somewhat bedraggled, and getting just desperate enough to waylay some passing travellers, regardless of how well armed they may appear. Still, banditry does not sit well with Gregor, and he will not draw blood unless it is to defend himself.

If you are using Gregor, then the reason for Sergeant Major Oldheim's presence in the forest is because of his search for the deserter. Oldheim does not wish to kill — he respects the man greatly, and views his fellow soldiers as family. Oldheim hopes that if he can get Gregor to return with him, he may be able to save him from the noose. If the bearers of Ghal Maraz were to vouch for Gregor, this outcome would be all the more likely.

Timeline of the Daemon Sheerargetru, Its Fell Deeds, and Imprisonment

All dates given list the Imperial Calendar.

C. -5500 IC

The Coming of Chaos. Sheerargetru entered the world along with a horde of its brethren, slaying, corrupting, and changing all that they touched. Even as the lands were covered by the corruption of Chaos, as Warpstone rained from the skies and Daemons walked openly in the world, the Dwarfs retreated beneath the mountains. In those times their Gods were with them still and walked among them, and the need for great weapons to turn back the flood of Chaos was clear. It was towards this cause that Smednir, the son of Grudni and Shaper of Ore, set out to create the greatest Runehammer that would ever exist.

By now Sheerargetru had become established as a mighty servant of Tzeentch, having conquered much and slain many Dwarfs and Elves who had foolishly attempted to defend their homes. The Daemon had begun its first true experimentations in the arts of prognostication, and was of course aided by its Tzeentchian nature. In this way it saw what Smednir was attempting and recognised the threads of fate which spelled out a simple truth — the hammer would be named Ghal Maraz, and it would be the end of Sheerargetru. The Daemon failed in its attempt to corrupt the hammer or its maker, and rather than risk the vagaries of fate it fled far from the realms of Dwarfs.

C. 50 IC

Like many of its kind, Sheerargetru viewed the rise of Sigmar with distaste, and perhaps the slightest hint of fear. The Empire he forged in this lifetime was a change, yes, but any Daemon of Tzeentch could see it would bring the sort of order their kind abhorred. Sheerargetru had special cause to fear the man, as he carried with him the hated Ghal Maraz, the weapon fated to end the Daemon. Therefore, as Sigmar began to step out of the pages of history and into the lofty realms of myth and perhaps even divinity, Sheerargetru struck.

The Daemon sent prophetic dreams to Sigmar, some truthful, some false, and lured him far from his allies. Whether Sigmar knew he was being drawn into a trap is debateable, but regardless, even in his later years, the first Emperor was the Daemon’s match. They battled deep beneath the earth, and Sheerargetru was sealed away by the magic of the Runehammer and the might of the one who had wielded it. Ater this Sheerargetru knew nothing of what became of Sigmar, though the knowledge that it was overcome by a mortal man haunts it still.

Sheerargetru despiared at its prison, recognising that it had played directly into fate's hands by confronting Sigmar. It would not be so foolish again.

C. 150 IC

The Dwarfs, noting the great trials that would likely be faced, provide another runehammer to the fledgling Empire. Knowing that the memories of manlings are short, they expect the hammer will come to be seen as Ghal-Maraz, though dwarf honour means that they never quite say as much. Over time they are proven right, and indeed even the hammer itself seems to fall for this ruse. At times, when weilded in the defence of the Empire and acting in Sigmar's name, it takes on powers that no Dwarf smith of this age could have imbued it with. Still, the shame of duplicity lingers, and would eventually give rise to The Chain, a cabal of Dwarfs who would one day aid in the return of the true hammer.

C. -3000 IC

Sheerargetru was one of many Daemons who made their home in what would become the kingdom of Nehekhara. There the Daemon tempted, corrupted and warped all that it touched, ever fearful of the weapon it knew to be its bane. The Daemon persisted there for an age, creating a minor cult of its own. Ultimately an alliance of desert gods lead by Ptra, whose cleansing light was anathema to all servants of the Ruinous Powers, rallied against the Daemons. Sheerargetru and all its kin were driven from the desert and fled to the far north. Sheerargetru chafed at this treatment, but foresaw the ruin that would one day befall Nehekhara, and laughed.

ALTERNATIVE EMPIRES

50-1152 IC

The Early Empire. During the first millennia of imprisonment, Sheerargetru could do very little. Ghal Maraz was still fresh, its powers mighty, and the Daemon could only rage ceaselessly as its fate. As time passed, however, it became able to sense more and more of what went on in the world. It saw that the heirs of Sigmar, though strong, often quarrelled. It noted that the forces of Chaos, though driven back, were hardly extinguished. Most of all it considered the foolishness of facing Sigmar head on, and decided that in the future it would be take a more subtle approach.

1248 IC

Sending the presence of the Golden Death Mask of Kharnut in Altdorf, a dozen Tomb Kings awaken and travel to the Empire to retrieve it. Among their armies is a scholar, Sadikur, who retained enough of his wits to note a presence as they passed through the mountains and into the Empire. Though half dreaming, the presence is reminiscent of a being described in ancient texts from the days of Nehekaran myth as a powerful entity capable of working great change. Spurred on toward Altdorf by a will not entirely his own, Sadikur would make war upon the Empire and return to Nehekara, but never forgot the presence in the mountains.

1359-1547 IC

Age of Wars . In the midst of growing civil strife following a succession, war engulfs the Empire. Seeing its chance, Sheerargetru begins its subtle work of influencing vulnerable citizens of the Empire through dream, prophecy, and temptation. The Daemons messages are always garbled, however, and only fragments make it through. Still, this is enough to spur the creation of several cults dedicated to an imprisoned god. These would go on, after several centuries of growth and mutation, to form the Nine Eyes.

During this time Sheerargetru begins warping the nature of the caves by its very presence, with the winds of magic refracting and warping them. Daemons and other vile creatures make the caves their home, slowly being put in place to play their part in Sherargetru’s ultimate freedom.

One such creature, a particularly ambitious Horror, proves particularly reliable. It will eventually take on the guise of Gideon, a human child, and begin testing the weaknesses of the human spirit (and the possibility of summoning greater Daemons) in the town of Bögenhafen.

1714 IC

Sheerargetru has determined it best not to again attempt to defy fate, but to work within its threads. The Daemon sends forth dreams of a great treasure hidden in its cave. It is eager to be rid of Ghal Maraz, but knows that if the time is not right, doom will quickly follow. Adelfried Jarmund is lured to the cave and imprisoned there.

2302 IC

The Great War against Chaos. Sheerargetru rails at being unable to participate, though the Daemon’s increasing sensitivity to the whims of fate means that the ultimate victory of the Empire over the forces of Chaos is no surprise to it.

However, as Magnus reforms the Empire and establishes the Colleges of Magic, the Daemon recognises the opportunity this affords. The Colleges will make Altdorf a focus of magical power, the perfect place for it to ultimately manifest. As power centralises in Altdorf to a degree not seen in centuries, Sheeragetru again rejoices. A single powerful leader is all the easier to weaken or corrupt, an idea that will go on to inform the ethos of the Purple Hand.

2225 IC

Graf Heimholtz, Templar Grand Master of the Knights of the Blazing Sun, leads an army from the Empire to attack the Nehekaran city of Numas. Though eventually turned back, the invasion necessitates a call to arms that awakens many, including the scholar Sadikur.

Unable to return to rest along with his fellows after the war ends, Sadikur instead follows the fleeing remanants of Heimholtz’s army back to the Empire. His long slumber has been wracked by dreams of the presence he felt in the mountains so long ago, and he is certain it has only grown in power. Wondering at the power of the Daemon and what might be accomplished could it be controlled, the scholar will spend two centuries in research. He becomes the first being in mellennia to learn the creature's name, Sheerargetru.

2304-2512

As the day of its freedom draws near, Sheerargetru uses its influence to infiltrate the dreams of ambitious imperial citizens, and through them slowly begins to form a great cult of Tzeentch. This organisation becomes known as The Purple Hand. Its foundation is as byzantine and complicated as the cult itself will eventually become, but through it Sheerargetru has sown the seeds of its liberation, and perhaps its downfall.

The Timeless Scholar

The history of Nehekhara is vast topic, sufficient to give even a scholar such as Quintus Fassbinder pause. Suffice it to say, in ancient times the land was home to a human civilisation that stood as a shining beacon of art, culture, and sophistication. Such times cannot last, however, and the fall of Nehekhara was swift once it came. The terrible necromancer Nagash laid waste to the land, slew much of the population, and was in the process of raising all the dead of the Nehekhara to his service when he was ultimately slain. This half finished spell gave rise, quite literally, to undead who were not merely outside the Necromancer’s control, but who retained a good portion of their previous selves.

Sadikur the Wanderer

Sadikur is one such risen denizen of Nehekhara. He was a learned scholar of some kind in life, of that much he is sure, but Sadikur is less certain of what his precise position was. Far too many of his memories have been lost to the grave for that. However, Sadikur remains a scholar at heart. Called up from his grave to join a campaign of war upon the Empire ago, Sadikur sensed something of Sheerargetru’s presence in the Empire, and found it oddly familiar. At the time he was not aware enough of his own will to pursue the question, but when he was called up from his unquiet grave again to turn back a Empire attack on Numas he knew he could not return without investigating.

That was two centuries ago. Since then Sadikur has learned to pass as a living man, at least to those who do not look too closely. He wears a mask of painted wood, sometimes claiming it is a part of his religious observations, sometimes saying it covers up the scars of a terrible pox. His voice is dry and raspy, and despite two centuries of dwelling in the Empire, the scent of must and desert sand still lingers. Under various names Sadikur has written to countless scholars over the years, and indeed even Quintus Fassbidner knows of him — albeit under two separate pseudonyms.

Sadikur splits his time between a dozen different coaching houses, where he is known as ‘The Painted Wanderer,’ often shortened to just ‘The Wanderer.’ He has not had to kill anyone to keep his true nature a secret, but he would without hesitation. He will happily discuss his research, but long experience has thought him that the Empire takes a dim view of those openly discussing Daemons, and waits for others to raise such matters first.

Sadikur may come into contact with the Characters in a number of ways. Quintus Fassbinder may direct them to the scholar, though he only knows the name ‘The Wanderer,’ and that it belongs to a surprisingly well informed scholar of Tzeentch who frequently sends letters from coaching houses in Averland. Alternatively, Sadikur may contact the Characters if they are open about their quest for the Hammer, as he has become aware that Ghal Maraz is tied to Sheerargetru in some way.

using sadikuR in eMPiRe in Ruins Sadikur is a useful tool for filling in any information gaps the Characters may have. The truth and history of Sheerargetru’s imprisonment are more hinted at than outright explained, as befits events surrounding ancient prophecies and imprisoned Daemons. However, if the Characters find themselves in possession of Ghal Maraz but completely unaware that in retrieving it they have freed a Daemon, Sadikur may prove useful in explaining what is going on. Be sure to have the Characters ask questions of the Wanderer, however. He should be used as a knowledgeable expert who may be interrogated by the curious, and not a shortcut to explaining things the Characters should have found out for themselves.

Sadikur is not a straightforward ally. Though he has not put all the pieces together himself, in life Sadikur was a worshipper of Tzeentch, and a member of the lingering cult Sheerargetru established before being driven from Nehekhara. Long years as an unchanging undead corpse have only heightened Sadikur’s desire for unbridled Change. If the Characters state their simple plan to slay Sheerargetru, this will stir something in Sadikur, and he may well attack, especially if he knows they have Ghal Maraz. This surge of emotion will be a surprise to Sadikur as much as it is to the players, though once he has begun he will see the fight through to the bitter end.
