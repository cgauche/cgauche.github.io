## <a class="line-anchor" id="L4"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L5"></a>Chapter 10 the Bigger They Are
## <a class="line-anchor" id="L6"></a>**Adventure Summary**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L7"></a>**The Bigger They Are** is set in the town of Kenselheim, which can be placed almost anywhere in the Empire. As such it could be dropped into one of the travel sections in **Empire in Ruins** , or employed earlier in the campaign in **Enemy in Shadows** or **Death on the Reik** . The Characters are hired by a nobleman to eliminate the local magistrate’s judicial champion, an Ogre Pit Fighter, so that he can challenge the magistrate to trial by combat in order to reclaim his ancestral lands. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L8"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L9"></a>Mentag the Ogre is a fearsome opponent, so the Characters will have to think carefully about how to defeat him. Lessexperienced Characters will have to be more inventive in dealing with the Ogre, while others may be able to beat Mentag through sheer force of arms. Peacefully minded Characters can conceivably complete the adventure without having to enter combat at all. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L10"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L11"></a>**Arriving in Kenselheim**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L12"></a>The Characters arrive in Kenselheim during a heavy rainstorm, feeling wet, cold and miserable. A stick-thin figure wrapped in a cloak limps out of the guardhouse as they approach, but just waves them past before scurrying back inside. Making their way through the town in search of an inn, they begin to hear a dull roar above the constant pounding of the rain. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L13"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L14"></a>In the town square they find the population of the town gathered around a huge pit. On the far side of the crowd a wooden platform overlooks the arena, upon which stand a group of men and women dressed in furs, observing the spectacle below, where two figures face each other. One struggles through the thick mud at the bottom of the pit, desperately trying to hold onto the rainslick haft of his halberd. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L15"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L16"></a>The Characters can make out the livery of a State Trooper beneath the thick layer of mud covering him from head to toe. Two other men dressed in the same uniform lie against the pit walls. One sits motionless, his head lying lost somewhere in the sludge below. The other struggles vainly to keep his internal organs from spilling from his chest, his pitiful wails barely audible over the roar of the crowd. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L17"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L18"></a>Their opponent is a huge wall of flesh and muscle. A rusty and battered breastplate barely stretches over a wide, round belly, and an open-faced helmet looks like it was forced onto the warrior’s thick, horribly ugly head. Standing almost 9-ft tall, the Ogre doesn’t seem at all slowed by the mud, or the spear jutting out of his left leg. The crowd screams for the kill, while the richer burghers on the platform look on, wagering on the outcome. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L19"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L20"></a>The Ogre lunges forward, battering aside the halberd with his axe. The Trooper barely has time to scream before the blade cuts through his helmet and into his skull. The Ogre kicks the corpse free from his weapon, before turning to the wounded guardsman. He pulls another axe from his belt and tosses it across the pit, finishing the combat with a full crunch. The crowd roars its approval. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L21"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L22"></a>As the screams of the crowd die down, Shufflefoot moves towards the front of the observation platform and pulls himself on to a high stool. His fine bright-yellow coat and long feathered hat make him look well-moneyed, if tasteless, but he seems out of place amongst the wealthy townsfolk. His voice carries over the still-shouting townsfolk. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L23"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L24"></a>_‘Once again the courageous Mentag the Undefeatable has dispensed the judgement of the gods upon the unrighteous! And once again, goodfolk of Kenselheim, let us show our appreciation for Magistrate Etzburger for providing us with this evening’s entertainment!’_ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L25"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L26"></a>The crowd roars once more as the halfling turns to a short, piggish-looking man standing on the platform. Dressed in spotless black and green robes and flanked by three bored guards, Magistrate Etzburger gives the crowd a dismissive nod before turning to leave with the other merchants. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L27"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L28"></a>Shufflefoot continues his patter. _‘Today we have seen three traitors to the Empire receive their fair punishment for their cowardice. Their trial by combat has only served to confirm their guilt, for would not Morr have stayed his hand, or Ulric strengthened theirs, had they been innocent?’_ The crowd screams again, though with somewhat less enthusiasm — many seem to be drifting back to their homes, though some seem to be staying to watch four men try to help pull Mentag out of the pit. Shufflefoot realises that he is running out of time, so he starts to speak even faster as he tries to wrap things up before the crowd disperses. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L29"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L30"></a>_‘Yes, ladies and gentlemen, justice is done, and don’t forget, should anyone wish to see another demonstration of Mentag’s great strength and skill, he is available for hire! Heavy objects moved, stubborn objects smashed, perfect for any of your labour-intensive chores!’_ At this point it is clear no one is listening, so he jumps down from his stool, looking somewhat dejected and letting out an audible sigh. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L31"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L32"></a>**What Happened? Just**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L33"></a>The Player Characters may be curious as to what just happened. If they ask the nearby townsfolk ( **Easy (+40) Gossip** Test), they will be told that the three men were deserters from the local State Regiment, who fled after it was rumoured they were to march to war. They were found wandering through the woods near the town. Knowing they would be found guilty of desertion and executed, they demanded trial by combat. Unfortunately for them, the ruler of the town, Magistrate Etzburger, has hired an Ogre Pit Fighter to answer all such challenges. He has found that it has reduced the number of challenges to his decisions dramatically, and as an added bonus occasionally provides some entertainment for the townsfolk. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L34"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L35"></a>Should the Characters want to talk to any of the other people present at the pit they may attempt to do so now, though everybody seems anxious to get indoors and out of the rain. Magistrate Etzburger and the other wealthy burghers have already made their way to his keep on the far side of the town square, and the guards standing watch outside aren’t in the mood for casual conversation. Mentag is busy seeing to his after-fight feeding, which seems to consist of most of a large cow (for starters), but Shufflefoot is in a more talkative mood. An **Average (+20) Gossip** Test with him (or a **Challenging** one with one of the guards or the other townsfolk, who want to get home) will yield the following information: 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L36"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L37"></a>**0 SL:** Magistrate Etzburger hired Mentag to answer any challenges to his decisions. Shufflefoot deals with Mentag’s financial considerations; the Ogre doesn’t seem to trust anyone else. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L38"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L39"></a>**1 SL** : The Magistrate more or less runs the town. He was appointed by the town merchants. The position usually goes to the wealthiest merchant in the area. He’s held it for almost a decade now. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L40"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L41"></a>**2 SL** : A noble family (the Oberholtzers) once ruled the town, but they lost all of their money years ago and had to sell their lands to the local merchants. They left the town soon after. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L42"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L43"></a>**3 SL** : Rumour has it that someone claiming to be the rightful ruler of Kenselheim went to see the Magistrate last week, trying to get the family lands back. Supposedly the Magistrate laughed him out of the room. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L44"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L45"></a>**Mentag the Undefeated**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L46"></a>Mentag is a happy Ogre. He gets all the food he can eat, the occasional fight (though rarely a decent one), a nice, comfy barn to sleep in and ‘loadsa’ gold. Sure, the ‘hummies’ in town tend to stare at him like he has two ‘’eads or sumfink’, but he likes the way they run when he roars and pretends to be mad. Toddy is Mentag’s best friend. Toddy takes care of gettin’ the food and keepin’ the ‘Maj-ee-strayt hummie happy’. Mentag does the fightin’; Toddy does the cooking. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L47"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L48"></a>Mentag hasn’t said anything because he doesn’t wanna hurt Toddy’s feelings, but he doesn’t really like the way he cooks the meat. He makes it all black ‘n’ crunchy, while Mentag prefers his food to have a little blood in it. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L49"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L50"></a>**Savage the Dog**

He tends to bark wildly whenever he sees a stranger, especially one in fancy clothes or someone more tastefully dressed than his owner. He occasionally bites the ankles of any Elves he sees.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L75"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L76"></a>**A Place to Stay**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L77"></a>The Characters can stay in the _Copper Keep_ , a large inn on the town square. Staying in the common room will cost 12d per night; a private room will cost 12s. The Magistrate has increased taxes recently, so the innkeeper,Erich Steingartmann, has had to raise his prices to make up for it. The same can be found throughout the town — raise the price of any item the Characters may want by a few coins. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L78"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L79"></a>Whether they stay in the _Copper Keep_ or find some other accommodation, Erwin von Oberholtzerapproaches them about an hour later. ## <a class="line-anchor" id="L80"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L81"></a>**Toddy Shufflefoot**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L82"></a>Toddy and Mentag met two years ago. Toddy had been seized by the watch of a small village for  selling fake lucky charms (one of his customers was thrown from a horse and died less than 10 minutes after buying a ‘lucky manticore spine’ from him). Mentag was in the same tiny  cell, arrested for accidentally snapping the neck of one of the farmers who’d accepted a challenge to wrestle him in a four-on-one fight. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L83"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L84"></a>Both were scheduled for execution the next morning, but managed to escape using Toddy’s quick wits and nimble fingers and Mentag’s brute strength. Since then Shufflefoot has worked as Mentag’s agent, making sure he gets a fair price for his services and keeping him out of trouble. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L85"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L86"></a>Toddy left the life of a Moot farmer behind eight years ago, determined to see the world. Unfortunately, the world turned out to be a fairly unpleasant place, and Toddy has the scars to prove it. He’s learnt to live by his wits and to trust nobody. The only people he cares about are Mentag and his pet dog, Savage. He’ll leave either of them if it looks like his own life could be on the line, however — better to mourn someone you care about than be the one mourned. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L87"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L88"></a>_As you are discussing your plans for tomorrow, a well-dressed young man walks up to you. His clothing is well tailored without being extravagant and he lacks the arrogant demeanour you have come to associate with nobles speaking to commoners, yet he has a sword at his side and the unconsciously graceful stride of a fencer. Reaching your table, he inclines his head slightly before speaking, his accent is that of a Reiklander. ‘Please, allow me to introduce myself. My name is Erwin von Oberholtzer. I was wondering if I might buy you a drink, while you listen to a business proposal I have for you?’_

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L102"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L103"></a>**Erwin Von Oberholtzer**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L104"></a>Erwin’s father, Otto,worked himself into an early grave to raise the money owed by the family (because of his father gambling debts) and to ensure his son received a nobleman’s education. Upon his father’s death, Erwin took up the challenge, determined to restore his family to its rightful place and claim the title of Baron for himself. For the past four years he has acted as a liaison among various nobles and merchants, securing financing for ambitious military endeavours or winning advantageous trading rights in exchange for a cut of the profits. In pursuit of his goal he has been completely ruthless, betraying friends and family alike. Finally, he paid off the debt through Haus Coyne, a merchant house in Altdorf, and returned to Kenselheim. But when Erwin tried to reclaim his title before the local Magistrate he was refused. He had anticipated that this might happen, however, and had acquired the services of a swordsman to act as his champion. Unfortunately the Magistrate has a champion of his own, and Erwin is not sure his champion can take him. Not one to give up lightly, Erwin is now looking for some deniable assets (thugs) to help him eliminate the competition and allow him to secure his rightful claim to the Barony.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L118"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L119"></a>**The Lost Lord**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L120"></a>Erwin explains that his family once ruled Kenselheim, until his grandfather spent all their wealth on gambling and women. The Oberholtzers were forced into debt, and they had to hand over their property to a group of merchants as collateral. The family was given 20 years to repay what they owed. Otto, Erwin’s father, worked himself to death trying to pay the debt, at which point Erwin took on the task. Finally, he now  has the gold he needs to pay the remainder of the debt, but the Magistrate has refused to recognise his claim. It would appear that Magistrate Etzburger has grown to like his position of power. With Mentag working as his champion no one can challenge him. He has increased taxes and enforces his rules ruthlessly. The other merchants toady up to him in order to win his favour and avoid his wrath. If von Oberholtzer can’t press his claim within the next three days his lands will pass to the merchants for good. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L121"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L122"></a>Erwin offers to pay the Characters 20 GC each if they can remove Mentag from the picture. He will give them 2 GC each now and the rest once his lands have been returned to him. He doesn’t expect them to defeat Mentag in the pit, as that would clearly be suicide. In fact, he doesn’t care how the Characters get rid of him. Injuring or delaying him won’t be enough, as the Magistrate will just delay the fight until he is able to compete (the Magistrate can postpone the fight for up to a week to find a suitable champion). Therefore, Mentag will have to be either killed or forced to leave Kenselheim for good. Once this is done, von Oberholtzer will issue his challenge, and fight anyone the Magistrate can find to stand for him. (This is a lie! Erwin has hired a mercenary to fight for him, but he wants to appear more noble and righteous before the Characters.) 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L123"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L124"></a>The Characters may decide to check up on Erwin’s story. An **Average (+20) Lore (Local)** Test or an **East (+40) Lore (Law)** Test will tell them that his story is certainly plausible. Enquiring about the von Oberholtzer family or Magistrate Etzburger’s rule is an **Average (+20) Gossip** Test: 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L125"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L126"></a>**0 SL** : The Oberholtzers were the rulers of Kenselheim, but few people remember anything about them. Most of the townsfolk were just children when they left. Magistrate Etzburger has increased taxes and doesn’t like people disagreeing with him. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L127"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L128"></a>**1 SL** :The Magistrate is hard, but he is fair. His judgments are usually even-handed, and the town has done well under his rule. Most folk believe, however, that he is lining his own pockets (this is untrue). Erwin von Oberholtzer arrived in town a few days ago and immediately brought his claim for the ancestral lands to the Magistrate, who denied it. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L129"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L130"></a>**2 SL** : Elsa Steingartmann, the innkeeper’s wife, was a young woman when the Oberholtzers left, but she remembers that the last Oberholtzer lord was a drunken wretch. He spent a lot of his time away in Altdorf, Middenheim or Talabheim, where it was rumoured he lost the family wealth in gambling dens. Von Oberholtzer has a few servants and arrived on horseback, but has no carriage and only a small entourage. He is letting rooms from _Mother Puttkammer_ , which is cheaper than the _Copper Keep_ . 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L131"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L132"></a>**3 SL** :  Amongst von Oberholtzer’s servants is a warrior, a huge woman who has the look of a northerner about her. She was wearing full mail and had a greatsword strapped to her back. This is Gerta, Erwin’s champion. Gerta will fight for Erwin if the Magistrate refuses to grant his claim. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L133"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L134"></a>**Problems Big and Small**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L135"></a>It should be made clear to the Characters that attacking Mentag openly would be a bad idea. Even a band of seasoned warriors might lose a limb or two (or at least a Fate Point). They need to find some way to either weaken him or get rid of him. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L136"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L137"></a>Mentag and Shufflefoot live in an abandoned barn outside of Kenselheim, as the townsfolk didn’t like the idea of an ogre living in the middle of town. While Savage is unlikely to scare a snotling, his irritating yapping whenever strangers approach the barn serves as an effective security system. Shufflefoot sleeps in the loft about Mentag’s stall, as he worries someone might try to eliminate the Magistrate’s champion before a fight. When not fighting in the pit or working for one of the local farmers (who sometimes hire Mentag to perform some heavy lifting) both of them can usually be found here. Toddy is usually cooking, roasting a large side of beef over an enormous fire in front of the barn, while Mentag eats the previous serving. Savage can be seen staring at one or the other, hoping for scraps. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L138"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L139"></a>If the Characters decide to talk to them, Shufflefoot will generally be wary but polite; while Mentag will ignore them, grunt, or tell them to ask ‘Toddy’. He’s happy to let Shufflefoot do all of the thinking and talking, as long as he gets plenty of grub and the occasional chance to beat up or kill someone. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L140"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L141"></a>The Characters have three days to figure out how to deal with Mentag. This should give them plenty of time to observe the odd couple and come up with a plan of action. A few possibilities are given, but Characters should try to come up with their own. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L142"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L143"></a>**Bribes/threats**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L144"></a>Bribing Mentag won’t work, as he’ll just ask Toddy what to do. Shufflefoot is making a lot of money here, so much that it is unlikely the Players will be able to convince him to leave town without a fight. Trying to bribe him will only tip their hand, making the halfling more cautious. On the other hand, Toddy has been expecting trouble like this for some time now. If he thinks that they are in any real danger, he’ll leave rather than take the risk. It will take more than a threat to convince him, but if it is backed up with an attack at some later point, the Characters may be surprised to find that Mentag and the halfling have skipped town the next day. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L145"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L146"></a>**Drugging/weakening Mentag**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L147"></a>The Characters may try to weaken Mentag before going up against him. This can be done in a number of ways. Drugging Mentag will be difficult, as Shufflefoot prepares all of their food himself, and they get their water from the stream that runs behind the barn. Any poison added to their food raw will probably be destroyed during the liberal roasting the halfling gives it (requires a **Very Hard (–30) Trade (Apothecary)** or **Challenging (+0) Trade (Poisoner)** Test). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L148"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L149"></a>If the Characters don’t have any poisons with them they could search the nearby wood. A **Challenging (+0) Outdoor Survival** or **Lore (Plants)** Test will allow them to find some herbs and fungi that can be mixed together to create ‘Taal’s Cleanser’ with an **Average (+20) Trade (Poison)** Test. Anyone ingesting this foul paste must make a **Hard (–20) Endurance** Test or gain 1 _Poisoned_ Condition, as well as suffer the need to move one’s bowels at least once every ten minutes. Usually the taste and smell would be sufficient to warn someone not to eat anything this ‘Cleanser’ had been added to (though a **Challenging (+0) Trade (Cook)** Test could disguise it somewhat), but Mentag will eat anything. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L150"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L151"></a>Another way to sap some of Mentag’s strength would be to hire him to perform an exhausting task, such as helping them cut down and move trees from a section of the forest. Mentag can keep working for hours without getting tired, but even he will start to feel the strain if they hire him for an entire day or two of work. Working with him in such a situation would also be the perfect opportunity to slip him some poison in his food or drink, as Toddy will quickly grow bored watching Mentag work and will drop his guard. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L152"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L153"></a>If the Characters can keep Mentag working through one day (about ten hours) without much rest he will gain a _Fatigued_ Condition, but will recover after a night’s rest. Hiring Mentag will cost 6s a day, but Shufflefoot won’t let the Characters overwork him unless they make a **Challenging (+0) Charm** or **Easy (+40) Bribe** Test. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L154"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L155"></a>**Mob Rule**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L156"></a>The Characters can try to turn the townsfolk against the Ogre. Some are angry because of the increase in taxes, while others don’t like the idea of having an Ogre living amongst them. Many are just looking for an excuse to lash out. Convincing enough of them to riot will take time and subtlety, however. A few harsh words about Ogres at a tavern, a complaint about rising prices at the marketplace, anything to increase the discontent amongst the locals will help, but if they aren’t careful, either Toddy or Magistrate Etzburger may realise someone is stirring up trouble. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L157"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L158"></a>Starting a riot requires 10 SLs on an Extended Gossip Test. Reduce the difficulty of the tests if the Characters think of particularly malicious rumours to spread about either the Ogre or Shufflefoot, or even Magistrate Etzburger. Each Character can make one test each day. If any Character fails by 2 SL, the Magistrate or Shufflefoot notices something is wrong. If the Characters get Shufflefoot out of town, they are only noticed if they fail by 3 SL. If they are noticed, the Magistrate’s guards begin asking questions, and suddenly the townsfolk are much less willing to talk to the Characters (Gossip Tests are now **Very Hard (–30)** ). If they fail another Gossip Test, they are caught by the guards, who attempt to seize them and throw them out of town. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L159"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L160"></a>If the Characters do manage to raise an angry mob, they’ll have to lead it to Mentag. If they don’t do so themselves, the crowd will falter at the sight of the gigantic warrior. A single bellow from the Ogre will be enough to send them scurrying back to their homes. A few hours later the Characters will receive a visit from the town guards, as described above. If they do lead the mob, they will have to get past Toddy, who tries to reason with the crowd. He has no wish to unleash Mentag on his employer’s subjects, especially as he isn’t sure the Ogre would win. A Character can make an **Opposed Charm** Test against Toddy to fire up the crowd. Other Characters can try to assist with supportive comments (yelling _‘death to the Ogre!’_ ). If the Characters win, the crowd charges forward, catching Toddy by surprise. Mentag will start lashing out left and right, killing people indiscriminately. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L161"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L162"></a>You may want to run a few rounds of combat between the Characters and either Mentag or Shufflefoot, but after three rounds the mob forces them apart. Unless the Characters strike a particularly telling blow against Mentag, he will survive, but Toddy is killed by the mob. Seeing the death of his friend, Mentag roars in anguish. Knocking the nearest villagers aside, he picks up the halfling’s body  and runs bawling into the woods, with a very distraught Savage hot on his heels. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L163"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L164"></a>Even if Toddy and Mentag should somehow survive or escape, they decide that they have clearly outstayed their welcome and leave town as soon as possible. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L165"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L166"></a>**Shufflefoot**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L167"></a>If the Characters realise how important Toddy is to keeping Mentag under control they may decide to take him out. More morally inclined Characters might decide to kidnap him and hold him until von Oberholtzer has a chance to make his claim. This is actually a more reliable option, as once Toddy is killed they will have no leverage over the Ogre. If they hold him hostage, Mentag will refuse to fight under any circumstances. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L168"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L169"></a>If Shufflefoot is killed, Mentag will go into mourning, hiding in the barn and refusing to leave. However, once von Oberholtzer makes his challenge, the Magistrate will convince Mentag to fight for him, telling him that von Oberholtzer murdered Toddy. The gullible Ogre believes him, charging into town and bellowing in rage. Von Oberholtzer will flee in terror, last seen galloping away on horseback with Mentag right behind him. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L170"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L171"></a>If instead Shufflefoot  ‘disappeared’ (whether dead or alive), Mentag will be filled with worry and will mope around the barn and town. The Magistrate will try to convince him to fight, telling him that it’s what Toddy would want him to do. The Characters may be able to convince him to leave town, however. An **Easy (+40) Charm** Test is enough to get him to head for the next village, ‘where Toddy is waiting’, if they can come up with a plausible reason for him to have left without saying goodbye. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L172"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L173"></a>**Assassination**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L174"></a>Of course, the simplest option is to attack Mentag and kill him (though hopefully after weakening him somehow). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L175"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L176"></a>The most obvious approach is to kill Mentag while he is sleeping. While it may be an option to somehow intimidate the Ogre and frighten him into leaving, this would be no small feat, as most Ogres love fighting and eating in almost equal measure. The biggest obstacle to getting the drop on Mentag is Savage. The little mongrel is a light sleeper and often wanders around the barn at night. If he notices any of the Characters entering the barn, he will start yapping loudly, waking Mentag and Shufflefoot. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L177"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L178"></a>Shufflefoot also sleeps in the barn and has set up some simple security precautions. The main doors of the barn are shut and barred at night and two ropes attached to a collection of pots and pans are tied to them. The bar can be removed with an **Average (+20) Pick Lock** Test. Opening the doors without raising a racket requires a **Very Hard (–30) Stealth (Urban)** Test against the inhabitant’s **Perception.** If the Players somehow find out about the makeshift alarm beforehand and have access to the right tools (Lock Picks), they can disarm the alarm with a **Difficult (–10) Pick Locks** Test (the planks of the main doors are far enough apart that they can slip the tools through to cut the ropes). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L179"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L180"></a>Instead of coming in through the main doors they could try climbing up to the loft entrance, where Toddy sleeps. Climbing up is **Easy (+40)** and the entrance isn’t locked, but it is rigged with the same alarm system as the doors below. Trying to remove this alarm will be a **Very Hard (–30) Pick Lock** Test, as the Character will have to do so while somehow bracing themself against the outside of the barn. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L181"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L182"></a>If they manage to sneak up on Mentag, they get to make an attack against him while he has the _Unconscious_ Condition. While this would usually be sufficient to kill a human, Ogres are far tougher creatures. Mentag may well survive and will not be in the best of moods. If a fight breaks out Toddy will stay in the loft, firing his crossbow from above after lighting up his lantern. If Mentag goes down or if someone climbs up into the loft, Toddy will escape through the loft entrance. If he makes it to the woods ( a distance of 90yd) he is unlikely to be caught, but he will have no intention of returning to Kenselheim. He could turn up at some point in the future, though, in an effort to gain some measure of revenge against the Characters. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L183"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L184"></a>The Characters may try setting the barn on fire. Unfortunately the recent rains have left the wood of the barn damp and difficult to light. They can start a fire if they use some lamp oil, but this will only warn Mentag and Toddy that they are under attack. They will ready their weapons and charge out the main doors. If the Characters think to block off the main doors then they will try to escape through the loft entrance, or if necessary Mentag will hack his way through the barn wall. After three rounds anyone inside the barn will have to make Endurance Tests each round or gain a _Fatigued_ Condition due to the heat and smoke. The first test is **Easy (+40)** , with the difficulty increasing every round. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L185"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L186"></a>If the Characters get inside the barn they can set fire to the straw piled high inside. Mentag uses a pile of straw as a mattress, and if the Characters can catch him while he is still sleeping they might be able to set him ablaze. Two rounds after the hay is lit, everyone inside the barn will have to start making Endurance Tests as above to avoid gaining the _Fatigued_ Condition. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L187"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L188"></a>The fire attracts attention from the town, and in four minutes two of the town guards will arrive, followed by a dozen more four minutes later. At this point it doesn’t matter, however, as Toddy (assuming he is still alive) decides that Kenselheim has gotten too hot. He and Mentag slip out of town at the first opportunity. Whether Mentag is dead or alive, the Characters have taken him out of the fight. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L189"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L190"></a>**Von Oberholtzer’s Claim**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L191"></a>On the third day Erwin will challenge Etzburger’s ruling against him, invoking his right to trial by combat. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L192"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L193"></a>If Mentag is out of the picture (one way or another), the Magistrate will desperately seek someone to fight for him, even offering one of the Characters 5GC if one of them looks particularly tough. Eventually one of his guards volunteers. In response Erwin reveals Gerta, his own champion. Gerta quickly cuts down the hapless guardsman, who fails to get past her careening flail even once. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L194"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L195"></a>As soon as the duel is concluded, Erwin strips the Magistrate of his position and orders him exiled from Kenselheim. He then begins to move into the keep. If the Characters ask for the remainder of their payment, he will assure them that it will be forthcoming. He just has to finish securing his new position as lord of the town, go over the books, find the key to the treasury, etc. He asks them to come to the keep the next morning, when he’ll give them the rest of their reward. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L196"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L197"></a>**Payment**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L198"></a>The Characters find the keep protected by some of the town guards. The guards insist on confiscating the Characters’ weapons before letting them in. Anyone who refuses must wait outside. The guards don’t bother searching for hidden weapons, however. They aren’t particularly fond of their new lord and don’t feel inclined to be overly thorough. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L199"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L200"></a>Once inside, one of Erwin’s servants leads them to the next floor of the keep. A guard sits on a stool outside Erwin’s new office, his halberd leaning against the wall. The servant stops and stares at him expectantly, until the guardsman realises that he is expected to open the door. Muttering something under his breath, he stands slowly, snatching up his halberd in one hand while pushing open the door with the other. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L201"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L202"></a>The Characters find Erwin sitting behind a massive oak desk, which is covered in ledgers and books. He gestures at some seats placed in front of his desk before turning his attention back to the accounts. Standing to the left is Gerta, dressed in her armour and with her greatsword strapped to her back. As they sit down, Erwin closes the ledger before him and sighs. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L203"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L204"></a>_‘I’m afraid I have some bad news. I know I promised to pay you more money upon completion of your services, but I fear I must beg your forgiveness. It would seem that I was a little rash in allowing Ruprecht Etzburger to leave town so quickly. After looking at his records it would seem that he has embezzled a great deal of money from the town coffers while in office, clearly one of the reasons he raised taxes so much recently. I seem to be in the unenviable position of inheriting a town in debt.’_ 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L205"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L206"></a>Erwin is lying through his large, protruding teeth. Ruprecht never stole any of the town’s money; Erwin just needed an excuse so he wouldn’t have to pay the Characters. If the Characters ask him when he will be in a position to pay, he becomes evasive, insisting that there is no way to know when he’ll have the money. It should become clear to the Characters that Erwin has no intention of ever paying them. He will maintain an easygoing manner as long as they don’t become abusive, at which point he will have them thrown out. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L207"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L208"></a>The Players may harbour thoughts of revenge, but Erwin is now safely ensconced inside his new keep, surrounded by the town watch and his clearly lethal bodyguard. If the Players are smart, they’ll accept it as a typical day in the Old World. If they’re not smart they’ll probably end up in the pit facing Gerta… 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L209"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L210"></a>**Rewards**

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L226"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L227"></a>**Other Characters**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L228"></a>While the most important Characters appear where they do in the adventure, other NPCs may have an impact on the adventure. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L229"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L230"></a>**Gerta Faernmaus**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L231"></a>Gerta left her home in Salzenmund in Nordland after she killed her brother in a fight over a woman. She fled to the Border Princes, where she joined a mercenary band. For the past ten years she has fought, looted and pillaged her way through the Border Princes, Tilea and Estalia, before returning to the Empire. She is currently working for Erwin in order to make some quick money, but is merely biding her time until something better comes along. She is utterly ruthless and surprisingly fast with her Flail. Her one weakness is her overconfidence. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L232"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L233"></a>If Mentag still lives and is willing to fight, things go rather differently. Erwin still makes his challenge out of desperation. If Mentag has been weakened seriously (actually wounded) the Magistrate delays the challenge for a week in order to give him time to recover. He cannot delay the challenge by more than a week, and Erwin’s claim will still be valid, despite the fact that the deadline for him to make his claim will have passed. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L234"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L235"></a>If the Characters weakened Mentag through some less obvious means (tiring him out through work, poison) the fight will go ahead immediately. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L236"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L237"></a>Either way, Erwin will not be pleased, and refuses outright to pay the Characters. The fight is close, especially if Mentag was weakened, but eventually the Ogre’s strength, size, and sheer bloody-mindedness win out. Magistrate Etzburger will order Erwin and his followers to leave Kenselheim and never return.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L271"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L272"></a>**Ruprecht Etzburger**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L273"></a>Ruprecht Etzburger is the current magistrate of Kenselheim. The Etzburger family were once servants of the von Oberholtzers, and one of Ruprecht’s uncles acted as the last baron’s steward and was loyal to the family. Ruprecht cares nothing for the nobility, however. The burghers of Kenselheim have administered the town for 20 years without the aid of some arrogant noble. Ironically he has turned the town into his own little kingdom, ruling over it with the surety of any lord. He is not a cruel man and his decisions are for the most part wise (though not necessarily popular), but he has convinced himself that no one else is fit to rule over the town. Therefore, he has hired Mentag to defend his position and his rulings — even if it means increasing taxes to pay for his service.
