## <a class="line-anchor" id="L4"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L5"></a>Chapter 7 the Changeling
## <a class="line-anchor" id="L6"></a>**The Changeling**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L7"></a>The Changeling personifies the part of Tzeentch’s psyche that is the trickster. He can take the form of other beings, from tiny insects to Greater Daemons. None save Tzeentch know the shape of the Changeling’s true form, even the Changeling has forgotten it. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L8"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L9"></a>The Changeling is a consummate prankster, setting the gods of Chaos upon one another through his japes and schemes. On one occasion he fostered war between Nurgle and Slaanesh by adopting the guise of a Daemonette and stealing the silver apples of knowledge from the Palace of Pleasure. It then morphed into the form of a Plaguebearer and left the fruit to rot in Nurgle’s garden. He sealed the entrance to Khorne’s citadel shut when the Blood God was away campaigning, forcing Khorne to shatter his own proud gates when he returned. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L10"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L11"></a>Although Tzeentch loves to take credit for the Changeling’s schemes, very few of the Daemon’s adventures are carried out at his patron’s direction. That many of his pranks have resulted in terrible wars is of no concern to the Changeling. He loves the discord of conflict, for it breeds opportunity to dismay. He impersonates messengers and generals to disrupt strategy wherever possible.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L19"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L20"></a>**The Changeling in Empire in Ruins**
## <a class="line-anchor" id="L21"></a>**Formless Horror**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L22"></a>This Trait is unique to the Changeling: no other being anywhere possesses it, except possibly Tzeentch itself. Once per round, the Changeling may shape change instantly into an exact copy of another creature: either a typical creature for its species, as presented in the Bestiary of the WFRP rulebook, or a specific individual whom the Changeling has studied for a number of hours equal to the creature’s Intelligence Bonus. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L23"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L24"></a>In Enemy in Shadows it was promised that the Changeling could be used to further complicate matters in Empire in Ruins. That a daemon of Tzeentch is capricious ought to go without saying, but of all servants of the Great Mutator the Changeling is perhaps the most mischievous and resourceful, with a talent for shapeshifting and adopting alternative identities that places even Gideon’s multifarious metamorphoses in the shade. But given that the Changeling is a servant of Tzeentch, and possesses shape-shifting abilities, what could it add to the course of the adventure that Gideon could not? 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L25"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L26"></a>Upon adopting a shape, the Changeling instantly gains all of the standard Traits for that creature. In addition, it may use any of the target creature’s Characteristic scores in place of its own. When impersonating an individual, it has full access to all of the individual’s skills, but not their memories.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L29"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L30"></a>**Gideon as an Aspect of the Changeling**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L31"></a>The simplest way to utilise the Changeling in Empire in Ruins would be to use Gideon as an aspect of the other Daemon. Gideon is a powerful shapeshifting Daemon of Tzeentch, but he is less potent a creature than the Changeling. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L32"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L33"></a>In this case the Changeling could itself have become compromised, cursed by Tzeentch of impeded through the activity of another Chaos god. In this scenario Gideon is a weak projection of the Changeling, working relatively incognito. The ritual at the end of Empire in Ruins is not just intended to free Sheerargetru, but to restore the Changeling to full power. Its appearance in the Volkshalle will add even more chaos and confusion to the finale. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L34"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L35"></a>**The Changeling as Rival to Gideon**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L36"></a>Another way the Changeling could be employed is as a rival of Gideon. Tzeentch may well be the patron of both Daemons, but this does not mean that they necessarily work towards the same ends. Tzeentch’s plans are so ineffable and labyrinthine that it is plausible that even his Daemonic servants may find themselves pitched against one another in order to serve a greater plan. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L37"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L38"></a>**The Changeling as an Ally of Gideon**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L39"></a>Where the Changeling could make the most direct impact on the adventure is as an assistant to Gideon, making sure that the ritual used to summon Sheerargetru goes off as well as it can. The most direct way the Changeling will try to do this to get the Characters to retrieve corrupt objects during their journey that will help top fortify Sheerargetru when he manifests in the Volkshalle. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L40"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L41"></a>The Changeling gets four chances to influence the Characters in the way during their return journey with the hammer. In each of these occasions the Changeling avoids the Character with Ghal Maraz, and leaves the area quickly if Ghal Maraz is flourished. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L42"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L43"></a>In any tavern or club the Characters stop at during the return journey the Changeling manifests as a gambling man called Rudi Böttcher. Rudi looks hangodg and desperate, with wet eyes that seem permanently on the verge of tears. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L44"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L45"></a>As a rival to Gideon the Changeling may want to see the summoning of Sheerargetru delayed, subverted, or prevented. There are several ways he might do this. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L46"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L47"></a>He may take the shape of one of the crew of the Carroburg, ensuring that the vessel is dogged with problems, leaks, supply shortages, mysterious deaths and other mishaps. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L48"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L49"></a>He may take the form of guards or soldiers in the employ of Marius Leitdorf or Countess Emmanuelle von Liebwitz. These folk may try to delay the characters by revealing them when they are trying to sneak through or hide in Streissen or Nuln. They may also make wild denunciations, accusing the Characters of having sympathies with Sudenland (to Emmanuelle) or the Alptraums (to Leitdorf). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L50"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L51"></a>He could take the form of Cruzzik Cacklespit or one of the other leaders of the Madcraw clan, ensuring that the Goblins put up much stiffer resistance to the Characters as they make their way through the gorge. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L52"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L53"></a>He tries to engage the players in a game of Red Empress, but he is having a run of bad luck, and soon all he has to play with is an old medal awarded to his grandfather by an Estallian Duke. The medal is gold and bears a magical symbol. It is worth 2 gold crowns if it is pawned. The medal is actually a magical artefact that attracts the winds of magic and adds +1 SL to any successful Channelling Tests the wearer makes. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L54"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L55"></a>Benni Shorthill is a travelling Halfling herbalist who passes the Characters on the road or river. He has a touselled thatch of red hair and is missing all his incisors. He offers them a discount on a case containing 6 Healing Draughts. Normally this would cost 3 gold crowns, but Benni is proud of his new recipe and says the case may be purchased of a single crown. If one of the Characters is injured Benni even demonstrates the efficacy of his potions on them.  The Healing Draughts are all effective, but the fifth bottle in the case is filled with Daemonic ichor and is most definitely not good to drink. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L56"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L57"></a>At the Wulfshugel the Changeling manifests as an aging veteran called Gerd Heideck. Gerd is a bent-backed and bald man with a deeply lined face. His right cheek is a mass of scar tissue and his right eye is missing. ‘Backfiring handgun’ he explains to anyone who asks. He has found an artefact on the hill, a small stone with a spiral diagram etched onto it. ‘You ought to hang on to it’ he tells one of the Characters. ‘These devices were left behind by those who raised the Oghams, it’ll bring you luck.’ The device does indeed bring luck, as the Character who bears it benefits from an additional Fortune Point per session until the manifestation of Sheerargetru. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L58"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L59"></a>Vroni Dachdecker is a young pedlar working the roads near Altdorf. She is a short girl with a pronounced limp. She sells arrows, bolts and slingshot bullets. If the Characters need any arrows or bolts, she will sell them a quiver full, decorated with bright blue feathers. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L60"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L61"></a>These items are all imbued with ritual significance pertaining to the summoning of a Daemon, and if they are present when Sheerargetru is being summoned they effect the summoning in the following ways.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L62"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L69"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L70"></a>**The Changeling as Herald of War**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L71"></a>The Changeling may not have any direct part in the events of the adventure whilst still being busy. Throughout many of the Empire’s provinces the servants of Tzeentch are engaged in ensuring that the Turmoil goes ahead as quickly and with as much intensity as possible. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L72"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L73"></a>The war has been building for some time, but even so it not easy to break down old alliances, beat plowshares into swords, and deploy armies to the field. Thanks to the efforts of the Purple Hand and their co-conspirators in the various Eye cults throughout the Empire the nation is poised to descend into violence far more quickly than an observer might otherwise imagine, but a shapeshifting daemon can really help exacerbate matters. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L74"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L75"></a>During the course of Empire in Ruins the Characters may be in the company of many scheming nobles who might drop some evidence that they, or the people around them, have been manipulated by a shapeshifter. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L76"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L77"></a>**On the Sky Wolf**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L78"></a>Baron Heinrich, shortly after picking up the characters in the Sky Wolf: ‘It had all been going so well. I had just about convinced Hans-Hals von Tasseninck that the Krieglitzes were sincere in their desire to build brides and hold a thorough inquiry into the death of the Crown Prince. I had him ready to call for a stop to all the border disputes and even punish any of his vassals who refused to refrain from further violence. Then, out of nowhere, Gustav von Krieglitz himself stormed into the chamber, grabbed the brim of von Tasseninck’s hat and yanked it down over his face, screaming all the while about the wages of slander. Really put an end to any hope of a constructive outcome. I did all I could to calm Hans-Hals, and then went off to give Gunther a piece of my mind, but he denied he had so much put a hand on the other man, or even entered the chamber. But as Verena is my witness I saw him with my own eyes.’ 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L79"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L80"></a>**In Middenheim**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L81"></a>Veiran Thugenheim, to a fellow Middenheimer at the reception in the Middenpalaz, and only if the Character listening in is able to successfully eavesdrop: ‘The Baron’s informants are among the best in the business but if this Gausser fellow is able to act as he seems to be acting, he must have information coming to him constantly and accurately. Several times we have sent out a squadron of Knights to intercept reivers from Nordland, and they evade capture every time. They are even able to work around our squadrons and lay waste to farms and villages whilst we chase after shadows.’

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L101"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L102"></a>**In Averland and Nuln**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L103"></a>Virtually anywhere the Characters stop on their journey there will be similar tales. Military forces happen to be directed through uncannily precise, or terribly misleading, information, into being just where they need to be, or don’t need to be, in order to cause the war the develop much faster and more fiercely than it otherwise might. Regiments on manoeuvres find themselves arriving at villages at the same time as raiders poised to pounce on them, wagon trains are ambushed by Beastmen or Goblins, hostilities are declared between nobles before they even realise that they had swollen their fighting retinues to massive capacities. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L104"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L105"></a>In the aftermath of Empire in Ruins the Characters may decide to chase these stories down, and in many cases they will find that the captains, quartermasters and noble lords who fought the war cannot account for strokes of good fortune, ill luck or exacerbating circumstances that contributed  to the conflict.
