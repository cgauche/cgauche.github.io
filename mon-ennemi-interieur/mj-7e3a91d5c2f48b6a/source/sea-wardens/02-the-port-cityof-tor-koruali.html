The Port City of Tor Koruali

The defensible port capital of Cothique is equally difficult to reach by land and sea. Rugged cliffs, defensive towers, and powerful enchantments deter attacks from war fleets, while land invaders face a long march along the rocky and mountainous peninsular ridge. However, the city’s reliance on an aqueduct and a few precious wells leaves it vulnerable to siege. Strategically positioned for both mercantile and military force projection, Tor Koruali has become one of Ulthaun’s richest trading ports in recent centuries, second only to Lothern.

Tor Koruali spans almost half the width of the peninsula at its narrowest point, and a significant portion of the city is tunnelled into the shale cliffs. Though the divisions between the social classes are more subtle in a High Elf society than it is in those of Humans, the city’s physical stratification highlights certain inequalities. Sea Elves from the island houses bargain in the snugs and private rooms of harbourside taverns at the base of the cliffs, whereas nobles eschew the docks, preferring instead to plot and scheme in the white marble towers of their palaces overlooking the bay.

Citizens of Tor Koruali are tough, independent, and enterprising, yet their adventuring and open-mindedness lead High Elves of the Inner Kingdoms to regard them as possessing a degree of moral laxity. The city’s traders love haggling at market stalls and carousing in dockside taverns. Goods that cannot be acquired through trade are plundered from foreign lands. Few exotic goods are ever banned outright in the city’s open markets, providing ample opportunity for citizens to enjoy luxuries from every corner of the world. Agents of the Dread King of Naggaroth regard Tor Koruali as a relatively easy target to infiltrate for this reason.

The city’s demographic makeup is cosmopolitan by the standards of other kingdoms of Ulthuan. Non-Elven merchants are welcome throughout the harbour district, where business and political deals are conducted by proxy with agents of Elven nobles. Despite their presence, they are seen less commonly elsewhere in the city. Unless foreign visitors are sponsored by a noble or a respected resident of Tor Koruali they are not granted permission to set foot in the upper city or explore Cothique’s mainland. Of course, Elves often use the leverage of promising such sponsorship to their own advantage.

History of the City

Tor Koruali was formerly a mining town, founded on a peninsula rich in rare minerals. Originally inhabited by Sea Elves, minor Elven nobles, and foreign labourers, the town’s remote location made it undesirable to Elves who were not engaged in the minerals trade. During the Sundering, Cothique’s capital of Anirain was razed by the Hag Queen Hellebron. Cothique’s princes lobbied endlessly for their own cities to become the new capital, so eventually Tor Koruali was chosen to maintain the balance of power.

When Finubar re-established contact with the Old World after Ulthuan’s long isolationist era, trade began to flourish in Tor Koruali. The city’s location was now an asset, and its newfound prosperity brought rapid growth. Phoenix King Bel-Hathor recognised the need to weave protective enchantments on the Shifting Isles, but despite these precautions, Morathi sacked the city at the head of a Norscan army in 2326 IC. After this invasion, Tor Koruali was rebuilt and fortified.

Politics

Cothique’s ruling council is led by Prince Dalloran and convenes at the Royal Palace of Tor Koruali. The kingdom’s capital is a hotbed of intrigue where the other six princes and princesses form coalitions and make backroom deals for tie-breaking votes, both to serve their cities’ interests and to further their own personal ambitions. Phoenix King Finubar has a longstanding relationship with the nobles of Cothique, and he and Prince Dalloran hold each other in sincere high regard, so he rarely interferes in the kingdom’s politics unless they present a security issue that would place the other kingdoms of Ulthuan at risk.

Some of the kingdom’s princes and princesses are influenced by cults of pleasure and luxury, or the bloodthirsty cult of Khaine, that seek to undermine Cothique and turn its rulers against each other. When such sensitive matters are brought before Prince Dalloran, council members profess virtue whilst pointing to the moral failings of their peers. In truth, none of Cothique’s rulers are blameless, because the luxuries that merchant adventurers bring to their courts are gained by spilling blood in lands abroad.

Adventuring in Tor Koruali

Though the Elves of Cothique are no strangers to war, most of the encounters described in this gazetteer focus on intrigue and subterfuge. To establish expectations upfront, Characters should be warned by authorities that fighting will not be tolerated in the city. Even a minor tavern brawl may result in imprisonment. However, there are plenty of opportunities for combat encounters in the archipelago, and the Forest of Cothique.

Non-Elven visitors should be made to feel like strangers in a strange land. Normal subterfuge tactics like shadowing or eavesdropping are ineffective when you are a conspicuous outsider who does not speak the language. Elven guides and interpreters can be hired to help Characters navigate the cultural and communication challenges posed by this environment.

First Impressions

A visitor’s first impression of Tor Koruali will vary depending on their experience of cities in general. Visitors from the Old World will immediately be struck by the beauty of Tor Koruali’s buildings and the architectural majesty of its tall marble towers and wide colonnades. They will be astonished at the manicured perfection of the green open spaces and the cleanliness of the streets, for even the poorest parts of the city are planned and tended to with more care than the palaces and private gardens of the Old World’s wealthiest aristocrats. They are also struck by a certain lassitude of the place. Many of Tor Koruali’s buildings stand empty when not in use or are occupied by only a few residents. Small and scattered groups of Elves wander the walkways and parks, speaking quietly and earnestly to one another. Only by the docks is there an echo of the activity and noise to rival the vitality and bustle of a Human port.

To other High Elves, particularly those of the Inner Kingdoms, the perception of Tor Koruali is quite different. They see it as a rather mean and utilitarian place, its underground chambers a grim necessity of war, and its bustling docks a raucous and malodorous site, disordered and unclean. As a port it may be compared to the capital of Lothern, which is also a busy and disordered place to many High Elves, though at least it has the saving grace of being the vast hub to which all of Ulthuan looks to for leadership and protection. Tor Koruali is a poor and obscure backwater in comparison.

Developing Intrigues

Throughout the guide to Tor Koruali and its inhabitants, several Developing Intrigues are referenced periodically in boxed text. These gameplay situations involve multiple NPCs and are intended for use with the Intrigue system provided in the High Elf Player’s Guide . The Developing Intrigue will give a guide to how a particular situation develops without the characters’ involvement, as well as what might occur if they introduce a power imbalance.

These intrigues involve several characters and places throughout the city, and each differs in the help (or hindrance) they might provide and what leverage might be exercised over them. Information about each intrigue may be found in many places, but a summary of the most critical factors governing each intrigue is given here.

  • Prince Dalloran’s Allegiance. Newcomers to Tor Koruali will obviously benefit from courting the favour of Prince Dalloran (page 26) but must avoid the counter-intriguing of Cireon Fleetcrest (page 66) to do so. Helpful characters include Fialthra Swiftprow (page 54) and Princess Adranna (page 26).

0 Prince Dalloran’s Judgement. If the Characters make themselves unwelcome in Tor Koruali, they may have to mollify Prince Dalloran to mitigate punishment. Prince Dalroth (page 44) may enjoy making things more difficult. Prince Interik (page 24) and Judge Bel-Kalandus (page 46) may be helpful contacts, and the latter may be compromised if his addiction is discovered. Prince Dalroth and Prince Dannor ensure their enemies receive harsh judgement.

  • Smuggling Showdown. Princess Trasinaya (page 23) is the mastermind of a smuggling operation. Princess Litharys (page 38) is trying to put a stop to it. Illorahin Seabrow (page 49) may provide helpful information.

  • The Mirkwine Sting. Agents of Morathi distribute contraband mirkwine using the Grey Oak (page 50). The drink is popular with students at the Naval Academy and some sailors at the Naval Base (page 37). Princess Litharys (page 38) has her suspicions that the Grey Oak is a site of interest but incorrectly suspects the proprietor, Torrelain Moonrose (page 51), of being a main supplier. For her part, Torrelain is desperate to see the real culprits exposed. Prince Dalroth and Prince Dannor (page 44) are mirkwine enthusiasts.

0 Leyline Dispute. To maintain a network of leylines in the Wasteland, Prince Erione of Tor Vael (page 24) bribes Imperial merchants into buying waystones. The Fellowship of Horinal, which includes Prince Salissael (page 23) as a member, seeks to block Erione’s deals. Herwig Algnar (page 57) can advise Characters and may hint that he has heard of Dark Elf involvement.

  • Withering Starwood. Starwood saplings wither en masse. House Spireleaf’s Aerescael blames this on the Spite Ceyluii (page 62), who regularly visits the grove. Prince Dalloran (page 26) could provide some context if he is consulted.

  • A Poisonous Plot. Several of these intrigues may lead the Characters to suspect a Dark Elf mastermind behind a sinister plot. Indeed, this is so, for the Druchii Assassin Shadowblade (page 74) is compromising Tor Koruali’s defences to enact a plot to poison the rivers of Cothique.

Law and Crime

Tor Koruali’s rulers have struggled at times to maintain law and order. Fighting and theft are therefore punished severely, and law enforcement requires more manpower than is typical for Ulthuan’s cities. The streets of the city are patrolled by members of a regiment of spearmen called the Scions of Mathlann.

Three different courts of law hold authority in Tor Koruali, though each one has a different purview. Crimes committed by visiting merchants, adventurers, and Sea Elves are tried at the House of Law. Cothiqui citizens and nobles are tried at the Royal Court. The highest court of law represents the phoenix king and is located at the Chamber of Accords. The city’s only prison is considered rather lavish and comfortable by Human standards.

Outlines of what constitutes a Moderate, Serious, or Heinous Crime can be found in Deft Steps Light Fingers . Elves are more likely to consistently match punishments to crimes and tend not to devise the creative and grotesque punishments that might be meted out in the Old World.

Thauma Ataractic Gems

Beautifully crafted bracelets are enchanted and inlaid with small faceted thauma ataractic gems. These are made of various metals — the more precious, the higher the wearer’s status. They cannot be installed, removed, or broken without magical means. The gem allows the wearer to be traced using the Second Sight Talent (see Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay , page 144), by following its signature in the flows of the Winds of Magic. Any act of violence by the wearer triggers dormant spells stored in the gem, often Shock or Sleep (see Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay , pages 241–242), though others are known.

Trade and Commerce

Cothiqui traders are roguish and free-spirited by nature. Merchant adventurers flagrantly ignore the Treaty of Amity and Commerce which governs Ulthuan’s trade with Marienburg and the Old World. If you are using the Maritime Trade rules from Sea of Claws (page 135), Tor Koruali has a Surplus of Ship Parts (+1), and a Demand for Timber (+1). Other cargos available here are Luxuries, Salt, Saltfish, and Wine.

The Legacy of Bel-Korhadris

The seventh phoenix king, Bel-Korhadris, also known as the Scholar King, was known for his love of learning, sponsorship of the magical arts, and considerate rule over Ulthuan in a time of peace. He is not well-loved in Cothique, for his cautious and studious approach to leadership does not appeal to the kingdom’s character. He led no daring exploration, and the Dark Elves were quiescent during his reign, meaning no military campaigns were waged against them.

The merchant adventurers of Cothique find more to admire. During his reign, Bel-Korhadris sponsored monster hunts within the Annulii Mountains, a practice that echoes their own tradition of slaying great beasts at sea.

The highborn of Cothique, its mages, and those who help to practice and enforce a kingdom’s laws, pay great respect to the Scholar King. There is nothing particularly unusual about this, as Bel-Korhadris is fondly remembered by such sections of society throughout Ulthuan.

Visitors to Ulthuan are wise to remember that the High Elves regard their phoenix kings with high regard, and that even those phoenix kings who blundered in war or exhibited unlikable qualities as a leader are still regarded as historical figures of note. No High Elf will be happy to tolerate an outsider showing disrespect to a phoenix king. Should an outsider be discerning and respectful in their opinions of a particular king, it may grant them bonuses to Fellowship-based tests with sections of High Elven society.

Districts of Tor Koruali

Tor Koruali’s districts are largely concentrated on the surface, but they also extend underground into the Crafting District, where luminous subterranean grottos house workshops, storage cellars, and other unsightly or noisy premises. Non-Elves are only allowed access to the docks and taverns at sea level unless a notable resident grants them sponsorship.

Palace and Military District

Overlooking the harbour are six grand palaces, flanked by mansions belonging to Cothique’s wealthiest highborn families. The district’s wide boulevards are lined with rows of elm and poplar trees that have been magically sculpted for perfect symmetry. Military barracks here house the citizen-soldiers that serve the noble families. They use the square plazas at boulevard junctions to train, drilling in shieldwall formations and swordplay against a backdrop of stunning topiary and shimmering fountains. Other than the soldiers, only nobles and their servants are typically permitted in the Palace District.

Merchant District

High Elven nobles conduct most of their business in surface-level conference halls in their mansions or at the Hall of Trade, but most of the day-to-day trading in the city is done in the Merchant District. The district’s subterranean warehouses are stocked with luxuries and fine Elven craftworks, and several merchants also hoard rare minerals. Warded vaults containing magic items for trade are well-guarded. Human merchants and foreign Elves are normally confined to trading in the cavernous underground fish market by the harbour.

The Colonnade

The main thoroughfare is part of a highway that runs the length of the peninsula. Lining the broad street are stone columns supporting a grey-shingled roof, which provides shelter against the elements. The street is home to many vendors whose stalls and shops nestle behind the columns, which are carved into statues of historical figures dressed in classical High Elven style.

Entertainment District

Elves spend much of their copious leisure time attending theatres, art galleries, and museums in the city’s north end. At night, laughter fills the district’s many bars, restaurants, and spas.

Winding streets with poor signage can make the district disorienting for visitors. Artists residing in colourfullypainted houses ply their trades in underground studios and workshops and the paintings, figurines, jewellery, and other ornaments produced by Elven artisans are highly sought after by foreign merchants.

Temple Quarter

Overlooking the entire city from the peninsular ridge, and dwarfing the surrounding shrines and abbeys of individual gods, is the main Cadai pantheonic temple. An atmosphere of divine serenity pervades this sparsely populated quarter. Only Isha’s novitiates and a handful of Asuryan’s priests reside aboveground. The forges of Vaul, worked tirelessly by smith-priests, are located underground. Though hundreds of Elves might congregate at the temple for major rituals, this quarter is usually quiet.

Residential Districts

Tor Koruali’s citizens favour sturdy dwellings of one or two levels, structurally reinforced against wind and rainstorms. Most houses are square or rectangular, with walls built of white stone blocks, protected from the stroms by roofs tiled in heavy blue-grey slate. Despite their simplicity (at least by Elven standards), these abodes are clean and well-appointed. The nobles of the eastern district pride themselves on being more resilient than most. Their ostentatious, storm-battered villas include terraces, turrets, fountains, and gardens that are shielded by magic in inclement weather.

Underground Levels

The network of caverns beneath the city is illuminated by glowstones and naturally phosphorescent crystals in the bedrock. Passages that interconnect the caverns are floored with white tiles, and even sections of intricate mosaics, and their walls are painted with frescos. Magichungry nature spirits gather by the dozens, drawn to the undercity by its enchanted glowstones, but by and large the Elves tolerate these mischievous Spites because they keep the wharf rats at bay.

In between the Crafting District and the dockside Open District are defensive positions, where attacking enemies are funnelled into underground choke points surrounded by positions for archers and bolt throwers. Visitors arriving from the port are ushered into elevation shafts by Sea Guards, then lifted by rope-and-pulley baskets (or occasionally air elementals) to the city above.

Council of Princes

The council of Cothique convenes each season at the Royal Palace to debate important issues. Prince Dalloran, Prince Interik, and Princess Arianne live in Tor Koruali year-round. The other princes own mansions here but usually reside in their home cities. Characters are unlikely to receive audiences with these princes unless they are introduced by a High Elf colleague or encounter them by chance. However, all council members could prove willing to provide permission to non-Elves to access Ulthuan, but will want favours in return.

Crown Prince Dalloran of Tor Koruali

Prince Dalloran is credited with embodying the open-minded character of his kingdom and his tolerant, politically minded, and genial attitude. Whilst he has lived many centuries and is starting to feel the weight of his years, he has yet to select an heir from his children, Princes Dannor and Dalroth and Princess Adranna. He has set them

tests to check on their suitability by increasing their local responsibility. Sadly, their governance thus far has ranged from overly cautious to unsavoury.

  • Regular Activities: Holds court at Royal Palace; business at the Hall of Sea Trade.

  • Sponsorship: Make a visit to the Starwood Groves for diplomacy with Ceyluii (page 62).

Developing Intrigue: Withering Starwood

Prince Dalloran has heard tales of drunken misbehaviour by young nobles within his hunting grounds and believes this may be the cause of starwood saplings failing to thrive, threatening a future shortage of this resource. He believes Ceyluii knows more about this than the Spite lets on, but has not been able to glean whether his suspicion is true. He would have some sympathy with the Spite, as the hunting grounds belong to him and he is frustrated to hear that they are being treated with disrespect. He needs the saplings to recover, so his patience regarding this matter has its limits.

Princess Trasinaya of Tor Andar

A storied hero who assumed her council seat out of a reluctant but sincere sense of obligation, Princess Trasinaya tours Cothique’s coastal cities in lieu of adventuring overseas. She is notorious for her handsoff approach to governance, but her easy-going demeanour belies a subtle mercenary nature. Trasinaya’s absence from Tor Andar deliberately

incentivises piracy, which has the benefit of providing her fleet with necessary military training. A friend to many of the High Elf commanders of far-flung islands and settlements, acquaintances she made during the voyages of her younger days, she carefully manages her reputation in hopes of garnering support for colonial projects.

  • Activities: Hires sailors and mercenaries at the harbour, patronises arts in the Entertainment District.

  • Sponsorship: Work as her servants or envoys for the duration of a stay in Ulthuan.

Prince Salissael of Tor Dynal

The aged Prince Sallisael is an intelligent and cautious ruler who has become neurotic following a series of increasingly deadly attempts on his life. The paranoid prince has called for constant investigations of his own closest advisors, which have subsequently led to numerous executions, and even innocents have been beheaded or banished from Tor

Dynal. Well-informed Elves understand he is in genuine danger, but they cannot guess why. Salissael regards foreign visitation as a security risk after narrowly evading poisoning from an assassin from Tilea. His greatgrandaughter, Kaele, is an accomplished envoy, currently seeking the favour of the Eonir of the Laurelorn Forest.

  • Activities: Hides out in Royal Palace guest wing; business at the Chamber of Accords.

  • Sponsorship: Buy 300 Encumbrance of Brionne grain in Tor Koruali and sell it in L’Anguille.

Prince Erione of Tor Vael

Regarded as something of an eccentric by the highborn of Cothique, Prince Erione is an unambitious, landlocked leader who governs by careful research and consideration. A devotee of Hoeth, he rarely makes a decision before he has read widely about the subject at hand and personally explored any matters of precedent. He frustrates his peers by delaying council

business, but his reverence of Hoeth inspires patience and deliberation. Erione is a friend of King Finubar, served as crown prince of Cothique before Dalloran, and was once mooted by many mages of Saphery to contend for the position of phoenix king himself. His daughter, Nielle, has been sent on a diplomatic mission to the Laurelorn Forest in an attempt to strenghten ties between Cothique and the Eonir.

  • Activities: Researches at Celestial Observatory; business at the Chamber of Accords.

  • Sponsorship: Identify and report an embedded Dark Elf spy or assassin.

Princess Litharys Starshard of Elisia

A gifted naval tactician and theorist, Princess Litharys also possesses abilities as a seer, having understood many of the principles that lie behind the Lore of Heavens. Her talents have not provided her with much in the way of happiness, and her counsel is typically pessimistic, bordering on doomsaying. Litharys openly fears Cothique will fall into decline and

incessantly argues for mercantile and adventurous assets to be kept in a defensive posture close to home, facilitating a better-regulated navy. Her fellow princes can find her lack of enthusiasm deeply dispiriting, though they are forced to admit she is no fool.

  • Activities: Supervises shipbuilding at Naval Base; training drills at the Gymnasium.

  • Sponsorship: Identify and report a Dark Elf smuggling ring.

Prince Interik of Anirain

A ruler-in-exile from a line that already waned, Prince Interik is the first head of his family to have never seen Anirain. Interik lives in Tor Koruali pursuing politics and intrigue in hopes of someday restoring Anirain. Maintaining his position is a constant game of playing tiebreaker in council, and Interik has begun exploiting Sea Elf clan politics for temporal power.

  • Activities: Hosts social events at Royal Palace; patronises arts in Entertainment District.

  • Sponsorship: Recover a lost pre-Sundering artefact from the city environs.

Princess Arianne of Anirain

The city of Anirain has been razed for hundreds of years, yet Princess Arianne rules in exile from Tor Koruali, maintaining her seat through clever politicking and natural inertia. Cothique’s Sea Elf clans, governed by lesser nobility and funded by newly wealthy merchant houses, have been aggressively politicking to grant clangoverned Mistnar the seventh seat, instead of Anirain.

  • Activities: Parties at the Palace of Respite; undercover business in Open District.

  • Sponsorship: Payment of 200+ gold dragons toward the reconstruction of Anirain.

Palaces and Mansions

Waterfront property is premium real estate to the sealoving Cothiqui nobles, and the Palace District on the western cliff overlooks the harbour. Terraces and balconies descend partway down the cliffsides, allowing nobles to bask in Mathlann’s salty glory. Most of the palaces and mansions incorporate cliffside towers from which archers and bolt throwers can shoot.

Palaces

The city’s grandest and most elegant palaces were built to garner prestige or political influence. Half of these palaces are rarely (or never) occupied by their owning families, but are kept in fine repair by their servants on the offchance that they may need to spend time in the city at a future point.

Royal Palace of Cothique

The city’s largest palace hosts the Council of Princes. Prince Dalloran holds court here but prefers to spend his own time at the Nautilus Palace. Prince Interik’s family and retinue occupy one guest wing permanently, and Prince Salissael also resides at the Royal Palace when visiting Tor Koruali.

Nautilus Palace

Prince Dalloran’s family estate is a multi-domed sandstone edifice with spiralling turrets. The crown prince lives here permanently with his daughter Adranna and their servants. Prince Dalloran’s sons favour the Royal Palace for the authority it confers its residents (when they are not sleeping off hangovers at the Palace of Respite).

Starfish Palace

This limestone palace consists of five spires arranged around a central dome. Several years ago, its noble family was condemned as pleasure cultists, and they have been incarcerated on the Shifting Isles ever since. The house’s patriarch was exiled permanently to Lustria. The palace grounds are being maintained by family stewards until their eventual return.

Mansions

There are dozens of mansions in the cliffside districts for GMs to populate with their own characters. Several featured NPCs own mansions in the city.

Princess Trasinaya (Tor Andar)

The princess of Tor Andar owns mansions in multiple cities, but favours Tor Koruali for its arts community. Trasinaya is the target of serious allegations by Princess Litharys. As a result, she spends more time than usual in the city conducting intrigues, meanwhile hiring crews for an impending kraken hunt.

Developing Intrigue: Smuggling Showdown

A lover of the arts with a passion for sculptures and paintings, Princess Trasinaya does anything to protect her collection and supply routes and may look kindly on Characters who offer to help. She hates anyone exploiting her smuggling operation, seeing it as an intricate web of artwork in itself, but shows affection for those who display loyalty or a kindred spirit.

Conch Palace

This circular, columned palace of white marble was funded with recently acquired wealth and is Tor Koruali’s newest palace. It is owned by House Waverider, a Sea Elf family from Mistnar who also own a significant trading house in Altdorf. Many of Cothique’s nobles refuse to acknowledge the Waveriders as true princes.

Palace of Respite

House Emeraldsea of Lothern owns this twin-towered marble palace, which includes cellar caverns for warehousing. The palace has become a party house for the city’s young nobles. Princess Arianne is permanently in residence as a guest. A complete description of the Palace of Respite can be found on page 43.

Prince Skystride (Tor Andar)

This piratical prince seldom visits his Tor Koruali mansion anymore because of the many warrants for his arrest. However, Skystride is prepared to defend the honour of Princess Trasinaya if she is ever summoned to the Pantheonic Court for trial.

Princess Litharys (Elisia)

Cothique’s naval commander spends more time in Tor Koruali than Elisia, which is governed by her husband during long absences. Litharys is on a private mission to apprehend cultists and smugglers. Her accusation of Princess Trasinaya is only partly true — though Trasinaya profits from smuggling, she has no direct involvement in the mirkwine trade.

Chamber of Accords

The four-pointed alabaster tower representing the Phoenix Court functions more like a trade embassy than a palace. Heralds of the phoenix king — and occasionally Finubar himself — reside here during diplomatic visits. A complete description of the Chamber of Accords can be found on page 56.

Prince Erione (Tor Vael)

Tor Vael’s loremaster prince does not attend the city regularly, preferring to send his daughters or agents to negotiate trade deals. Currently, he is planning a project to examine and reinforce the waystone network through the Chamber of Accords. Potent artefacts are delivered to his mansion for inspection prior to storage in the Vault outside the city.

Crown Prince Dalloran of Cothique

Dalloran was elected crown prince two centuries ago, following the Great Chaos Incursion. Under the rule of his predecessor, Prince Erione of Tor Vael, the kingdom was ravaged by Norse and Dark Elf invaders. Though Erione is a respected loremaster and councillor today, during those hard times, he was deemed too close to the Phoenix Court, and his devotion to Cothique was in question. Therefore Dalloran was chosen, for his pragmatism and more modest personal ambitions, to lead Cothique’s reconstruction.

Tor Koruali’s emergence as a mercantile power can be credited in large part to Dalloran. His household, which has owned mines on the peninsula for generations, are savvy sea traders. At Dalloran’s behest, Tor Koruali has nearly tripled its imports and exports by declaring itself a free market. Prince Dalloran’s business acumen exceeds his martial prowess, and though his tall frame looks dashing in a suit of armour, he often delegates Cothique’s military commands to more battle-hardened princes.

Prince Dalloran has retained his crown by bringing wealth to Cothique and catering to each faction within the Council of Princes. Expansionist princes enjoy free licence to explore, raid, and colonise at will, whilst more isolationist princes are satisfied with Dalloran’s naval investments and strict enforcement of ylvathoi trespass laws. However, Dalloran’s moderate political stance will not serve him indefinitely as the council becomes increasingly polarised. Cothique’s princes are demanding bolder leadership in the escalating trade disputes with foreign powers such as Marienburg and Brionne.

Princess Adranna

Whilst Prince Dalroth waits to inherit the crown, Prince Dalloran’s eldest child, Adranna, is viewed by many in Tor Koruali as a better candidate. Adranna is perceptive and guards her father’s interests with affection and a sense of duty, and adventurers seeking sponsorship or trade with Dalloran may be greeted by Adranna, as she keeps her father’s schedule and screens petitioners. Despite her skills as a diplomat, she has no interest in leadership, is indecisive on matters of politics, and prefers to learn about magic rather than statecraft.

Dalloran as Patron

Prince Dalloran is famous across Ulthuan. In many Old World ports, he is known either by name or as ‘the Elven merchant king’. Only the most respectable merchant adventurers receive Dalloran’s patronage. Though he is open to sponsoring foreigners for temporary visits to Ulthuan, Dalloran is very discriminating when choosing trade agents to fly his banner. Merchants under Dalloran’s banner receive +1 SL to Haggle Tests in Skeggi, Marienburg, and Miragliano.

The treaty violations and aggressive business practices of Cothique’s merchant adventurers, such as House Waverider, have earned the Elves of Cothique a reputation for unreliability in foreign ports. Prince Dalloran seeks to undo some of the damage to his kingdom’s reputation, and as such, he is often more interested in the diplomatic merits of a proposed venture, rather than whether it will make a lot of money. Characters who impress him with the likelihood that their ventures will lead to warmer relations with distant nations may find him eager to help.

Dalloran prefers to use High Elf agents and allows them to attend council debates at the Royal Palace. Recently, High Receiver Fialthra has shown him the wisdom of employing foreigners for certain diplomatic missions. Agents of Dalloran are allowed into the House of Sea Trade and the exchange houses in Cothique’s other cities, even if they are not High Elves. Human agents of Dalloran may also have the opportunity to purchase rare minerals and more powerful magical items than would otherwise be available.

Dalloran as Nemesis

As the high justice of Cothique, Dalloran has the authority to exile foreign miscreants from Tor Koruali. Normally, he only lowers himself to such matters at the request of a Cothiqui noble. Dalloran is aware of Cothique’s reputation abroad and wants to avoid rumours of unjust executions spreading to the Old World, so he strives to be firm but fair in his dealings. As a result, death sentences are reserved only for convicted spies or saboteurs who pose future threats to Cothique’s security.

Parties with Dalloran as a nemesis will find it difficult to conduct affairs in Tor Koruali, even if they have not been formally charged with a crime. Dalloran’s children ensure that word of their father’s disfavour spreads quickly through the city. Though not every prince supports Dalloran, political opponents are wary of associating with his public adversaries. Should a known enemy of Dalloran ever try to make a Persuasion Test in Tor Koruali, the GM should impose significant penalties.

Reprisals against offending Characters are usually ordered by Dalloran personally, while the fine details are left to the imaginations of his sons Dalroth and Dannor (page 44). Parties might redeem their status with Dalloran by exposing a plot against him or ingratiating themselves with his children. However, Dalloran is naturally inclined to stand by his judgements, whilst Dalroth and Dannor encourage their father’s intransigence with unctuous assurances that he has made good decisions.

Prince Dalloran’s Judgement

Arrested foreigners who have made enemies of highborn in the city might be judged by Prince Dalloran himself at the Royal Palace, instead of by High Judge Bel-Kalandus at the House of Law. Failure to persuade Dalloran in this arena results in him becoming a party nemesis. His son Dalroth (page 44) sits on the tribunal. As the party’s antagonist, Dalroth viciously attacks their integrity while exploiting Dalloran’s fear of appearing too soft. Prince Interik (page 24) who also sits on the tribunal may be open to bribery. Judge Bel-Kalandus (page 46) can be blackmailed, however, this is a risky gambit requiring tact and discretion. Characters are likely to only have a day of freedom to engage in intrigue before the trial.

Royal Palace

The Royal Palace of Cothique is famous for both its architectural grandeur and the political intrigues that transpire within, for, in addition to being the seat of Cothique’s government, the palace hosts the high court where criminally indicted nobles and citizens of Tor Koruali face trial. Prince Dalloran is the crown regent of Tor Koruali, which formally makes him steward of the palace. In this capacity, Dalloran has authority to preside over the Princes’ Council.

The palace is a marvel of Elven engineering and stoneshaping magic, protruding almost organically from the cliffside. Its majestic outer towers run nearly half the height of the cliff, then rise above neighbouring palaces on the surface. Turquoise coloured flags and pennants bear Cothique’s traditional flying fish emblem alongside Prince Dalloran’s house insignia.

Though royal affairs are conducted aboveground, the palace has many underground vaults and workshops where the kingdom’s richest fineries are crafted by household artisans and servants.

The original palace was relatively modest, dating back millennia to the time when Tor Koruali was little more than a mining colony. After wealth began flowing into the city following the Sundering, the old palace was extensively renovated. Today, it features nine towers and five spacious levels with high, vaulted ceilings. Spanning the lower three levels is a massive, 50-foot arched hallway with stairs ascending to balconies on each level. Four new wings were added to the palace over time to accommodate visiting nobles. Each mansion-sized guest wing, built around a central tower, can accommodate a visiting noble and several dozen of their relatives and attendants.

The Council of Princes meets in a cavernous, vaulted hall around a large, crescent-shaped table made of polished dark hardwood brought to Tor Koruali from Lustria. Some distance from the table is the ceremonial throne of Tor Koruali, made from white marble carved into the form of a giant scallop shell. Prince Dalloran usually sits at the council table during assemblies and only mounts the throne to greet foreign ambassadors.

The Royal Court uses the palace’s council hall for criminal trials. Citizens who face trial stand upon a podium and address a jury of eight nobles seated around the crescent table. On those rare occasions when a noble stands trial, Prince Dalloran acts as judge. Two nobles are appointed by Dalloran to act as interlocutors and advisors. Usually, Dalloran selects Prince Interik and one of his own sons to serve this role.

Cult Intrigues

Nefarious cults are causing strife within the Council of Princes. Many of the sponsorship conditions and courtly intrigues that Characters encounter will involve investigating cult activities. Outsiders are considered useful tools by Cothiqui nobles because they are generally oblivious to past scandals and the subtle gamesmanship that underpins political life in Ulthuan.

Cults of Morathi

Elves who lived through the sack of Cothique 200 years ago harbour an enduring hatred of Morathi and the Norse. Meanwhile, the new generation has no firsthand memory of that war, and many are disinclined to share in their elders’ prejudices. Some of the kingdom’s young nobles — including Prince Dalloran’s own sons — have unwittingly participated in Khainite pleasure cult practices. Most of these delinquents are not aware they serve Khaine, nor that Morathi controls the cult from afar.

Morathi’s undercover Dark Elf agents lace red wine with daemonic ichor and powerful narcotics, then sell it to Cothiqui merchants. This potent dreamwine variant fuels many raucous parties in Tor Koruali, some of which have become violent. Although drunken combat is supposed to be resolved outside the city, illegal duels have been fought in the cellars under the Palace of Respite. Occasionally, bands of mounted revellers binge on the wine for days while embarking on brutal hunting expeditions.

A handful of pleasure cultists have degenerated into a murderous state called Aeskhaine. Unfortunately, Elves seldom recover from Khaine’s madness and the victims of Morathi’s scheme are therefore slain by their cohorts or tracked down and executed by swordmasters of Hoeth. Those few who escape into Ulthuan’s forests rarely survive for long before falling prey to lions, eagles, or worse.

Pawns of N’kari

A cell of embedded Dark Elf spies has lost contact with the Dread King of Naggaroth. Their membership includes sorceresses living in a hidden grotto and assassins operating covertly among High Elf society. These isolated agents have become dupes of the Greater Daemon N’Kari. During the last major Dark Elf invasion, N’Kari was bound and commanded by Malerion. Now, N’Kari is repaying Malerion’s humiliating treatment by using the Dread King’s own spies in Slaanesh’s service.

N’Kari, disguised as an Elven occultist, provided Malerion’s sorceresses with a list of lesser Daemonic true names. After summoning these Daemons, the sorceresses have kept them bound within ensorceled cages in their underground ritual chamber. Dark rites are performed to extract the Daemons’ ichor without destroying them. N’Kari did not tell the Dark Elves that these Daemons’ patron is a minor Chaos God, Zuvassin the Undoer, whose domain is the unravelling of plans.

The Daemons’ noxious ichor is mixed into drugs and poisons for use by the assassins. Poisons are slipped into food at banquets, whilst drugs containing highly addictive ingredients are sold in Tor Koruali. Elves who become addicted to these drugs are pressed into working as messengers or informants for the spy cell, and the servants of Cothique’s ruling princes are favoured targets. Prince Interik’s household aestheticist was recently exposed as an informant, leading to paranoia and mistrust within the Council of Princes.

Aenarion Revivalists

The first phoenix king Aenarion is widely revered as Ulthuan’s saviour, despite most High Elves being aware of his bloodline’s curse and fearing the Sword of Khaine’s baleful influence. On the islands off Cothique’s coast where the Elves face constant Norscan raids, a warrior sect awaits Aenarion’s reincarnation in the body of one of his fated scions. These fanatics believe that Ulthuan will eventually fall to the Norse unless Aenarion’s Chosen draws the Widowmaker from its shrine.

This populist movement is not outlawed, although their motives for coveting the Sword of Khaine are considered highly suspect. If Prince Dalloran knew that these revivalists ultimately wanted to replace him with a descendant of Aenarion, he would probably monitor them more closely. The devotees of Aenarion are vocally opposed by the Cult of the Forbidden Blade.

The revivalist movement originated in the Shifting Isles, where its cultists maintain a secret shrine dedicated to Aenarion. Statues of his famous Cothiqui descendants, Tyrion and Teclis, flank the shrine’s central altar. On the mainland, a cult of personality has emerged around the twins, whose popularity is greatest in the city of Tor Alin, on their father’s ancestral lands. The revivalists have been spreading their hero worship to Tor Koruali, prompting calls for Tyrion to return to his homeland of Cothique.

Shrines and Temples

The centre of religious worship in Tor Koruali is the massive Cadai temple where all eight gods of the pantheon are represented. Other shrines and religious buildings are situated around the main temple, in other parts of the city, and on the outskirts. Shrines serve ritual or practical functions associated with their patron gods. Elven spirituality is a personal matter that does not require formal prayer attendance.

Temples to Mathlann

On the western cliff facing the sea is a temple to Mathlann where storm-shielding rituals are performed. Citizens of Cothique worship Mathlann above other gods, even Asuryan himself. This is a primary reason why the Elves of Cothique are considered strange by those of other kingdoms. Ulthuan’s largest shrine to Mathlann can be found on the Shifting Isles off Cothique’s coast, though as a result, its exact location can never be certain. In other parts of Ulthuan, particularly the Inner Kingdoms, Elven scholars have debated the legitimacy of Mathlann’s membership in the Cadai pantheon. They argue that, as a child of Ellinill, Lord of Destruction, the sea god is often regarded with suspicion. Amongst scholars who revere Mathlann, there are those who question the Cothiqui practice of hunting sea monsters, claiming the hunters play into the hands of the Cytharai goddess Anath Raema. Defenders of the monster hunts insist that to tussle with Mathlann’s children is a divine ordeal and test of their skill and courage.

Pantheonic Court

The religious court convenes in a square, domed hall located by the water park. High Elves enforce their canon laws more liberally than Imperials do. Heresy and blasphemy charges are uncommon, because Elven spirituality is personal and subjective. However, pleasure cult activities and crimes committed by magic are too complex for Elven judges to sentence fairly, so these cases are transferred to the Pantheonic Court.

Typically, verdicts have already been decided before the pantheonic jury deliberates on penances. The jury consists of eight mages who wear ceremonial masks representing the Cadai gods. This jury of eidolons decrees penances befitting the circumstances of the crime. Every minor detail is considered to ensure proper restitution and encourage behavioural reform.

Execution by a swordmaster of Hoeth is reserved for Khainite assassins, serial-murdering mages, and cult leaders who worship the Cytharai or Gods of Chaos.

Library-Temple of Hoeth

Beside the Cadai temple is a library-temple to Hoeth, Lord of Wisdom. The library is staffed by a handful of loremasters who maintain communication with the larger order of Hoeth at Tor Vael using messenger seabirds. Tor Koruali’s loremasters play a role in maintaining the sea magicks that protect the city from invaders. A dozen swordmasters of Hoeth are based here, tasked with investigating reports of cult activity, most of which implicate Sea Elf traders in the dockside taverns.

Shrine to Ladrielle

On the fringes of the Temple District is a minor shrine to Ladrielle, Lady of Mists. Travellers returning from dangerous journeys come to pay gratitude at the statue of a veiled lady. Although Ladrielle is a Cytharai goddess, her worship is not prohibited in Cothique. Before embarking on long voyages, travellers pray to Ladrielle for guidance and protection at the Seven Sisters monument, located at the border with Yvresse.

The Mist Weavers of Ladrielle are itinerant mages who serve as oracles to Elves in search of lost places or people. They are endowed with divine knowledge of many hidden secrets, including the scandals and intrigues of Ulthuan’s elites. This makes them unpopular with nobles, especially those from the Inner Kingdoms, and Mist Weavers are often accused of slander. Mist Weavers cover their heads with scarves and veils, both to honour their goddess and protect their identities.

Forges of Vaul

Beneath the shrines and sanctuaries of the Temple District the smith-priests of Vaul toil tirelessly, creating exquisite arms and armour with which to equip Tor Koruali’s regiments of citizen-soldiers. The most skilled devotees of the Elven smith god have the honour of crafting artefacts of immense magical power for the city’s nobles and heroes, harnessing the rarest materials found in the depths below. Such wondrous items cannot be made on any normal forge however, and so Vaul’s devotees make regular pilgrammage to the Sky Forge high up in the Annulii mountains, where the most skilled among them fashion works of great artifice for Ulthuan’s worthiest sons and daughters.

Cadai Temple

The Cadai temple is octagonal in shape with eight tall spires. Arched entrances on each side of the temple are inscribed with runes that provide powerful wards against Chaos. Inside the temple is a central ritual area where priests and mages representing all eight Cadai gods convene in times of war to perform rites of courage and resilience that bolster the city’s defenders. Votive sacrificial rituals are also performed in times of famine, plague, or drought.

Around the central ritual area are shrines to each of the eight Cadai gods. Elves who pray at this temple honour all the Cadai, and though they often favour two or three gods, they do not elevate them above the others. Private chapels are used for yenlui balancing meditation. In the temple’s sublevels are scriptoriums, an armoury, and storerooms containing dry foods and other essential supplies that might be needed in the event of a siege.

Attached Buildings

Adjoining the temple is a small monastery where a dozen priests of Asuryan live in simple quarters, little more than a small chamber with space for a bed, wardrobe, devotional fireplace, and little more. Though Cothique’s largest temple of Asuryan is in Tor Alin, the lighthouse flame-shrine at the tip of Tor Koruali’s peninsula is held sacred by the order. In addition to maintaining the flameshrine, Asuryan’s priests watch the city’s residents for Khainite influences. Most of these priests are lay shrine attendants, however, and therefore lack the advanced prognosticative abilities of their superiors.

Expected Code of Behaviour of Those Who Venerate the Cadai

  • Remain at all times attuned to the present moment. The skill of one’s actions is greatest when temporally grounded.

  • Mindfulness of one’s agency is essential to exercising choice between rationality and instinct.

  • Life is sacred. Do not kill unless another’s life will be forfeited as a consequence of your passiveness.

  • At the end of each day, offer a prayer of gratitude for the body and mind you have been gifted.

  • Maintain cleanliness of body and mind. The gods enshrined within oneself are not to be sullied with impure excretions or wicked thoughts.

  • Self-sacrifices for Ulthuan’s survival are the noblest acts of all. Follow the example of Caledor, who watches eternally over Ulthuan from the Great Vortex.

Opposite the monastery is a dormitory for over twenty novitiates of Isha. These aspiring mages help the community by performing magical cantrips to clean public areas or improve farmers’ harvests, and they sometimes serve as counsellors or chaperones at the prison. Novitiates are closely supervised by priestesses of Isha who observe their interactions with the public and ultimately evaluate their suitability for training at Tor Vael or the White Tower of Saphery.

Sub-Shrines

Pilgrims from Tor Koruali’s archipelago and the surrounding lands regularly visit the temple’s sub-shrines. Elves use these sacred chambers not only for prayer, but also to meet contacts from the Environs (page 59).

Isha Shrine

Leafy vines frame the entrance, while a wooden statue of the goddess, carved from timber taken from a lornalim tree, stands behind an altar, clasping a bowl to catch the tears that drip perpetually from her left eye. Worshippers are expected to care for the plants in the shrine, bringing fertile soil for repotting and watering them from the bowl of tears. Farmers from the Burgeon on the mainland come here to pray for bountiful growth and request the aid of horticultural magic from Isha’s novitiates.

Loec Shrine

The shadowy interior of the alcove is darkened with soot, while a ray of light illuminates one side of the marble statue of Loec. Offerings of food are left upon the altar, and various musical instruments are nearby, to be played around a central firepit. Smugglers from the artist colony south of the city often meet their customers here to arrange deliveries of contraband goods.

Vaul Shrine

A sulphuric odour lingers from incense burnt upon an altar shaped like an anvil, while magical flames burn in braziers along the walls. A statue stands bent and chained to the altar, with hammer and tongs. Precious metals are left as offerings, then taken by others, crafted into items of beauty, and returned to the altar. Smith-priests are quick to claim the golden fish scales offered by pearl divers from Tor Daroluth on the western side of the bay.

Mathlann Shrine

A seaweed curtain separates the shrine from the earthly domain, hiding walls of coral and shells. A statue of Mathlann stands bearing a trident, wearing a crown with five spikes, decorated with seashells. The altar, made of driftwood, contains luxurious offerings of perfume, coral combs, and exquisitely hand-carved wooden figurines of sea life. Ever since the oracles’ temple at the north of the bay was closed by royal decree, Sea Elf smugglers have been selling contraband sauce here instead.

Lileath Shrine

The walls are decorated with frescoes depicting the goddess riding her celestial steed Cindermane above meadows and forests, amongst the constellations. A statue of Lileath stands behind the altar, which is draped in white cloth and contains an ornate pestle and mortar amongst offerings of herbs. Seers of Lileath use special flowers picked from the Gorge of Nymphs as ingredients for their sacred rites.

Kurnous Shrine

The walls are adorned with animal furs, bones, and scales from sea monsters; trophies of the kills made to protect Cothique. The altar, surrounded by harpoons, spears, and other weapons, sits under the gaze of a statue that is part Elf and part deer. A small brazier is used by worshippers to make a fire around which they pray.

Asuryan Shrine

A timeline of Asur history adorns one wall, its opposite wall remaining blank for the time yet to pass. A marble statue of Asuryan, flanked by Phoenixes, holds a bowl containing a magical flame. On the altar are scissors, parchment, and quills for pilgrims to write prayer scrolls, which are tied with a strand of their hair. Prayer scrolls are then delivered to the lighthouse flame-shrine at the southern tip of the peninsula, where they are thrown into Asuryan’s sacred flame.

Hoeth Shrine

Prayer wheels on either side of the entrance cause visitors to produce a harmonic sound as they enter. Bookcases line the walls surrounding a lectern, and a great tome with quill stand before a statue of Hoeth. Pilgrims are expected to demonstrate cleverness by answering a riddle from the tome before writing their own. Loremasters pay at least 1 GC for Spites captured in the city’s grottos or the Starwood Groves and brought here in clay jars.

The Harbour

Tor Koruali is a well-defended port city, with its harbour securely enclosed by seawalls and small islands. An impressive fleet of warships is supported by artillery and archers from the towering cliffs. The port’s bustling ship traffic is a mix of sea traders, fishing vessels, and naval patrols. Non-Elven visitors are generally restricted to the southern harbour area until they clear inspections.

Trade and Commerce

Non-Elven merchants who pass inspection are allowed to moor their ships at the southern cliffside docks. Cargos of all kinds can be unloaded and traded at the Fish Market, a series of large, smelly caverns frequented mostly by Sea Elf traders from the Outer Kingdoms and colonies. Sea Elves are notorious for driving hard bargains and demanding bonus favours to seal transactions with non-Elves. No excise taxes or tariffs are collected in Tor Koruali’s Open District.

Defences

Characters who have never seen Elven cities before are likely to be awestruck by the sight of so many strange and beautiful ships. Albatross and golden eagle trading ships come and go constantly from the merchant port, whilst the military port harbours up to 40 hawkships, ten eagleships, and three dragonships. Half of these warships belong to Prince Dalloran, and the rest are owned by the other princes of Cothique.

The city’s harbour is protected by a mighty seawall, with five gates and watchtowers facing the main southern approach. The sea gates are usually kept open unless enemy ships have been reported in the vicinity. Cliffside battlements and arsenal towers provide a clear vantage over the harbour for archers and Eagle Claw Bolt Throwers. Armouries containing normal, flaming, and enchanted ammunition are secured by magical wards in caverns beneath the city and at the island naval base.

Inspections and Customs

Visiting Elven ships are inspected by the port authorities for Dark Elf spies, illicit drugs, and other contraband goods, whilst foreign ships are searched for Chaos followers and tainted cargos. A typical boarding party consists of a harbourmaster and four port guards. No excise taxes are collected as these inspections are strictly security measures. In the event of trouble, the harbourmaster blows a whistle to summon reinforcements, and swift hawkships respond within minutes.

Ship captains are requested to anchor at the Lighthouse Gallery docks for inspections. Crews are welcome to disembark on the lighthouse island, provided they cooperate with the port guards and do not show signs of corruption. Non-Elven merchants who pass inspection may only trade in the sea level Open District. Elves who pass inspection can proceed into the city. Foreign visitors who are not merchants are brought to the Azure Halls for customs inquiries.

Reputable Elven merchants may sell their goods at the Sea Trading House in the upper city. Small tariffs are collected by the trading house, usually amounting to only a small pouch of silver per deal. Elven nobles frequently conduct business at the trading house, but they rarely venture down to the Fish Market. Old World merchants who wish to trade with Elven nobles must arrange meetings through messengers or sales agents. If a merchant from the Old World becomes trusted by their Elven counterparts, they may be invited to palace events or the more open occasions at the hall of Sea Trade, but will have to be on their best behaviour.

Unscrupulous and daring Human merchants are willing to risk sailing through the Shifting Isles to benefit from the city’s tax exemptions. A dozen or so merchants from Miragliano and Marienburg therefore trade in Tor Koruali at any given time, as well as the occasional trader from Nordland or Ostland. According to the Treaty of Amity and Commerce, Marienburgers can only buy New World exotics from Ulthuan, and Elves can only buy Old World goods from Marienburg. However, the treaty has not dissuaded Imperial merchants from visiting Tor Koruali, nor has it prevented Cothiqui merchants from trading in Altdorf.

Ylvathoi

All Ulthuan is considered sacrosanct and most places in most kingdoms are off-limits to non-Elves. Trespassers are called ‘ylvathoi’ , which translates to ‘imperfect youth’. The term was popularised during Ulthuan’s isolationist era following the War of the Beard. In recent centuries, Phoenix Kings Bel-Hathor and Finubar the Seafarer have favoured more inclusive policies to rebuild relations with the outside world. The term ‘ylvathoi’ has therefore acquired something of a derogatory connotation, though it remains widely used by High Elves.

Harbour Locations

Naval Base

The city’s naval fleet operates from a diamond-shaped island-fortress. At either point of the diamond is an archer tower. A pair of arsenal towers faces southwest. Officers’ quarters and war rooms are inside the fortress, while Sea Guard barracks are outside the walls. Military drydocks and shipyards are in a cliffside cove opposite the island. Only serving soldiers and sailors are allowed on the naval base. Foreign visitors are unwelcome unless accompanied by an Elven sponsor.

All Cothique’s cities are represented by the capital fleet, currently under command of Princess Litharys (page 38). The capital fleet’s size fluctuates depending on the kingdom’s state of war and its readiness for anticipated threats. Whenever Dark Elves attack the Shrine of Khaine north of Ulthuan, the capital fleet anchors at Elisia or Tor Dynal, leaving only a single squadron at Tor Koruali. Princes may withdraw warships from the capital fleet if their home cities are endangered.

Thanks to powerful enchantments protecting the Shifting Isles, Cothique’s mainland has not been invaded since the Great Chaos Incursion. Norscan raiders are effectively countered by the island-cities’ home fleets. As a result, an uneasy complacency has pervaded the naval base. Sailors and soldiers spend much of their time carousing in the Open District. The officers organise mock battles, raiding expeditions, and occasionally sea monster hunts to maintain their crews’ combat readiness.

Developing Intrigue: the Mirkwine Sting

Though most Sea Elf sailors are as upstanding as their land-loving relatives, some of them drink mirkwine purchased from covert Dark Elf agents. Mirkwine has also become popular with students at the Naval Academy who acquire it from the sailors. Despite being widely consumed in the navy, mirkwine is an illicit substance. Transactions larger than a few bottles are therefore conducted close to the Seven Sisters shrine. Elven sailors from all walks of life frequent Ladrielle’s shrine between sea journeys, making it an inconspicuous meeting place. Characters encountering drunken, violent sailors might acquire a half-empty bottle of the narcotic wine for evidence or sampling (see page 50).

Fish Market

The largest harbour market is a series of dockside caverns near the southern seawall. The fish market is usually crowded and noisy. Despite daily cleaning with magical cantrips, the poorly ventilated caverns always smell of raw seafood. When Tor Koruali was a mining town, the caverns were solely for fish vendors, but in recent times, all varieties of trade goods are exchanged. Foreign merchants are allowed to trade at the fish market after passing inspection.

Goods are usually displayed on colourful blankets, and only the freshest fish is available here, kept cool throughout the year using ambient enchantments. If decaying seafood is brought to the market, Elven citizens soon raise complaints about the unsightly and malodorous fare at the market. Starting prices are set to double, with the expectation that customers will engage in a degree of dealership and haggling. Many deals are frantic, informal auctions in which services are bartered in lieu of trade goods. The limited warehousing here often forces cargos to be transferred ship-to-ship, across gangplanks. Most merchants are Sea Elves from the island houses who are excluded from the Hall of Sea Trade. Highborn citizens of Tor Koruali rarely set foot in the fish market, instead dispatching sales agents and servants to handle the work.

Imperial merchants trade at Cothique’s fish market to avoid the taxes and bureaucracy of Lothern. Marienburg’s ruling council is boycotting Cothique for treaty violations, but merchants from the city still visit to negotiate. Elven nobles are willing to pay for political news from the Old World to help formulate their influence-trading schemes. Herwig Algnar (page 57), a semi-permanent Human resident of Tor Koruali, profits from the fish market’s information trade.

Forbidden Fish

Cultists of the Cytharai goddess Anath Raema are selling meat from megalodons hunted in the Dead Shale south of Tor Koruali. Though shark meat is a staple of the Cothiqui diet, megalodons are sacred to Mathlann, and may only be eaten if they are hunted under the supervision of storm weavers. Funerary priests of Morai-Heg also pursue the hunter-cultists, because megalodons devour dead bodies after sea burial. Characters who have encountered renegade monster hunters might be asked for the identities and whereabouts of these cultists, and to lead a patrol of Scions of Mathlann to hunt them down.

Princess Litharys of Elisia

Princess Litharys of House Starshard is the ruler of the city of Elisia, which lies many miles west of Tor Koruali, close to the border with Chrace. These days she is rarely encountered in her home city, leaving its day-today management in the hands of her talented wardens. Instead, she can be encountered in Tor Koruali or upon the deck of her personal dragonship, Nightfang . A talented and experienced naval commander, Princess Litharys was appointed the role of acting commander of Cothique’s combined navy. Though several princes are well-qualified for the role, Litharys is a veteran of many battles against Norse and Dark Elf raiders off the northern coast.

Not only is Litharys a talented captain, she is a gifted seeress with an almost intuitive grasp of the prognostic abilities granted to those sensitive to Azyr. Whilst she undertook some training under a storm weaver at the Celestial Observatory to learn to properly grasp the basics of the Lore of Heavens, visions and dreams come to her whether she wills them through her command of magic or not. Her magical precognitive sense and terrifying, lucid dreams of the future can help her to anticipate enemy fleet movements, enabling daring and effective pre-emptive counterattacks.

But whilst her ability to foresee events that loom up to threaten her in the near future has saved Litharys’ life and enabled her to lead audacious actions at sea, they have also taken their toll on her psyche. Litharys has witnessed her own bloody demise on several occasions, and each time that her abilities have allowed her to cheat death, she is only afforded a period of brief respite before she begins to dream of the next time that she will be slain. As a result, she is grim and brooding, fixated on identifying threats to Cothique and planning to defeat them.

Litharys knows her command depends on Prince Dalloran’s continued patronage, but this has not prevented her blunt manner and downbeat counsel from ruffling feathers at the Council of Princes. Each Cothiqui prince is expected to commit warships for peacetime duty, but Princess Litharys claims they are not meeting that obligation, and smugglers and spies are slipping through their patrols as a result. Recently, Litharys accused that Princess Trasinaya of Tor Andar of willfully turning a blind eye to Dark Elf smugglers. Litharys also believes Cothique’s culture is in a general state of decline. She fears the navy has become a haven for pleasure cultists and suspects Seasong Academy is largely to blame.

Princess Litharys is using spies to gather information in support of her accusations. She wishes to avoid any further debate until the evidence is weighted in her favour, and is eager to get to the bottom of the matter. This may give her cause to consider working with any Characters who might assist in making her case, and she seeks the help of likely investigators and adventurers in her usual brusque manner. Litharys attends council meetings in stylish dresses but otherwise presents herself as ready and willing to confront the enemies of Cothique without a moment’s delay, armed with the Incandescent Spear and wearing a suit of the finest ithilmar armour.

Developing Intrigue: the Mirkwine Sting

Litharys suspects harmful narcotics are being trafficked at the Grey Oak by navy sailors. She wants names and activity reports on suspected pleasure cultists from the Naval Base or Academy. Characters who witness sailors dealing with Torrelain Moonrose or Dalroth and Dannor may benefit by acquiring Bribery or Intimidation leverage over these Characters, as they will not want details of their illicit activities getting back to Litharys.

Developing Intrigue: Smuggling Showdown

Agents of Litharys try to entrap smugglers by posing as black market customers. Entrapped Characters are threatened with legal action unless they will work as moles and divulge all their contacts. Litharys arranges a transaction that incriminates Princess Trasinaya and the Characters are expected to testify.

Lighthouse Gallery

The harbour’s lighthouse sits on a small, rocky island. Although the lighthouse remains functional, it has become largely redundant as Tor Koruali has grown large enough to radiate ambient light visible from two miles away on a cloudy night. When foreign ships first arrive at the city, they are required to dock here for cargo inspections, and so the Elves have repurposed it to function as a pleasant waiting facility and art gallery where visitors can learn about Elven culture, history, and craftsmanship. Elven interpreters greet visitors, offering their services for a modest fee.

At ground level, the granite tower is twelve yards in diameter and serves as a showroom for Elven craftworks. Foreign merchants can appraise samples here before arranging bulk purchases from their favourite artisans. Elves from the Qyl-Kirior cultural centre refer to these mass-produced pieces as trinkets when sold to Humans. Despite the implied condescension, the items are finely crafted and quite expensive, and Elves in Tor Koruali depend on this business.

The upper six levels of the lighthouse contain statues of heroes from the kingdom. Hanging on the wall is a renowned tapestry woven under the instruction of Torion Fireheart, a famous artist who did his best work during the reign of Aethis the Poet. It spirals upward, following the entire length of the stairwell. The tapestry depicts Ulthuan’s long history in a series of tableaux, beginning at ground level with a scene showing Asuryan and Isha nurturing the Elves during their prehistory, and culminating with the apocalypse known as the Rhana Dandra at the top level. The lighthouse’s beacon fire is only lit on foggy nights, as the sharp senses of Elf navigators do not require assistance on moonlit nights.

A Warm Welcome

Joran Silverfish is a freelance interpreter who solicits customers at the lighthouse. As a side business, he operates a scheme selling trinkets on behalf of the city’s artisans. Silver jewellery with inlaid mother of pearl and aquamarine gemstones are currently in vogue. Joran can advise friendly Characters on Ulthuan’s laws and how to apply for sponsorship at the Azure Halls. Trustworthy characters with wealthy contacts might be invited into Joran’s scheme. His agents keep 20% of all their sales profits while Joran takes 10% for himself. Foreign merchants can be sponsored by Joran to conduct any business from which he stands to profit.

Gymnasium

Situated on a cluster of harbour islets is the gymnasium where Tor Koruali’s citizen-soldiers train in marine warfare. Mock battles, aquabatics, and team relay contests are conducted daily, as Cothiqui Elves are passionate about playing and fighting in the water. The gymnasium is often considered part of the Naval Base, but it is open to all Elven citizens, not soldiers exclusively. The islands’ main building is a large boathouse with an attached barroom, where meetings and planning sessions are held. Visitors from the Old World are prohibited from using the gymnasium unless arriving aboard Elven ships. Whilst citizens who aren’t actively involved in the military are allowed to use facilities here, it is considered very poor form to disrupt any formal training or military use of the gymnasium. Those who fail to respect this convention may be sequestered in the House of Reflection until they learn a proper degree of consideration.

The central island is dotted with climbing walls, narrow walkways, and rocky obstacles. A series of suspended bridges cross to smaller islets, and floating archery targets bob about in the straits between them. Monthly tournaments attract athletic Sea Elves from the Shifting Isles, and spectators congregate to observe these spirited competitions. They watch the show from their ships, the Lighthouse Gallery, or the city above. Flying fish races through aquatic obstacle courses and daring horse races along the harbour seawall are always popular events.

Merchant adventurers often come here. They are invited by the princes they work with to train alongside their patrons’ Sea Guards, and princes may require a team of Sea Guards to join the merchant’s crew to help defend them, and ensure their loyalty. The gymnasium is also a recruitment site for merchant adventurers seeking flying fish riders as trade messengers.

The Lost City

Wandelyn Freebird is an independent sea explorer from the island of Rokhame. On her last pearl gathering expedition, one of Wandelyn’s flying fish scouts found a sunken city dating back to the preSundering era. The scout’s descriptions were barely comprehensible because he was raving mad when he finally surfaced. Wandelyn is now preparing her crew at the gymnasium for a return to the site. Characters who possess skills or experience that might make them useful members of this expedition are offered a share of the spoils if they offer to join.

House of Reflection (Aroth Na Ildra)

Located in open manicured gardens, containing relaxing pools and surrounded by an ornate fence guarded by Scions of Mathlann, the House of Reflection is a villa devoted to re-education. Non-resident citizens (including Sea Elves), lowborn citizens, and non-Elves who spurn the traditions maintaining peace in Tor Koruali are brought here for self-improvement and realignment of yenlui . Guests practise gentle exercises in the gardens, painting, poetry, and meditation, schooled by aestheticists under the watchful eye of Harmony Master Taradhir Clearwater. Non-Elves are often given menial tasks, such as cleaning and repainting the building, as the villa’s staff believe their minds are incapable of growth.

During their period of internment, guests are housed in comfortable but plain rooms designed to encourage reflection. Highborn and nobility are permitted guest rooms at Prince Dalloran’s residence, with private guidance in balancing yenlui .

Permits to leave the compound are granted following an improvement in behaviour and a successful petition, but to safeguard the citizens of Tor Koruali, guests provided with passes must also be fitted with Thauma Ataractic Gems. Re-education is expected to continue in the community, so suitable tasks are allocated. For Elves, this could be as chaperones assigned to Old Worlders to act as interpreters and teach them local customs and correct social etiquette. Princess Litharys sometimes employs such guides with an ulterior motive, to uncover information on those she suspects of being smugglers.

Non-Elves that repeatedly transgress are taken to the Azure Halls, where they remain until they leave Cothique.

The Flower Thrower

Guests of the House of Reflection are cleaning provocative graffiti across the city. Rumours suggest the artist is protesting against the capture of Spites. They mark each piece with flower petals, which has earned them the nickname ‘the Flower Thrower’. Ceyluii (page 62) wants the agitator silenced — he can tolerate a few Spites being sacrificed if it means weakening the swarms outside his control on Driftspine isle. If Characters work to unmask the artist, Ceyluii will judge their sympathy to the activist’s cause.

Temple of Mathlann

Located apart from the other gods’ shrines, Mathlann’s temple protrudes from the eastern cliff, facing the open sea. Priests and mages cast protective rituals at this sacred site to shield the city from storms and maintain the sea magicks over the Shifting Isles. The interior is a vast, open hall with an altar at its centre. When the tides are high, the rising water reclaims the lower half of the temple. When the tides ebb away, the receding waters leave the temple floor covered in long green fronds of kelp and the cast off shells of crustaceans.

Storm weavers that frequent the temple venerate megalodons and kraken as Mathlann’s favoured creatures. From a narrow stone plinth at sea level, priests can summon Mathlann’s children to aid in the city’s defence. Geomantic rituals produce powerful currents in the Shifting Isles, which deposit flotsam in a bay to the south. Drifting corpses left over from sea battles in the isles are funnelled by these currents into the Dead Shale, thus keeping its voracious megalodons well fed.

Cultists of the hunter goddess Anath Raema kill megalodons purely for the adrenaline rush. Hunter cults are outlawed by the priests of Mathlann, who offer bounties for bringing them to justice. Convicted cultists are chained to the temple’s altar at dusk, then left to drown in the midnight tides. On the rare occasion when convicts survive until the next morning, they are deemed to be pardoned by the divine will of Mathlann.

On the Hunt for Kraken

Princess Trasinaya has ordered a hunt to curb the growing kraken population in the Shifting Isles. Her island city of Tor Andar has lost four ships to kraken attacks over the past year alone. As a former merchant adventurer with monster-hunting experience, the princess will lead the expedition herself. She is presently consulting the storm weavers of Mathlann on acceptable protocols and quotas.

The Characters might be employed as expendable mercenaries on the princess’s hunt. Trasinaya often ignores sea laws when it is convenient, so the storm weavers are sending a monitor vessel to ensure that she does not endanger other craft whilst on her expedition. The princess does not appreciate this oversight, and ideally she would like it if she could set the Characters against the storm weavers in some way, so that she can be rid of them and kill kraken to her heart’s content.

Reservoir Park

Surrounded by a lush park, this freshwater pool at the aqueduct terminus supplies the entire city with potable water. Pipes sloping down the ridge supply fountains, palaces, and mansions. Drains around the pool enable water to cascade to the undercity, where it collects in overflow basins. At any given time, a dozen Elves with jugs and buckets gather at the park collecting water for their families. Comfortable benches provide seating around the pool.

The reservoir is always guarded by a patrol of four Scions of Mathlann looking out for Dark Elf saboteurs. Placing any foreign object or substance in the pool is a serious crime. Tor Koruali’s rulers are deeply concerned about the city’s reliance on the aqueduct, particularly in the case of a future siege. Most households keep extra water jugs in their cellars as a precaution, and the city’s storm weavers are experimenting with spells to render seawater drinkable. Cisterns also collect water that runs from the streets during storms to be used in fountains and to help with keeping the streets clean in dry weather.

Elves enjoy socialising before walking home with full vessels. Typical interactions involve boasting of their recent accomplishments, singing together or sharing stories, and flirting playfully. A public stone stairwell, usually wet and slippery, descends from the park into the underground Crafting District. Spites from the grottos occasionally make their way to the surface and flit amongst the trees. Some of these Spites are seeking a way back to the Starwood Groves, while others merely yearn for a bit of daylight.

Poisonous Intentions

Dark Elf agents have poisoned the reservoir over the years, prompting the Cult of Isha to post a novitiate whose duty is to test the water regularly. A Druchii spy is living in the nearby Halls of Solace disguised as an itinerant muralist named Gloriana. Gloriana’s mission is to survey Tor Koruali’s infrastructure for vulnerabilities and leave behind coded murals to instruct future saboteurs. She has been contacted by some of the Dread King’s assassins but has not yet answered their appeals for assistance.

The Statue of Caledor

Among the many works of public art found in Reservoir Park is a large statue of the phoenix king Caledor II, constructed from bone white marble and towering 50 feet in height, brandishing a spear. There are some in Tor Koruali who worry it may give a poor impression to visitors from the Old World, given Caledor’s role in the War of the Beard. However, most Elves, mindful of the need to appear supportive of their culture, history, and relations with other kingdoms of Ulthuan (including the kingdom of Caledor), do not concur. Certain intriguers may inquire as to the opinions of Old Worlders as to what they think, and it would be wise to respond with bland approval. If a person such as Cireon Fleetcrest or Prince Dannor were to overhear an outsider give vent to strong opinions on the subject, they would gossip that this individual is indelicate in their manners.

Celestial Observatory

Palace of Respite

This tall, pentagonal tower is the highest structure in Tor Koruali. It is both an early warning weather station and astronomical observatory for scrying the Winds of Magic. Storm weavers, loremasters, and seers of Lileath work to ensure the city’s defences are not compromised. They run messages back and forth between the observatory, the nearby library-temple of Hoeth, and the temple of Mathlann. Visitors who do not make polite and pertinent inquiries are quickly turned away.

On the cliff overlooking Silverwave Shipyard is a palace belonging to House Emeraldsea, one of Lothern’s preeminent merchant families. House Emeraldsea once had ties to Cothique through a marriage to Prince Arathion, the father of Tyrion and Teclis. Impressive by Tor Koruali standards (but modest compared to their Lothern palace), the Emeraldsea property has not been used by the family since the twins’ mother died. With Tyrion’s permission, the palace is now a guest estate and social club for young nobles.

Hurricanes pass frequently through the Shifting Isles, endangering ships and rousing sea monsters from the depths. Weather reports are dispatched daily to harbour officials who inform the ships’ captains. The occasional warp hurricanes that blow from the poles produce powerful surges of magical energy, and they can develop into great storms of magic that overload leyline networks or stagnate into pools of Dhar . Mages must therefore bolster their defences in advance of a warp hurricane and work to resolve the fallout afterwards.

Tyrion has occasionally enjoyed late-night festivities here. However, when visiting Cothique, he usually spends his time in Tor Alin. Mirkwine has become the drink of choice in his absence and other potent narcotics are available. The palace’s furniture is draped with skins of Lustrian jaguars, Yhetees from the Mountains of Mourn, and giant wolves from Norsca. While sensuous rituals to Atharti transpire behind closed doors, select revellers watch illegal duels fought between wine-crazed dilettantes in the palace’s sublevel.

A Human wizard, Joos de Groot from Baron Hendryk’s College in Marienburg, has resided at the observatory for two years. Joos is sponsored by Prince Erione of Tor Vael, who has proposed a controversial program to upgrade the Wasteland’s leylines. Prince Erione hopes that Joos will eventually provide insights into how leyline surges near Marienburg affect Ulthuan (and vice versa) and report his findings to Marienburg’s Directorate. Joos is learning how to use Elven astronomical instruments and finds the process challenging and stressful.

Half of the palace is a temporary residence for Princess Arianne of Anirain and her court. One day, Arianne intends to reclaim the ruined city of her birthright, but for now, she enjoys a party lifestyle while profiting from mirkwine consumed at the palace. Arianne is friends with Prince Dalloran’s sons, who are regular patrons themselves. The charismatic princess has little trouble deflecting the attentions of swordmasters and priests of Asuryan from the seedier activities that take place here.

Favour for a Friend

Philosophy Club

Over the last year, Joos de Groot has become acquainted with an interpreter named Calariel Mistwise. The Elf has accompanied Joos to public events, taught him Elthárin phrases, and suggested appropriate styles of dress. Recently, the pair sailed to Tor Dynal where Joos wished to consult their seers, however Prince Salissael turned them away without explanation. The brothers purchase bottles of mirkwine from Elven sailors at the Grey Oak. If any Characters are investigating wine smuggling, they might be bribed by Dannor (10–20 GC) to stop asking questions. Princes Dalroth and Dannor

Crown Prince Dalloran’s spoiled sons embody the worst of Tor Koruali’s young nobility, and so far, they have escaped all consequence for their wanton ways. Both princes have their father’s lean build and angular face, but neither of them inherited his moral fibre. Though their sister Adranna is better respected by Elves of their parents’ generation, the brothers cannot be easily impugned. After all, many of their critics’ own children are little better.

Dalroth is the heir apparent and a tribunal member at the Royal Court, whilst Dannor was bequeathed the family waystone quarries by their grandfather. Both princes drink too much mirkwine and spend their leisure time partying at the Palace of Respite or hunting on the family estate. Despite the many political tools at their disposal, the brothers can be goaded into fights when they are drunk, which is as often as not outside of public ceremonies and state occasions when they know better than to misbehave.

More scheming and sly than his brother, Dannor accepts bribes from smugglers in exchange for access to the Caves of Bones near his quarries. This is the primary route by which mirkwine enters Tor Koruali. When Dalroth grows frustrated with Dannor, he is known to accuse his brother of associating with Dark Elves, but Dannor is always ready with a plausible excuse.

House of Law (Taigh Na Ceyl)

The distinguished marble courts of the House of Law, situated in the Defence Level, also maintain a presence in the Open District below via the barracks of the city guard. The two are connected, with the city guard in the tunnels between them to enforce the courts’ higher position of authority.

Interpreters are provided for impeached members of other species as they are not trusted to speak Elthárin fluently in court (even if they are actually able to). Inspection protocols and taxation concerns can also be logged here, but the lengthy paperwork and consultation period are often beyond the patience of members of other species. High Judge Bel-Kalandus Pureheart finds the work tedious and often frequents the Grey Oak to escape the drudgery (page 50).

The barracks below are used as a holding area before and after processing, with members of the Scions of Mathlann used to transfer offenders to either the Azure Halls or House of Reflection. Though not as ornate as the courts, the facade of the barracks still presents itself as an important place. Commander Korralaen Goldenhelm is meticulous in every detail, running the barracks efficiently. He often comes into conflict with lawyers and judges above, whom he believes are frivolous with timings and order.

The Flying Fish (Eldoir-Iasg)

Flying Fish The bar is located on a vibrant cliff-ledge terrace, filled with entertainers and vendors selling trinkets or grilled fish and calamari, between the Fish Market and Azure Halls. A series of incidents involving tainted fish dishes, sourced from the Fish Market, have caused Naraneth to suspect a ploy to discredit the cabaret bar and Hathenaer’s expertise. The ex-swordmaster’s past is shrouded in mystery since Naraneth discovered him wounded in the Shifting Isles while on an assignment that his mind cannot recall. While in the background, agents for Princess Trasinaya of Tor Andar seek to use the scandal at the bar as leverage over Illorahin Seabrow to not pursue smugglers.

Bethranail Blackwood of the Hall of Sea Trade looks for potential smugglers while sharing a booth with Caeliseth Surehand of the Silverwave Shipyard. Rocs’ Roost

The Roc’s Roost is a tall tower rising from the walls surrounding the Palace District. At the top of the tower is a single turret tapering outwards to six sides, within each of which is a single bare room, unfurnished save for a scattering of straw and sticks. The Roost houses four swiftfeather rocs, majestic birds of prey that can be trained to pull Skycutters, small flying vessels used by the High Elves for scouting on land and sea.

The rocs are native to the Glittering Coast, which runs west from the port of Lothern to the Bay of Tears. This is to the far south of Ulthuan, where the weather is balmy, and the winds are gentle. The birds fail to thrive in Cothique, where the sun shines pale and cold gales blow. This massive cote was built for the birds in the hope that it would provide them with adequate shelter and a nest close to the ocean, where the rocs can hunt for their preferred food, small sharks and young kraken.

In 2160 IC, the Roost was built and three breeding pairs of rocs were transported here from Eataine. It was hoped that they would find the Roost conducive enough to live and breed there, but most of the birds housed at the Roost take a brief breath of the cold air of Cothique before they fly away and do not return. Sometimes the birds are found again, but only after having made a journey all the way back to their homes in the Glittering Coast.

Currently, there are two breeding pairs housed in the Roost. A third pair lived there until recently, but the male was eaten by a megalodon and its partner pined away. The remaining birds spend most of their time hunched up as if sheltering from the bitter cold, even on sunny days. Whilst there are some in Cothique’s navy who insist that the birds are necessary to pull Skycutters and patrol the Shifting Isles, many Elves in Tor Koruali feel that it would be kinder to accept that the birds will never take to Cothique’s climate and return them to Eataine.

Swiftfeather Roc

Swiftfeather Rocs are massive birds of prey whose natural habitat is the cliffsides of the Glittering Coast. As such, wild Rocs are rarely seen as far north as Tor Koruali. Whilst they are fierce birds of prey, the Rocs are not as proud and wilful as the Great Eagles, and they can be harnessed to war machines such as the High Elves’ Skycutter chariots.

Cultural Centre (Qyl-Kirior)

Nestled in the northwest corner of Tor Koruali’s Entertainment District is a borough known to locals as the Qyl-Kirior , a pre-eminent nexus of art, learning, and creation. Former ship’s cooks interpret foreign cuisines in themed eateries, young Elves dress in daring styles, and bar-side debates run throughout the night.

Visitors from the Old World are welcomed with mixed feelings here. On one hand, the younger Elves crave fresh inspiration. They interrogate visitors on foreign politics, culture, and trade opportunities. On the other hand, any haughty High Elf who wishes to regard the activities here as a threat to tradition can find evidence here to fortify their prejudices.

A young High Elf ’s fascination with people from other nations can be intoxicating, but most visitors have only a few decades of experience to draw on, and few are sharp enough to impress a High Elf for long. Once they become a bore, they are no longer courted by a rapt audience and, unless they think of something new to offer, are thoroughly ostracised.

The Far Shore Collection

This Druchii narcotic wine carries an elusive note of salt and iron. It is intended for use in Khainite rituals and provokes rhapsodic, bloodthirsty mania when imbibed. The Grey Oak (Yn Edri Ar)

At the base of the cliffs below the Royal Palace and Cothique’s naval academy is the Grey Oak, a risqué establishment that deals in specialised vice. At all hours, patrons are offered entertainment, company, and a wide range of beverages and herbal tonics.

Yn Edri Ar occupies a sizable cavern. It is dominated by the Edri Ar at its centre, a grey oak that is the place’s namesake. This etiolated tree has grown huge and is wreathed in the Grey Wind of Ulgu . Cloth lanterns dangle from its branches, Spites flit about its leaves, and dancers of Loec play among its roots.

The room is furnished with cushions, fine carpets, and seating pits. Low tables are stocked with amphorae and smoking paraphernalia and are sparsely occupied by rakish Elves. Courtesans ply guests with dreamleaf and narinocha wine, making conversation that ranges from lewd banality to sharp political discourse, depending on the desires of the guest. Throughout the course of an evening, clients are escorted up to the canopy level for further forms of recreation.

A second-storey balcony overlooks the venue, looping around the oak, and is lined with private snugs carved into the cliff. Here, suitors woo chosen courtesans in a process that can take months, if not decades, to fulfil. The criteria for final acceptance as a partner are always selective and involve evaluation during several amorous excursions, and successful suitors partake in moonlit romances worthy of song.

Developing Intrigue: the Mirkwine Sting

Agents of Morathi distribute contraband mirkwine using the Grey Oak. This euphoric, mania-inducing narcotic corrupts both visiting foreigners and students from the nearby Seasong Academy. Princess Litharys surmises (incorrectly) that Torrelain Moonrose is the source. She seeks evidence to justify a fullscale raid and is employing agents to investigate on her behalf. Torrelain seeks accomplices, hoping to undermine the mirkwine trade without compromising her reputation. She knows Princess Arianne distributes the wine, and that she is protected by Princes Dannor and Dalroth, mirkwine enthusiasts who are secretly Arianne’s suppliers.

Torrelain Moonrose

Torrelain Moonrose’s name is a pseudonym, combining the names of two flowers native to the Shifting Isles. She favours simple cosmetics, wears loose robes decorated with silver scales, and stylish jewellery. When Torrelain talks, she does so in a warm, relaxed manner, and habitually smokes a blend of herbs from a lengthy, flower-engraved pipe. She is easily amused, keenly analytical, and has an aptitude for spells employing Ulgu .

Torrelain is the proprietor of Yn Edri Ar and is occasionally embroiled in scandals. Her activities have seen her asked to present herself at the Palace of Respite on several occasions, where she has made high society connections, notably Prince Dalroth, who uses her as his main supplier of narcotics.

Torrelain is an adherent of Loec and believes her work sacred. Intoxicated Elves often divulge more than they should, and her serving staff inform her of any interesting secrets their clients let slip. Torrelain has a reputation for discretion, thanks to carefully confirming her suspicions before passing them on. Torrelain enjoys using Old Worlders in her intrigues as they have plausible deniability by virtue of ignorance, and any knowledge of her involvement goes with them when they leave Ulthuan.

Seasong Naval Academy

Seasong Naval Academy is Cothique’s finest institution of military study, and thought by many to be the finest in all the world. It has educated Cothique’s most brilliant naval theorists, including Princess Litharys of Elisia, Prince Dalloran himself, and Rythian Seasong, the famed captain for whom the Academy was renamed. The curriculum emphasises practical instruction, and students can often be seen receiving hands-on education at Tor Koruali’s ports and shipyards.

Students attend from every Ulthuani kingdom, though the majority are from Cothique. Generally, Elves attend instruction prior to returning home for their initial bout of militia service. Cothique’s natives sometimes continue attending part-time while enlisted to pursue graduatelevel courses. Many instructors teach only one season a year, spending the rest of their time in high-ranking positions amidst Ulthuan’s fleets.

The Academy allows non-Elves to observe classes occasionally, so long as they obtain a staff recommendation. These are more frequently bartered to pompous knownothings than granted to the deserving, and foreign observers rarely have strong enough Elthárin to comprehend instruction. Most stop attending classes once embarrassed in practical instruction. The Academy has developed a problem with violent initiation ceremonies, and physical harassment is seldom lost in translation.

This problem centres around the Aesmenlui, a fraternal order of sailors to which Princes Dannor and Dalroth belong. Membership in this social club has long been a mark of pride for Cothique’s gentlemen, but recent disciplinary problems among newer, less influential members of the brotherhood suggest something rotten in leadership.

A Dangerous Proposal

Johan Rothemurr (of Marienburg’s House Rothemurr) wishes to be a true peer to Seasong’s Elves and has made a dangerous deal. Prince Dannor will make him the first non-Elven Aesmenlui if he can cliff-dive a pearl from Whitereach Bay. This challenge was posed in mocking jest, but Johan swore to perform the feat. Johan is a gifted diver with a strong chance of success but wants the Characters to act as witnesses and possible rescuers: Johan rightly fears monsters and foul play alike.

The Azure Halls (Ima’llian Ulathir)

Embedded within the cliffs by Tor Koruali’s busiest wharves is Ima’llian Ulathir , the ‘Place of Flotsam’. NonElven visitors call it the Azure Halls, from the beautiful semi-translucent tiles on the ceiling, through which a soft blue light illuminates the subterranean chambers.

Three significant halls surround a small courtyard, each assigned to the receipt of travellers arriving in Tor Koruali and, by extension, the kingdom of Cothique. The Starlight Hall is a meeting lodge for Cothiqui pilots, those who guide ships through the Shifting Isles. The Repose Hall is a temporary hostel for non-Elven visitors to the city, who must reside here until they are given leave to spend time on the mainland of Ulthuan.

The main chamber is the Hall of the High Receiver, a position responsible for vetting non-Elves who come to Tor Koruali and ensuring they have a noble or highborn sponsor to vouch for their behaviour while in Ulthuan. On arrival, the pilot who brought them into the city must deliver them to the Hall of the High Receiver, where their presence is acknowledged. They are then sent to the Hall of Respite, to wait until Archmage Fialthra is ready to receive them, hear a deposition from their sponsor, or find an Elf ready to vouch for them.

Finding a sponsor can take time and the High Receiver will not be hurried. Parties awaiting an interview must stay in the Repose Hall, guarded by Scions of Mathlann. They are free to fraternise with other travellers. Conditions in the Repose Hall are rough by Elf standards, but considerably better than almost any Human hostelry. There is no charge for staying, but once a sponsor is found (for travellers are forbidden to stay in Cothique by themselves), they must depart. Officially, the sponsor must send an agent to accompany the travellers at all times, but this rule is not always respected by the Elves of Cothique.

High Elves sometimes visit the Azure Halls to amuse themselves with the antics of non-Elves visiting the city. On occasion, they may decide to sponsor a party who take their fancy, rather like adopting an amusing child for a short time. Whilst the three halls are built beneath the ground, there is a fortified mansion on the surface, within walking distance of the Palace District. It is furnished with the comforts and luxury that a High Elf noble would find suitable as a dignified place within which to meet new people and welcome them to the city.

Galineth Amphitheatre

The Galineth Amphitheatre is an open-air auditorium, close to the northern gate. Within tall, galleried walls stand dozens of stone benches sloping down the interior of a natural bowl-shaped depression. At street level, an arcade of marble arches provides open entrances to the auditorium. The whole structure is contained within a large canvas tent, whose many awnings keep the wind and rain off participants even in stormy weather. Atop the main portico is a striking relief depicting figures from all levels of Tor Koruali society — all may enter the amphitheatre for entertainment and enlightenment.

Between sunrise and sunset, the amphitheatre is an agora for political debates, philosophical discussion, and declarations of news from the city and elsewhere in Ulthuan. Robed speakers stand and declaim from the arena to the assembled audience, while citizens listen, talk with one another, or simply rest. Conduct is polite and mannered — there is no heckling or rowdiness, although speakers are often passionate. Topics are highminded and erudite, proof that High Elven society prizes intelligence and knowledge far more than Humans.

King Finubar in Residence

The phoenix king is aware that his presence, together with all the courtiers, mages, and members of the phoenix guard who accompany him, does much to disrupt the affairs of cities travels to. As such, he is circumspect about spending time in residence in Tor Koruali, but has such affection for Cothique that he rarely lets a decade pass without making a fleeting visit.

GMs may consider that the presence of the phoenix king permits some breathing space. High Elves are on their best behaviour during the royal visit, so it may give them a break from the schemes of Cireon Fleetcrest, or wanton wiles of Dannor and Dalroth. Of course, members of Finubar’s court have their own intrigues and eccentricities to beware of.

At sunset, the amphitheatre is given over to performance, hosted by Custodian of Ceremonies, Maeron Starvault.

During the Day, One Might See:

  • Priathra the Sagacious, a philosopher who expounds on the nature of the self.

  • Rhithirel Broadlight, a herald from the Royal Palace, who shares news from the Princes’ Council and matters from elsewhere in the kingdom.

  • Elanta and Surien, scholars who often debate one another in dialogue to advocate their position regarding Elven relations with other species. Elanta believes non-Elves are a corrupting influence, while Surien advocates an alliance with all who denounce the Ruinous Powers.

  • Brelamith of Tralinia, a melancholic thinker from Yvresse, who believes that only in stasis can the Elven mind know peace.

  • Herwig Algnar, a Human merchant invited to talk of his experiences on Ulthuan for an Elven audience. He does not realise they find his observations amusingly quaint, though many Elves believe they can learn something from a primitive perspective on their culture.

By Night, the Audience May See:

  • The Elisian Masque, a troupe who specialise in performing tragedies accompanied by slow, desolate choral singing. They perform exclusively in masks, adding many complex layers of allegory to their interpretation of classical pieces. Their interpretation of Dethelion’s The Forest of Midnight is said to be the best in 200 years.

  • The Leafbyre Company, an acting troupe who perform energetic, comedic plays of their own devising. The setting is typically pastoral, though the subject is thinly-veiled satire of Cothiqui politics.

  • House Alerac, a troupe of Sea Elves who perform traditional folk dances alongside complex, acrobatic dances inspired by seaborne life.

  • Gaion of Tor Vael, a poet, preoccupied with themes of restraint and unfulfilled destiny. Some say he is an enthusiastic member of a cult of excess.

  • Athrien, Lillamis, and Pellania, three duellists from Rokhame who specialise in bouts of incredible athleticism.

  • The Helicai, a trio of singers whose radical interpretation of Tazelle’s creation operas divides audiences.

High Receiver Fialthra Swiftprow

Archmage Fialthra is an official appointed to interview travellers on their arrival and to judge whether they should be permitted to stay in Ulthuan. After becoming a high mage, she pursued an interest in visitors from the Old World and became High Receiver. In the intervening 164 years, she has met many thousands of Elves and nonElves who come through the Azure Halls. The former pass through with little scrutiny, while the latter have arrived in greater numbers in the last 50 years.

Fialthra is unusually tall, with a long, expressive face and a serious countenance belying her general good humour. She wears her hair swept back from her face to keep it out of the way. Her robes of office are appropriately grandiose, with deep blue embroidery tracework around the collar and vambraces of her pale turquoise vestments.

After decades of service as High Receiver, she remains fascinated by Humans. Fialthra is deeply patronising to those she appraises, but she has an affection for their diversity and peculiar practices. Discerning citizens mutter that too many Humans get the benefit of the doubt, which may be true — Fialthra has been drawn into Lord Tathar’s faction that favours greater acceptance of the younger species.

The High Receiver spends much of her time presiding in the Azure Halls, receiving petitioners, meeting with Elven sponsors, or drafting and dispatching missives to the Council of Princes. Her duties extend into the city, as she and her lieutenants share responsibility for monitoring and reporting on problems created by the presence of non-Elves in Tor Koruali. The agents of the High Receiver operate rather like investigators, travelling wherever in the city they are needed.

Developing Intrigue: Prince Dalloran’s Allegiance

The High Receiver refers non-Elven characters with compelling stories and fair cases for visiting Tor Koruali to Prince Dalloran, so he can grant them permission to wander freely in Cothique. Merchants seeking Dalloran’s patronage for trade must first run an errand for Fialthra by delivering news from the Old World to Caeliseth Surehand at the Silverwave Shipyard. Caeliseth knows to gently test their manners and patience before reporting back to Fialthra. Cireon Fleetcrest (page 66) is aware of this arrangement, and keeps a couple of Elven dockers on the books to complicate matters by instigating a fight.

The Chamber of Accords

In the echoing stone forum of the Chamber of Accords, the heads of High Elf merchant houses deliberate and negotiate on matters of trade. Unlike the petty bickering and squabbling of Human merchants, the High Elves are usually reasonable enough to settle disputes amicably and with impeccable manners, so the oversight of guilds and input of kommissions is not necessary. The merchant houses are usually able to regulate their own business practices adequately.

The Chamber of Accords is a great alabaster tower atop the cliffs, with a commanding view of the horizon to the west. Several similar assemblies were established in 2140 IC by Bel-Hathor on the advice of Finubar, as a means to draw together the kingdoms before opening fully to trade with the Old World nations.

Ethics and Commerce

The Fellowship of Horinal

Amongst the merchant houses of Ulthuan, devious minds wield trade as the perfect tool to manipulate other species and extend Asur control across the world. They aim to undermine the influence of other kingdoms and slowly exert greater control over Human nations. They plot to monopolise trade routes between Lustria, the east, and the Old World, and are especially active amongst the trading clans of Marienburg, Al-Haikk and FuChow. Lord Forellian of Marienburg’s House Ulliogtha leads the Fellowship’s intrigues in the northern Old World. The leading delegate in the Chamber is Khalion Whitespear of Cairn Thel in Yvresse.

Light of Hoeth

The Chamber in Tor Koruali hosts delegates from Eataine, Cothique, Yvresse, Tiranoc, Chrace, and Sea Elves from Sith Rionnasc’namishathir (Marienburg) and Fu-Chow. These individuals represent the prominent merchant houses from each kingdom. Also in attendance are Prince Quellion, an envoy from the phoenix king, and a chosen representative of Tor Koruali’s Council of Princes — proof that trade is held in high esteem by Highborn in Eataine and Cothique.

The assembly meets in a rectangular forum surrounded by low marble benches. The floor is a mosaic depicting High Elf ships in all corners of the Known World. Most days there are discussions in the forum. They tend to be formalised and tranquil, with strict rules against the display of undue passion. Trade rivalries rarely surface at the chamber. Rather, they play out in the corridors and private offices or in distant ports away from the eyes of other Asur.

The main subject of disagreement in the Chamber is an ongoing dispute between two informal mercantile factions — the Light of Hoeth and the Fellowship of Horinal. Foremost amongst the Light of Hoeth’s number in the Chamber is Merella Greensward of Lothern. The Fellowship of Horinal push to dominate other species by controlling the movement of goods across the oceans. Their leading delegate to the Chamber is Khalion Whitespear of Cairn Thel in Yvresse.

A circle of enlightened merchant houses led by House Greensward of Lothern, the Light of Hoeth collude to use trade as a means to enrich and enlighten the lesser species and improve diplomacy with the Old World. For them, trade is a benevolent force to enhance inter-species relationships and secure mutually-beneficial alliances against the dark forces. In the Chamber of Accords, their foremost spokesperson is Merella Greensward of Lothern. Unfortunately, Druchii agents infiltrated the circle several decades ago. Now they twist the Light of Hoeth’s agenda to sow distrust and resentment amongst the Humans they patronise.

Developing Intrigue: Leyline Dispute

Any party with a ship and trade goods can profit from this social arena. The Light of Hoeth is transporting waystones to Marienburg to upgrade the Wasteland’s leylines, some of which cross Imperial soil. To circumvent the Treaty of Amity and Commerce, Prince Erione of Tor Vael (page 24) requires Imperial merchants to ‘purchase’ waystones — strictly a paper transaction — for which he offers 30–70 GC. Meanwhile, the Fellowship of Horinal offers slightly larger bribes (40–80 GC) to block Erione’s waystone deals. Prince Salissael of Tor Dynal (page 23) fears a major leyline junction in Marienburg will compromise Cothique’s security. Sales agents of both princes open with low bids then escalate their counteroffers. Herwig Algnar (page 57) can advise characters on the nuances of Elven intrigue.

Herwig Algnar

Algnar is a citizen of Altdorf, though it has been many years since he saw his home. For decades he plied a trade as a merchant, sailing from port to port around the Old World before making his way to Lothern, where he made a home in a harbourside area where many traders from the Old World make their homes. Owing to his incessant questions about the Asur, Lord Harathon Emeraldsea took Algnar under his wing and decided to show him Ulthuan and its marvels. For 15 years, Algnar toured the island with Harathon. He even witnessed High Elf warhosts in battle with Norse and Dark Elf invaders — a sight which made quite an impression on the Human.

Algnar never lost his desire for profit. He set down his observations of Elven splendour in A Man Amongst the Elves , which was printed in Marienburg and Altdorf. It has become an essential text for Imperial citizens who wish to learn more of the Asur.

Now in his late 50s, Algnar travels between Lothern and Tor Koruali, overseeing relations between Human merchants and his contacts in House Emeraldsea. His understanding of Ulthuan and its power make him a reliable go-between. The Elves of Cothique recognise him as eccentric, even by Human standards, though they find his appreciation of Elven culture endearing.

Shipyard of House Silverwave (Ithilytha Hadrinar’avalu)

A committee of elders of the Silverwave merchant house run the oldest shipyard in Cothique, said to have been founded in the time of Aenarion, which offers innovative designs and services. Their designs are perennial favourites in the Twilight Regatta ship race, and their competitors in both sport and business often attempt to discredit them, disparaging their origins or even going so low as to sabotage their ships. To safeguard their secrets, Elves from other houses are only allowed to work here if they are greatly talented and work under strict scrutiny. Currently, the reclusive Elaria Wavekeel designs the most advanced vessels. Agents of the house keep an eye on her as she is flighty and whimsical, disappearing for days to analyse how dolphins, sharks, and giant flying fish move through the water.

The shipyard is a hive of activity with ships at various stages of construction, modification, and breaking. The intricate art of Elven joinery, which eliminates the need for nails or glue, is taught by masters to apprentices. Once, the house did a great deal of trade with Chrace, but their relationship has soured ever since they were sold a shipment of wood from the vicinity of the Burned Glades that burst into flames as soon as a saw or hammer was taken to it.

Now timber arrives as bespoke shaped wood from the treesingers of Avelorn, along with hidden Spites. Seen as lucky by the workers, their number has steadily increased, leading to an infestation that has spread to the tunnels of Tor Koruali. To avoid falling out of favour with Prince Dalloran, the elders have commissioned groups to capture Spites in the city. Reclaimed wood is also used to reduce costs and limit deforestation, and reclaimed wood from military ships is highly prized for its durability.

Merchant adventurers are often seen on site bartering salvaged wood or transferring goods to storage warehouses they rent. These are often inspected by customs officials from Prince Dalloran’s treasury, ensuring all merchandise is correctly logged. Storage requests must be approved by Caeliseth Surehand, who represents the committee of elders. Anyone seeking to book ships in for repair does not need approval, but may be refused by Caeliseth without explanation.

Dusk to Dawn

Every year, teams from competing shipyards hold a prestigious race called the Twilight Regatta, spanning the treacherous seas between the Citadel of Dusk and Fortress of Dawn (see Lustria , pages 77 and 86). This year the competition is fiercer than ever, with Clan Ithilytha’s falconship, the Kestrel , piloted by renowned Haelion Swifthelm, favoured to win. Working in the shadows, Cireon Fleetcrest (page 66) seeks to secure a win for the teams from Yvresse or Chrace by any means, involving Characters if he can, in his goal for an Outer Kingdom Alliance.

Tor Koruali Environs

Shrine of Asuryan

This flame-shrine to Asuryan doubles as a functional lighthouse guiding the Asur through the Shifting Isles from where it sits at the southern point of Cothique’s peninsula. It is tended by adherent devotees, who also maintain a small monastery beside the Cadai temple in Tor Koruali. The flame of the shrine burns at all hours and the very sight of the flame is rumoured to cause physical pain in the corrupted.

The shrine is a centre of religious study, and also a nexus of informational warfare in the battle against Khainite corruption. Adherents of Asuryan meditate on complex intrigues playing out in Tor Koruali, but infrequently take action based on their gleaned insights. Petitioners sometimes beseech guidance from the shrine’s priests on matters of intrigue, but those without pure motives are always denied, which makes refusal from the shrine a condemnation. Some try to ask their questions by proxy, but such trickery can backfire spectacularly.

Driftspine Isle

This forested island is shielded from the elements by wind-breaking hills and towering piles of bleached driftwood. Driftspine’s resident Spites gather in skydarkening swarms to reinforce these barriers with washed-up wreckage, while its Dryads coax thick-vined roses to grow in reinforcing tangles. Some smugglers make use of Driftspine’s proximity to Tor Koruali, but must protect their packages from the nature spirits with subterfuge, bribery, or magical wards.

Lianllach Palace

A coastal manor formerly belonging to Prince Lianllach, House Lianllach’s recently deceased ex-lord. His notoriously suspicious death took place shortly after a successful vote to remove him, and the resulting legal situation saw the property returned to the trust of his house. Now the heads of House Lianllach throw networking galas for foreign traders, artisans, and merchants, often hosting luminaries from Marienburg. House Llianlach’s willingness to tolerate mysterious visitors who promise to help with their profit-making ventures has made the palace’s subterranean halls an epicentre of Druchii intrigue. Even so, it has a reputation as a safe place, and royal hunting parties disembark here after crossing the bay from Tor Koruali.

Parks and Green Spaces

Harmony with nature is a central concern of High Elf architecture. This provides a unique challenge in Cothique. There, life is so harsh that residents have been driven into the cliffsides, and the natural features of the environment are considered among the least appealing on Ulthuan. The residents of Tor Koruali make up for this by cultivating gardens and parks behind the great stone walls of their city.

Tor Koruali is considered a testament to the aesthetic spirit of Cothique as a whole, and it is home to the best of these. Greenery is sewn into the white stone of the city using a multitude of horticultural techniques that back a sophisticated understanding of plant growth with useful enchantments. Path-lining gardens are dug for sea mosses and wildflowers, tropical plants line glass houses that double as receiving sunrooms, and trees from all over Ulthuan can be found in stands that emulate forest conditions in miniature.

Leeward Landing

On the west face of Tor Koruali is the Leeward Landing, a sunset-facing plaza that, as its name suggests, is shielded from the winds of the ocean. The Landing is at the far end of the city’s Colonnade and connects a downward ramp from Tor Koruali’s surface level with a cavernous entrance to its largely subterranean crafting district. The Landing is known as a gathering place for players of dice and card games, and its overlook attracts fishermen interested in the challenge posed by its distance from the water.

The plaza’s centre is a park of flowerbeds and flowering trees donated by Finubar himself. These bloom during the annual war games between Lothern and Cothique, and the landing affords a view of where the games are conducted only surpassed by that from the nearby Palace of Respite. While the plaza grows especially crowded during this event, it is always a popular destination, especially on rare warm and sunny days.

This fact irritates the Spites, who find the noise particularly bothersome, and cannot understand why boats are so exciting. They take these frustrations out on passersby, stealing small belongings from anyone they find obnoxious and pitching them into the sea.

Fated Place Gardens

This connected series of sizable glass houses on the edge of Cothique’s harbour contains multiple full-scale terrariums. Samples of trees, shrubs, soil, and animals are taken from different locations throughout the world and cultivated to resemble impossibly beautiful patches of nature from their point of origin. As the name suggests, most of the exhibits are taken from the Old World, known to the Asur as the Fated Place — Elthin Arvan .

Structures are made in local styles (though to luxurious standards), and from regional materials. Each artificial environment showcases its most economically important natural resources, from live Averland steer to veins of Grand Cathayan jade that line artificial waterfalls. Seating areas throughout the garden occasionally host small-batch tastings of regional produce such as teas, meads, and wines, judging Asur-made emulations against famed regional samples. Clan Tallaindeloth, the garden’s sponsors, avoid serving their very best; they feel it better for business meetings held at such events if foreign fare compares favorably to its imitations.

Hot Tea, Hot Blood

A pop-up teahouse in the Grand Cathayan exhibition has become a madhouse. A delegation from Weijin is locked in frantic debate with their trading partners from Clan Tallaindeloth. Each party fervidly accuses the other’s tea of being sickeningly strong or even poisoned. Erdannael, head botanist at the gardens, begs the Characters to keep the matter from growing violent while he fetches help. In fact, both teas were poisoned: Spites residing in the garden have discovered that the red leaves of an oak in the Estalian exhibit are a potent stimulant, similar to Ranald’s Delight ( WFRP , page 307)..

Parks, Green Spaces, and Spites

Tor Koruali’s green spaces are so lovingly tended, magically healthy, and filled with life that they have begun to attract Cothique’s long-suffering Spites. While this is an endorsement of Tor Koruali’s beauty to be sure, the presence of the creatures in the city is a recent development that leads to some consternation. The presence of Spites in Cothique, however, predates any of its gardens.

Spites have an affinity for magical forests. Ulthuan is full of these aethyr-suffused environments, but they grow thickest along the Worldroots, a network of aethyric pathways that connects the world to Yn Edri Eternos , the Oak of Ages in Athel Loren. All the roots crossing beneath Ulthuan pass through Cothique. In ancient times, Cothique was covered in lush, magical forests, but the kingdom’s troubled history has ravaged its landscape, lashing it windswept and barren.

Because so many citizens of Cothique spend their lives on the ocean, and because the land on the coastline tends to have sparse vegetation at best, many Elves of Cothique grow up unfamiliar with Spites save for rare, hostile interactions and local superstitions. The creatures are a known pest in the green areas around Tor Koruali, making them slightly better understood by Asur who spend time in the capital.

The spirits steal small items, unbraid hair (or braid unbraided hair), and slide thorny flowers into underclothes. Spites infrequently collude to pull off spectacular (and dangerous) pranks on those whom they particularly dislike: spooking horses, slipping needles into food, and catching passersby with bundles of fishing hooks. Spites responsible for such dangerous behaviour are often targeted by mages for ritual purposes.

The creatures are aware of the superstitions that surround their true nature, and take great pleasure in further confusing matters. As might be expected, many of the rumours they encourage prove advantageous to Spites, make a fool of the Elf who believes them, or both.

Superstitions Include:

  • That Spites can be tamed with gifts of butter and wine.

  • That Spites can be banished with powdered silver.

  • That Spites are repelled by the rain.

  • That Spites are sacred to Kurnous and Isha.

  • That Spites curse those who injure them.

  • That Spites can only travel a short distance from a particular tree.

Although few sailors realise it, the last superstition has some truth to it. Local superstitions of ship spirits refer to Spites, which sometimes grow so attached to a starwood tree that they accompany it to sea after it has been shaped into an eagleship. These Spites are protective of their vessels and may subtly benefit their crews.

Alin Loren and the Starwood Grove

Since Ulthuan’s earliest days, its finest naval vessels have been fashioned from starwood — a magical lumber hewn from tremendously large trees anathema to all things unclean. Naturally, Cothique’s formidable navy has much need for starwood. Starwood trees only flourish in places rich with magic, which makes them a rare yet unmistakable sight amidst Cothique’s woodlands: most forests can only support one of the towering trees.

Necessarily, forests containing a starwood tree are magical places. These woods are lush by the standards of Cothique, thick with game animals, and littered with flickering Spites. Cothique’s loremasters hunt Spites in such forests for use in rituals. Although catching Spites is possible in Tor Koruali’s green spaces, the practice is viewed by many as cruel or unlucky. Rather than acquire such a reputation, many prefer hunting afield. This is not without danger: vengeful Dryads resent the use of Spites in this way more than any Asur.

Most of Cothique’s starwood is tended by House Spireleaf, an Isha-blessed lineage loyal to Prince Dalloran. Spireleaf ’s arborists oversee every forest in Dalloran’s dominion, most importantly tending starwood from seed to shipyard. When mature, they are uprooted via a specialized ritual, then transported to Silverwave Shipyard in a logistically complex affair requiring dozens of Elves. Spireleaf develops innovative forestry techniques, like starwood splices that allow ten-foot sprigs to grow from olive trees like giant candelabra. The narrow timber these splices produce is well suited to making starwood arrows and staves.

Developing Intrigue: Withering Starwood

Aerescael

Aerescael manipulates the situation to his benefit. Resolving matters would benefit his reputation, and in eliminating Ceyluii, he hopes to justify uprooting his home, the oldest and finest starwood in Cothique.

Although House Spireleaf has small camps throughout Tor Koruali’s sphere of influence to better care for nearby starwood, their most permanent holdings are a series of luxurious tents nestled between Spireleaf ’s most important responsibilities as forest-tenders, Cothique’s Starwood Grove, and the personal hunting grounds of Prince Dalloran, which run between Tor Koruali and the borders of the territory around Tor Vael.

Prince Dalloran’s Hunting Grounds

This sizable stretch of forest is among the healthiest in Cothique, and has long been the personal hunting grounds of the crown prince of Tor Koruali. Dalloran permits many of his friends and allies to hunt in his woods, and House Spireleaf has grown wealthy by tending to the needs of these noble guests. Such hunting trips have caught on among a hard-drinking sect of Tor Koruali’s young nobles, which has influenced how House Spireleaf entertains visitors.

The hunting grounds occasionally attract griffons that have descended from the Annulii Mountains, drawn to the plump game animals that roam here. Sometimes, Elven hunters find themselves becoming the hunted. Over the last year, three braggadocious princelings have fallen prey to hungry griffons.

The Starwood Grove

The starwood saplings wither en masse. House Spireleaf’s Aerescael blames this on the Spite Ceyluii, who regularly visits their grove. Ceyluii takes offence at this accusation and snubs the court, though he seeks aid in his own investigation. The situation is growing tense: though Ceyluii is a diplomat, some call for interrogation. Dalloran seeks foreigners to investigate and make peace, since Ceyluii is at odds with the Asur. In truth, Aerescael is to blame — he has learned a language understood by the trees of the grove, and whispers terrible truths to them. He enlists numerous members of his house to testify implicating Ceyluii, wishing the Spite undone. Ceyluii is taking revenge for raucous behaviour in the forest nearby.

A stand of starwood trees towers above the surrounding forest, standing straight, tall, and still. Even the storm winds that batter the kingdom cannot penetrate far into this forest. Wide glades filled with flowers stretch between the colossal trunks of the starwood, for even the saplings need space to grow away from their neighbours. Being in the grove calms most living things, and Cool Tests made here receive a bonus of +2 SL. Creatures of Chaos are negatively affected and must instead pass a Challenging (+0) Cool Test to enter the grove. Those who fail gain a Fatigued Condition.

Ceyluii — Spite Patron or Nemesis

Ceyluii is an ancient Spite who recalls the days before the first phoenix king. He does so fondly, as the wizened creature has acquired a distaste for High Elves over the millennia. He is wickedly clever, takes pleasure in inconveniencing, embarrassing, and injuring well-to-do Asur, and, as the most venerable, powerful, and fearsome Spite in Cothique, easily coerces lesser Spites to aid him in his scheming.

Bitter Ceyluii is a Shrike: a sort of Spite so named for its propensity to impale living things. He is naturally aggressive, but hones this inclination into an unpleasant cunning. When Finubar’s reign began, and Tor Koruali first grew lush with gardens, Ceyluii made himself known to Cothique’s court, voicing foul-mouthed complaints about the waning of Alin Loren’s starwood grove. Much to his surprise, many of his demands were taken seriously, and he was made an official representative of the forest spirits thereafter.

Although he is loath to admit it, Ceyluii has become more like an Elf over the century he has spent in their court. His poor composure leads most Asur to underestimate him, a fact which he uses to his advantage: Ceyluii surreptitiously gathers information in Tor Koruali using the Spites who work at his beck and call. He has developed a keen understanding of the matters of Elven court, eavesdrops to predict future events based on hearsay, and conducts a guerilla war of intrigue amidst the Asur that is infrequently punctuated by sudden violence.

He instructs his minions to cause mischief for any Asur they particularly dislike, both for the humour of the matter and to disguise the methodical intent of his meddling. On rare occasions, Ceyluii takes things a step further, using skills honed by mischief to arrange lethal accidents for anyone he thinks must be removed. These pranks often involve agents and patsies; it is imperative Spites not be seen as potential assassins.

One of Ceyluii’s favorite pranks is framing Elves for the use of Dark Magic, a task he accomplishes with his own talent for unpleasant witchcraft, a skill that is scarcely manifested among Spites, but that that he has secretly been practicing for millennia.

Tests made to detect or identify spells cast by Ceyluii are always Very Hard (–30), but his curses are always immediately broken on contact with starwood. This is because Ceyluii’s true nature is born of starwood, and he makes home in an ancient stump at the centre of one of Cothique’s starwood groves.

When he tires of appearing in humanoid form, Ceyluii may also appear as a wizened wharf rat or a green seagull.

Motivations

Ceyluii wishes to restore the Burned Glades to life once more. Although he is loath to trust the Asur with any matter of import, he has grown cautiously optimistic about progress toward restoration made over the last two hundred years. The delegation of mages managing the project originates from Tor Dynal but is privately funded by Prince Interik of Anirain. Even though Interik’s interest in environmentalist works is a political gambit, the Spite admires how he survives in Cothique’s politics from such a lopsided position.

He has developed a soft spot for Interik and works to subtly aid his efforts — something Interik has noticed but chooses to ignore. Ceyluii accomplishes this not only through sabotaging the prince’s enemies, but also by having his Naiads recover pre-Sundering artifacts from the waters of his ancestral home. Although he makes the prince work for these trinkets, Ceyluii seeks them on Interik’s behalf.

Although he expects it will never occur, Ceyluii wishes to stop the harvest of starwood trees in Alin Loren. The dwindling forest breaks his heart, and he wants little more than to see it recover. Ceyluii used to interfere with the uprooting ceremonies that extract starwood trees for transport, but now he meddles with the construction of new vessels instead, knowing that delays at the shipyard mean more time for the trees to grow.

Ceyluii is vengeful and uses his minions to punish those he dislikes with humiliating mischief. In extreme cases, he attempts to arrange the creative deaths of the forest’s enemies. It is critical that it not be obvious a Spite was involved in such matters: typically, he achieves this by engineering clever accidents, setting up Human patsies, or both. At present, his wrath is concentrated on a swarm of Spites on Driftspine Isle that will not bow to his will. He has caused incidents in the seas nearby for years, and intrigues to see them eradicated.

Ceyluii as Patron

The Spite has amassed many stolen trinkets that he is happy to trade, though he pretends that each item lost is a grave burden. He will part with Elven bangles and pins in exchange for aid with its schemes, some of which may carry small enchantments. Ceyluii is a peerless guide to Cothique’s wilderness, and is also willing to aid in the intrigues of his allies (albeit with complaint), hexing their foes or engineering convenient problems at critical times.

Ceyluii enjoys enlisting Humans in his pranks and schemes, whether they are witting participants or (more usually) unaware of his trickery. They tend to be easy to bribe, coerce, or deceive into cooperating with simple, humorous tasks, which often challenge their competence and skill at avoiding consequences. Promising candidates are marked as possible accomplices, who he will attempt to enlist in the frustration of any Elf he believes may be doing wrong. Ceyluii excels at creating distractions and will enlist characters to plant incriminating items or steal valuable documents with his aid.

Ceyluii as Nemesis

Ceyluii uses his command of witchcraft to cause misfortune for his foes at inopportune moments, smuggles contraband such as blackpowder weapons into their belongings if they are ylvathoi , and enlists lesser Spites to muddle their hair. He is adept at complicating social situations, and will make victims miserable until they placate his wrath.

Monuments

Cothique, like other kingdoms, is full of monuments, from natural wonders to statues created with stoneshaping magic. Some stand alone, prominent in the landscape, others are built into and from the land. Locations of natural beauty are believed to have once been visited by a god, while some monuments, often created in the image of a god or past hero, are created by Elves seeking prestige. Origins of the largest are lost and the preeminent are wrapped with powerful spells forming nexuses of power (see Winds of Magic , page 197), part of a network that keeps Ulthuan stable and above the waves.

The Seven Sisters

The origin of this ancient shrine to Ladrielle, Lady of Mists, is shrouded in mystery. The weathered faces of the seven statues carved into the cliff are badly eroded, making identification impossible, leading to many contentious hypotheses over who they represent. Down the cliff face, wreathed in magical mists, treacherous slick steps lead to pathways between the statues to tunnels into the bedrock. An investigation to unlock the monument’s secrets is underway, sponsored by Prince Galean Waverider of Tor Koruali who seeks prestige and honour.

Esteemed loremaster Jorielis Handswift of Tor Vael is conducting the archaeological and magical survey, mapping the tunnel network, recording all discoveries and measuring the magical energy of the leyline entering the site. His students mingle with the pilgrims that come to make offerings before a voyage, and some disappear within the mists, an occurrence that often brings an elusive mist weaver to tend the shrine.

There is to be an official exhibition of artefacts and presentation of the theory on the monument’s origins. However, rumours have spread that finds are disappearing, those not in keeping with Prince Galean’s narrative of the site representing members of the Cadai as he hopes to prove the shrine a more wholesome site.

The Bearded One

Taken as a trophy during the War of the Beard, this Dwarf statue was judged by the other kingdoms to be too ugly to stand on the soil of Ulthuan so was dropped into shallow water. Cothiqui youths swim to the edifice, and the navy uses it for bolt thrower target practice. There are a few Elves who wish to petition for its relocation in remembrance of past battle honours, though others would rather forget about the whole conflict.

Statue of Mathlann

Standing at 500 feet, this colossus is located on a conflux of leylines within the Shifting Isles and a focal point for magic that protects the region (see Winds of Magic , page 193). The magical energy that crackles between the points of the gigantic trident draws many scholars. Members of the Scions of Mathlann guard the shrine from those that would tamper with the trident. Merchant adventurers leave offerings to Mathlann upon returning from trading or raiding voyages abroad.

Lake of Tranquillity

In the lowlands of the Annulii Mountains, a dam holds a clear lake that supplies many of the aqueducts with fresh water. The springs that fill the lake are high in the Annulii, and griffons, chimeras, and other monsters that reside there follow the streams down from its peaks. A small garrison is stationed to protect the dam and aqueducts that supply irrigation water to the glass houses of Cothique and drinking water to Tor Koruali.

The Golden Beast

The fishing village of Tor Daroluth is the port where starwood lumber is transported across the bay on barges to Tor Koruali. Tor Daroluth is considered cursed, for in the harbour is an immense golden kraken suspended at its moment of attack. The magic unleashed by Archmage Elandor Goldweaver to halt the sea monster went unchecked and Chamon saturated the surrounding waters, making diving treacherous in the sluggish waters, giving fish golden scales and turning pearls into nuggets of gold. Loremasters of Tor Vael are tasked with cleansing the waters, but others seek to let the kraken loose.

Underground Grottos

Cothique is riddled with tunnels, formed by natural processes or from mining salt and minerals. In times of danger, sanctuary was sought underground, and the caverns were repurposed into shelters or converted for recreation. Swathes of this underworld are still uncharted, and rumours of subterranean spirits and creatures in the depths bring adventurers from afar, while loremasters seek igneous rock rich in quartz for the construction and repair of waystones.

The Vault

Built within a disused quartz quarry, the Vault is a site of great controversy between the Elves of Tor Vael (the closest city) and Prince Dalloran (who owns the land). The reason for the consternation is that the Vault has been used for over two millennia to store and study foreign magical artefacts. The loremasters of Tor Vael carry out the duty of cataloguing the items, but cannot reach consensus on whether their task honours their expertise, or recklessly risks their souls.

Khial’s Gate

The barren ground around the entrance to this cenote is found deep in the Forest of Cothique, warded with sprigs of blackhame, tied to nearby bushes. Perceived to be one of the gateways to Mirai, the Underworld, known as Adir Cynath, only those desperate to commune with the spirits of the dead approach this place. Its shimmering purple waters have unknown depths and are guarded by a hooded devotee to Nethu.

Gorge of Nymphs

At the source of the Whitereach River, an enchanted wooded gorge thrives filled with wondrous creatures and Nymphs, like blessed Avelorn. Elves have long sought sanctuary within the caves that riddle the canyon walls, which amplify the acoustic melodies of song and laughter. Here those seeking asylum are escorted under the mountains, through a treacherous maze of twisting tunnels and a stretch of treacherous underground river, to the kingdom of Avelorn.

Artist Colony

On the outskirts of Tor Koruali is an artist colony sponsored by Princess Trasinaya of Tor Andar, a member of the Hall of Sea Trade. Situated within an old salt mine, its tunnels and caverns are in the process of being transformed into works of art. Some caverns are sculptured to enhance acoustics, others modified into galleries for wall painting or workshops. In truth, the colony is a cover for a smuggling operation.

Caves of Bones

This notorious stretch of coastline is home to numerous littoral caves that reclaim prizes from the sea, with lost sailors and forgotten treasures routinely washed inside. Fishermen, supplementing poor catches, risk being caught by changing tides and becoming lost themselves as they search the labyrinth of caves. The largest cave holds a sea monster carcass, its putrid flesh studied by Mage Kyraelle Palemoon seeking to surpass her mentor, Calaundr, who studied the Scrolls of Kadon and mastered control of monstrous beasts. The warren of caves and tunnels are used by smugglers, and it is rumoured that nefarious Dark Elves have hidden enclaves in their depths.

Cireon Fleetcrest

The Royal Palace is the domain of a host of courtiers and functionaries, each with specific roles devoted to the smooth running and aesthetic balance of courtly life. The Master of the Palace Grounds is no mere gardener. Cireon Fleetcrest bears responsibility for the harmony of the palace grounds and the perpetuation of yenlui throughout the exterior of the buildings. He has an exalted position in the palace hierarchy and a coterie of underlings to do his bidding. Such a role provides Cireon with connections at all levels of Tor Koruali society — it also keeps him close to Cothiqui politicking.

Cireon is overwhelmed by an obsession, and he has given himself over to the prosecution of intrigue. His senior position allows ample time to indulge himself in intricate scheming to advance his favoured causes. He believes that he is bringing balance to disorder, but in truth, he is lost in a labyrinth of his own design, where planning and plotting are more important than the ambition itself. Some of his causes are noble, others less so, but Cireon no longer concerns himself with the ethics of his habits.

Most Elves who know of Cireon see a kindly, softly spoken Elf dedicated to his position in the Royal Palace. A small number of Tor Koruali’s upper echelons recognise Cireon’s obsession and try to enlist him for their intrigues, though they are wise to remain wary in doing so. They keep knowledge of his obsession secret, in fear of what he might do to their reputation.

Cireon’s appearance belies his character. His clothing and appearance are perfectly in line with protocol and his role, and there is nothing engaging about his unremarkable features and neat dark hair — he merely resembles an Elf of over 600 years. He is unfailingly courteous in conversation and displays polite, earnest curiosity about non-Elves in particular. This humble demeanour serves him well — none would suspect him as the puppet master of so many convoluted intrigues. As such, he prefers to use unwitting intermediaries as tools who rarely realise their role in a greater conspiracy.

During the day, Cireon is usually found in the Royal Palace, remaining until sunset. At night, he often watches performances at the Galineth Ampitheatre, dines with acquaintances, or spends time in his elegant home near the palace grounds, writing correspondence.

Cireon’s Intrigues

Cireon strives to advance many causes and personal ambitions. Currently he is trying to advance the following private schemes.

Rivalry with Priolla Silverheart

Priolla is a high-level factotum for Prince Dalloran, who attends to various tasks on behalf of the regent of Cothique. Cireon believes (correctly) that she is intriguing to replace him as Master of the Palace Grounds with her protégé, Aubellan Lightfall. Cireon strives to attack the reputation of both Priolla and Aubellan before they can achieve their aims.

Outer Kingdom Alliance

Cireon belongs to a political faction hoping to elevate the fortunes of Cothique through a closer alliance with Yvresse and Chrace. He offers clandestine support to Princess Litharys of Elisia in making overtures to other Outer Kingdoms. He also plots to undermine the political dominance of Caledor, Eataine, and Saphery through his letters to friends in other kingdoms, letters that contain a variety of poisonous political polemic and ribald gossip. Recently, Cireon has singled out Lady Brelliana of Tor Dynal as a target for his ire, due to her opposition to an alliance with Chrace.

Prince Dalroth and Lady Rivitha

Dalloran’s eldest son is an eligible bachelor. Lady Rivitha is an Elf of great intelligence from Mistnar. Cireon deems them the perfect romantic match, based on his aesthetic rather than political judgement. He plots to arrange a courtship, although both Dalroth and Rivitha have many admirers who Cireon must remove from play, as he simultaneously engineers serendipitous liaisons between the two highborn.

Expel the Humans

Cireon finds Humans unsettling and would see them chased from the isle of Ulthuan once and for all, though in public, he voices quiet enthusiasm for the younger species. He has an ally in Prince Dannor, who considers non-Elves little more than vermin. Cireon works patiently to damage the reputation of a vocal Cothiqui faction advocating for greater alliance with the ylvathoi , particularly Lord Tathar (who favours Humanity) and High Receiver Swiftprow (who he deems too accommodating to non-Elves).

Cireon as Adversary

Cireon presents himself to the Characters tactfully, telling them that he would appreciate their help with tasks made difficult by his responsibilities at the palace. Anything he requests appears innocent or in service of a noble cause. Once Characters are entangled in Cireon’s intrigues, he will betray them in service of a greater scheme, to prove the unworthiness of ylvathoi or to frame Elves who fraternise with non-Elves as traitors. These betrayals are subtle and plausibly deniable, and even if Characters realise what Cireon is doing, they may struggle to prove his manipulations.

Aubellon’s Associates

Cireon needs intermediaries to help ruin Aubellan Lightfall’s reputation. He expresses concern that the younger aesthetician is becoming withdrawn, so he would like the Characters to accompany Aubellan to Tor Dynal. They can keep him company and report back on his movements and behaviour in the city. Aubellan is in fact fairly ebullient company, not withdrawn at all. Cireon is hoping that the grim mood of Tor Dynal will soon make him so.

Revna Tharvig

As a rule, the Norse are brutal and blunt, yet some act with the guile of the Raven God to subvert and infiltrate. Revna Tharvig is one such Norscan, a wanderer who came to Ulthuan to seek weaknesses in the Asur’s defences and discover the whereabouts of the Seven Instruments of Laerial, magical artefacts sacred to Khorne.

Revna was born into the Baersonling tribe, where she learned to hunt and stalk prey in the frozen Norscan forests. When she was captured by an Aesling raiding party, the Bloodfather Alfkaell recognised her stealth and cunning, which spared her from slaughter in the name of Khorne. The Aeslings charged her to prepare the path for a great invasion of Ulthuan, spying on the Elves and uncovering the whereabouts of powerful treasures.

It took the sacrifice of a longship of reavers to land Revna on the shores of Cothique, sunk by hawkships patrolling the Shifting Isles. Now Revna hides in the wilds of Cothique, spying on the Asur to find weaknesses that the Aeslings can exploit. She shelters in a ruined shrine of Meneloth in the woods west of Tor Vael, undetected thanks to her skill and perhaps the attentions of Tzeentch. From here she stalks the land and coastline.

Several months ago, Revna stole an Imperial merchant’s clothes in Tor Koruali. Now she occasionally passes herself off as a Nordlander to walk the streets of the city — very few Elves can see through the disguise, although a real Nordlander or Ostlander might detect the ruse if they pass a Difficult (–10) Intuition Test.

Revna’s pale face is set with piercing eyes, unnerving in their intensity. Her dark red hair is plaited for practicality, and she wears tough leather and furs for warmth and concealment. Her gait is purposeful and efficient, and she speaks bluntly, though only rarely tells the truth.

Motivations

The Norse gods are unforgiving and Revna wants to please them by fulfilling her quest on Ulthuan: finding flaws in the defence of Ulthuan. She seeks physical weaknesses such as isolated coves or poorly-defended coastal settlements, as well as shortcomings in the military defences, hierarchy of command, or fissures in Asur society. She is particularly interested in understanding how the Norse could penetrate beyond the Shifting Isles with a whole fleet.

Norscans tell tales of Laerial, an ancient Elf who turned to the worship of Khorne when the world was young. The Chaos champion Laerial wielded seven items of great power which the Aeslings covet for the conquests they will bring. Revna seeks information of the relics’ whereabouts.

Once she has gathered sufficient information, Revna must return to Norsca. Ideally she will do this as crew on a Human vessel leaving for the Old World.

Revna as Adversary

Revna is alone on Ulthuan and self-sufficient. She can hunt and survive in her forgotten shrine, but she knows she will be overwhelmed and killed in direct conflict — this is not the fate she believes the Raven God intends. If she meets the characters, she will try to acquaint herself with them and bring them into her plans, posing as a merchant or fellow traveller.

If the Characters discover her mission or become a threat, she will use the Asur to rid herself of them. She denounces them to the authorities, plants evidence that they are spies or saboteurs, or suggests they are in league with Dark Elves or pirates.

If cornered, Revna uses her survival skills to pick off as many of the party as she can before they defeat her.

The Legend of Laerial

Amongst the survivors of Aenarion’s war against Chaos, a handful of Elves were corrupted and became champions of the Ruinous Powers. Most notorious is Dechala, the Denied One, but there were others, including the warrior Laerial, who pledged his soul for Khorne and left Ulthuan for the Wastes.

Laerial revelled in slaughter. Legends tell that he and his followers exalted the Lord of Skulls by fighting one another in endless battles. Each leader wielded a powerful magical artefact — the Seven Instruments of Laerial. These are now scattered across the Known World, other than Laerial’s mace, which belongs to the Bloodfather Cormanti. An obscure sect of Norscan priests seek out the remaining instruments, inspired by an inscription on the mace itself.

Message Undelivered

A herald from Tor Koruali to Tor Vael has gone missing. She was carrying scrolls with detailed muster rolls of Cothique’s citizen warhosts, making authorities at the Royal palace concerned. They would rather ask outsiders to investigate than risk embarrassment with the military. The herald lies dead near to the road, several miles from the ruined shrine to Meneloth — Revna killed her, stole the scrolls, and tried to make it look like an animal attack.

Laerial’s Legacy

Several hundred years after Laerial’s demise, one of his descendants documented the tragic events of his life in a short tract to act as a warning to others. Revna has discovered the existence of this document but cannot get into the private library in Tor Dynal where it resides. She needs more scholarly (or at least more urbane) allies to help, and she is willing to lie as much as necessary to secure their services.

Oracles of Mathlann

Rising from the waves, four towering sea stacks stand in front of the Seven Sisters. A tidal causeway leads to a hazardous stairway, climbing the tallest stack to a temple that sits upon its pinnacle. Those seeking guidance are greeted, as though expected, by an Elf maiden. This is the first oracle, who is training to replace the second oracle in time. Beyond the doors, pilgrims enter a circular room brightly lit by braziers, its arched ceiling supported by marble columns. Here, the third oracle, the elder of the three, helps visitors understand the guidance provided.

Periodically, fishermen from Sea Elf houses arrive with catches of sea bream, known as dream fish for their visionary properties. From these catches, a broth is made and served to those seeking guidance. This induces a trance as pilgrims wander through roughly hewn tunnels filled with burning incense and drapes of drifting kelp. The second oracle awaits within the central chamber, sitting within a roiling storm of Azyr . There, she interprets the visions of her visitors.

Dream Fish

The elusive dream fish, or caellin-iasg , is of iridescent blue-green colouring. Its ability to induce visions makes it a staple in Elven religious rites. Cooked with additional ingredients, its makes an illicit sauce called caelain. Rumours suggest this is used as a condiment at high society banquets to induce collective visions.

Should a Character eat food seasoned with the sauce, they must make a Very Hard (–30) Endurance Test or be lost to visions of the future for 10d10 minutes. After the initial effects, a Character gains the Sixth Sense Talent for 1d10 days.

A Change of Tide

The temple has been closed by royal decree. Rumours amongst the fishermen speak of a prophecy concerning the fate of Cothique, sparking both fear and curiosity. Meanwhile, in the shadowy corners of high society are whispers of a new trend, an illicit hallucinogenic fish sauce consumed by the elite. The Characters are approached by a former temple pilgrim who believes the prophecy holds the key to saving Cothique. Meanwhile, agents of Princess Litharys of Elisia ask for help to stop the flow of the sauce, fearing its effects on the elite.

Analdar’s Shrine

To the east of ruined Anirain runs the shallow, sluggish River Esselli. It meanders through a stretch of marshland before oozing into the sea. Those who wade through the reedbeds may find Analdar’s Shrine, a ring of tumbled stones overgrown with briar and nettles, surrounding a hollow pit filled with reeking, maroon swamp water.

Analdar was once a noble of Anirain who won renown fighting alongside Aenarion against the Daemons. Cursed with a wasting disease, she is said to have vanished into the wilds so as not to spread the contagion to her people. Tradition in Cothique has it that Analdar built the small shrine to Drakira, goddess of vengeance, in her dying, delirious days, calling out that her suffering should be inflicted back upon those who caused it.

The story has its doubters, for it is unlikely that an ailing Elf, alone in the wilds, could summon the strength to dig out the pit and line it with stone, yet the legend persists, and those who fear to utter Drakira’s name are happy to refer to her tumbledown temple as Analdar’s Shrine.

The Rite of the Maroon Pool

It is said that if an Elf has been wronged by another, they may wipe their own blood along the blade of a knife and slip the weapon beneath the stinking water of the maroon pool, calling out to Drakira and asking her to strike down their persecutor.

To perform this rite in Cothique today would be considered criminal unless the petitioner had been clearly and publicly wronged by an enemy of Ulthuan. Even so, piratical ship’s captains, the maddeningly bereaved, and those who have fallen into dark obsessions sometimes trudge through the marshes to perform this grim rite.

Should a Character be so reckless as to invoke Drakira in this way, their Yenlui will move to Dark. However, if they bear this knife against the named person, they will find that the weapon loses the Undamaging Flaw and benefits from the following Qualities:

Damaging, Fast, Impale, Practical, Precise.
