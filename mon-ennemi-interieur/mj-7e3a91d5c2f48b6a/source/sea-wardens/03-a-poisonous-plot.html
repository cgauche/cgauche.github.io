A Poisonous Plot

Smuggling Showdown

A terrible threat is looming for Cothique and its citizens. In Naggaroth, the Cult of Khaine has developed a concoction of manbane and daemonic ichor so toxic that a single vial can pollute the waters of an entire river, and the Dark Elf assassin Shadowblade has been dispatched by Crone Hellebron, Blood Queen of Har Ganeth, to poison the Elves of Cothique.

Shadowblade would not rely on smugglers for a supply line, but he has been keeping an eye on them to work out how best to navigate through the Shifting Isles. Many smugglers have seen a small, swift, mysterious vessel following them through the archipelago, but it disappears almost as soon as it is sighted.

The intrigues currently at play in Tor Koruali may provide Shadowblade with the cover he needs to steal onto Ulthuan’s soil and poison its rivers. It could be left up to intrepid adventurers to stop him.

Pertinence: A clue as to Shadowblade’s coming and going to Cothique.

Prince Dalloran’s Allegiance

Fialthra Swiftprow presents Shadowblade with a problem. This powerful archmage is vigilant about the protection of Tor Koruali, collegial towards High Judge Bel-Kalandus Pureheart, and magically sensitive. Shadowblade is in a race against time before Fialthra realises daemonic ichor is present in the city and is being used to drug the high judge. It could play into Shadowblade’s hands if visitors to Tor Koruali were to distract Fialthra by asking for her help in winning the ear of Prince Dalloran.

The Mirkwine Sting

The mastermind of the importation of mirkwine to Cothique is Morathi, the Hag Sorceress of Ghrond. She and Hellebron are bitter rivals, and part of the reason Hellebron has dispatched Shadowblade is that her plans to spread poison and disease will render Morathi’s plot to gradually corrupt the Elves of Cothique a wasted effort.

Whilst the Mirkwine Sting intrigue may lead the Characters to Dark Elf agents, they are Morathi’s minions and know nothing of Shadowblade’s activity. For his part, Shadowblade realises that getting entangled will compromise his own mission and leaves them well alone.

Pertinence: To get his plot moving, Shadowblade needs to ensure Fialthra’s attention is focused elsewhere. He might recommend that the Characters seek Fialthra’s advice in getting to know Prince Dalloran. He would prefer to do this at some remove, using a go-between such as Herwig Algnar, but if this does not work, he might approach them and make the suggestion himself.

Pertinence: Even though this intrigue has direct Dark Elf involvement, it is irrelevant to the poisonous plot, but may serve Shadowblade as an effective distraction.

Withering Starwood

Prince Dalloran’s Udgement J

Shadowblade is confident that he is stealthy and clever enough to avoid detection from the High Elves, but even he cannot move through the wilds without coming to the attention of Spites. That there is growing tension between local Spites and the Elves of Tor Koruali suits him. He does what he can to ensure that the situation continues to degenerate, and anyone who seeks reconciliation between the Elves and the Spites may become a target.

Shadowblade knows that to transport daemonic ichor to Ulthuan himself would risk exposure, as the substance can be detected by those who have magical sight. Instead, he leaves transport of the stuff to an agent named Caranath Bloodblade, who believes the ichor is merely the prime ingredient in the addictive substance he peddles to High Judge Bel-Kalandus.

Caranath expects that one day an assassin will be sent to end his life, and he is reconciled to the fact, knowing that the sacrifice of his own life may well be necessary to ensure the long-term victory of the Druchii over their hated kin. He does meditate on his mortality and makes regular but fleeting visits to Tor Dynal to pray at the shrines of Morai-Heg that can be found there. Like Shadowblade, Caranath masquerades as a merchant adventurer, Allerion Seastrider from Nagarythe, to provide cover for his comings and goings. His cover suits his personality, for many Elves of Cothique expect their neighbours from Nagarythe to be sullen and brooding.

Pertinence: If the Spites are alienated, they will be reluctant to make a report of suspicious Elves in the wilds or take steps to prevent Shadowblade from accomplishing his mission.

Leyline Dispute

Herwig Algnar suspects that Dark Elf subterfuge lies behind the leyline dispute. This is a false impression, as Herwig has only a basic understanding of the trouble between High Elves and Dark Elves and so tends to believe that any problem that rises to trouble the High Elves must have Dark Elf mischief behind it. The Sea Elf sailor who raised these suspicions with Herwig was Shadowblade, wearing a disguise. He is hoping that anyone worried about Dark Elf plots wastes their energy investigating this rumour.

Shadowblade does not want the Characters to realise that High Judge Bel-Kalandus is compromised. If Characters are spending time in the company of the high judge, he seeks to end their acquaintance. To resort to straightforward violence would be a vulgar process, and though Shadowblade is ready to resort to it, he would prefer to use a way that does not risk leading to his own exposure. He thinks it would be better to have an intermediary settle any legal problems for the Characters before they get to know the high judge.

Pertinence: Depending on their focus on this intrigue, the Characters may enable Shadowblade’s plans if they convince more important High Elves to expend their energies chasing the false rumour. If, on the other hand, they seek to track down Herwig’s source, it may prove a serious risk to Shadowblade’s mission.

Pertinence: To protect his supply of ingredients, Shadowblade must ensure the high judge does not lead anyone to Caranath. If Caranath is apprehended, Shadowblade acts fast to acquire the vials of daemonic ichor and kill anyone who bears witness. He will also have no compunction about slaying Caranath if he feels the agent has been compromised.

Caranath’s habit of taking brief trips to Tor Dynal from time to time may be used by the GM to buy investigators more time (as any plots in motion may have to wait until he returns), give them a clue (as Elves in Tor Koruali may begin to gossip about his strange and morbid behaviour), or complicate matters for them (if they think to trail Caranath to Tor Dynal they may notice that he is a devotee of Morai-Heg, but this is not enough to mark him out as anything other than a gloomy eccentric).

A POISONOUS PLOT

Framework of the Plot

Shadowblade moves slowly and subtly. He takes as much time as he needs for the heat to die down and his schemes to ripen, but he is not so foolish as to let a suitable opportunity pass him by.

The following schedule assumes Shadowblade is inspired to act following the arrival of a group of visitors to Tor Koruali. These are folk from the Old World who are there to forge a new connection with Prince Dalloran, maybe as trade envoys, or seeking an alliance, or merely wishing to explore the wilds of Cothique. If the party happen to meet this description, so much the better.

Groundwork

When not going equipped for assassination, Shadowblade adopts the guise of a young merchant adventurer, Farrion Farseeker. From his small sailing boat, he hunts kraken in the Shifting Isles and runs errands for traders and travellers. He recently arrived from his home of Ellyrion, which explains his eccentricities of manner and habit.

As Farrion, Shadowblade has made a few acquaintances in the Grey Oak, Hall of Trade, and taverns by the harbour. He has not left much of an impression on people, being rather unremarkable in his appearance and behaviour. Farrion has made the acquaintance of Herwig Algnar and informed him of a rumour that Dark Elves have an interest in waystones being sold to Marienburgers, but was careful to remain vague about details. For his part, Herwig cannot recall Farrion’s name or a description beyond him being of average height for an Elf, dressed in blue and purple travelling clothes, with long blond hair.

First Move

Before Shadowblade makes his next move, it would be best for him if Fialthra Swiftprow were to be distracted. Fialthra would make a good contact for anyone wanting to gain the ear of Prince Dalloran, so if they do not think of it themselves, Shadowblade tries to get the Characters interested in speaking to Fialthra and diverting him as much as they can. Should Characters ruin things by getting into trouble, Shadowblade may try to make things easier for them, particularly if he thinks doing so will prevent the exposure of the high judge. Shadowblade does not murder anyone as conspicuous as a prince or an archmage, but he might arrange for the disappearance of Cireon Fleetcrest should he prove difficult.

Trouble with Spites

The situation between the local Elves and Spites may cause Shadowblade to delay his next steps. He would prefer this situation to get as bad as possible and will seize the opportunity to act quickly if the Spites are alienated. If things improve, that may make things difficult, but Shadowblade will persevere nevertheless.

Second Move

Shadowblade will seek to keep anyone from discovering Caranath Bloodblade until he is ready to make his second move. He and Caranath meet in a grotto beneath Tor Koruali. Shadowblade acquires the Daemonic ichor and murders Caranath. If Fialthra Swiftprow is not busy with the Characters, Shadowblade leaves Tor Koruali right away. If the Characters prove to be an effective source of distraction for the archmage, Shadowblade can continue to bide his time.

Final Move

Shadowblade strikes out for the Annulii Mountains and the source of Cothique’s rivers. If relations between the Elves and the Spites are cordial at this point, Spites will seek to impair his progress whilst also warning the Elves of Tor Koruali that a lone Elf travels the land in possession of a poison concocted with Dhar .

Poisoning

Shadowblade infects the sources of rivers with the poison. Over the next two months, any creature who drinks from a river or nearby well throughout the kingdom of Cothique must Test for exposure to the Crippling Plague.

Crippling Plague

The Crippling Plague results from a reaction between poisonous manbane, daemonic ichor, and water, based on research carried out by the Cult of Khaine. It is a dangerous, debilitating disease that is resistant to cures. Any Test made in order to cure the disease, whether mundane, magical, or miraculous, suffers from –3 SL.

Contraction: If you fail a Hard (–20) Endurance Test after drinking from an infected water supply.

Incubation: 1d10 hours

Duration: 5d10 days

Symptoms: Flux (Severe), Malaise, Nausea, Wounded

Shadowblade

Shadowblade is still young, being a mere 150 years old. His reputation, however, is already legendary. Stories of his grisly adventures are used to scare Dark Elf children. His most famous exploit was the murder of the crew of a High Elf hawkship, one by one, over a period of several days. Only the horribly mutilated captain was left alive so that he could tell his kin of the mounting horror on the ship as the crew desperately attempted to corner Shadowblade, to no avail.

Shadowblade reports directly to Crone Hellebron, and it is rumoured that she has used him to eliminate several of her political opponents. The truth of such stories is difficult to substantiate because no one has seen Shadowblade’s face and lived. He slew his master when he had learned all that he could, and even Hellebron is not certain about what he looks like. Dark Elves must be careful about what gossip they share, and some of those who have been loose-lipped about Shadowblade’s supposed exploits have met with sudden, violent ends.

In the hold are five bales of fine Grand Cathayn Silk worth 100 GC each, a small chest of pearls from Miragliano worth 250 GC, and a chest containing spare clothing (a secret compartment at the base of this chest contains Shadowblade’s standard trappings and 20 gold dragons).

A POISONOUS PLOT

Potion of Diabolic Strength

This potent magical brew is a noxious concoction of troll blood, chimera bile, and the heart of a griffon. Crone Hellebron has perfected the process by which these potions are made, and gifts them to her favoured servants.

On drinking a Potion of Diabolic Strength, Shadowblade gains +60 Strength for 1d10 + 3 Rounds.

The potions are made with the Elf constitution in mind, and so are harmless, but unpleasant and ineffectual if consumed by other species. Furthermore, Hellebron makes the potions in such a way that they will quickly denature into toxic sludge if Shadowblade does not make efficient use of them. If a potion is acquired from Shadowblade in some way, the GM should make a secret roll of 2d10. After this many days, the potion spoils, and anyone imbibing it must pass a Hard (–20) Endurance Test, taking a Poisoned Condition for each –SL.

Heart of Woe

This large ruby is a magical gem that throbs and beats like a living heart. Should Shadowblade die, the crystal shatters into thousands of jagged shards, slaying those responsible for his death.

Dark Venom

Even the slightest scratch from a blade coated in dark venom can become a life-threatening injury. When a weapon coated with dark venom causes a Wound, the injured character must take a Difficult (–10) Endurance Test. Those who fail immediately experience agony as the injury inflames, suppurates, and bleeds. This is treated as a Festering Wound ( WFRP , page 187) save that the Incubation is instantaneous.

Gifts of Khaine

These abilities are part technique, part religious rite, and part strict regimen. For the purpose of an NPC follower of Khaine, a Gift of Khaine can be used as a Free Action provided that character is behaving in such a way Khaine would approve of (sincerely seeking to kill another individual), regularly worships Khaine and sacrifices to him each Death Night (as all Dark Elf assassins and Witch Elves do), and spends a point of Fortune.

Dance of Doom

A character using this ability performs a weaving dance during melee combat. For the rest of the Turn, they may add +2 SL to any Dodge Tests they make.

Hand of Khaine

If Shadowblade is killed, the Round after his demise, the Heart of Woe explodes with the same effect as a Bomb (see WFRP , page 295).

Rending Stars

These light, curved throwing weapons are commonly employed by Dark Elf assassins and can be thrown further than most throwing weapons. Their mass is too small to inflict more than minor injuries, but thanks to Shadowblade’s black lotus and Potions of Diabolic Strength, they can become deadly weapons in his hands.

A character using this ability can choose to trace a complex pattern in the air as their Move, hoping to enthral an opponent and leave them stupefied. This opponent must be able to see the character. If so, an Opposed Perform (Dancing)/Cool Test is made. If the character succeeds on this Test, their opponent receives the Surprised Condition.

Touch of Khaine

A character using this ability is inspired by Khaine as they strike in melee. For the rest of the Turn, they may add +2 SL to any Melee Tests they make.
