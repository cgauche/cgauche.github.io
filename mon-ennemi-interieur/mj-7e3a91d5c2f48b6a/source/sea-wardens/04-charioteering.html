# <a class="line-anchor" id="L1"></a>Charioteering

<a class="line-anchor" id="L2"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L3"></a>*Pages PDF 76-80*

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L4"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L5"></a>Charioteering
<a class="line-anchor" id="L6"></a>The High Elves are one of several cultures that use chariots in warfare, with the Elves of Tiranoc being particularly associated with chariot riding. Many cultures employ various sorts of carts and wagons in war, such as the armoured war wagons built by Empire engineers or strange forms of artillery pulled by rhinoxen in Ogre mercenary forces. Chariots differ in that they are not primarily designed to transport heavy loads into battle or provide a mobile form of fortified position. They are fighting platforms from which a small number of warriors can fight, benefiting from some protection from the carriage and the impetus of their galloping draft animals. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L7"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L8"></a>**Carries:** This refers to the number of Encumbrance points the vehicle can comfortably fit in its cargo area. In the case of crew, a creature of _Little_ Size counts as 2 Enc, a creature of _Small_ Size counts as 5 Enc, a creature of _Average_ Size counts as 10 Enc, and a creature of _Large_ Size counts as 30 Enc. Items worn or carried by a character do not count against the Carries limit of a chariot unless they are very bulky and weighty. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L9"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L10"></a>**Motive Power:** This refers to the number of creatures needed to pull the vehicle. When determining whether a creature is encumbered when pulling or pushing a wheeled vehicle, the combined Encumbrance of the vehicle, its occupants, and its cargo is reduced to one tenth (this number is then shared between the team). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L11"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L12"></a>These rules are adapted from those given for vehicles in the **Enemy in Shadows Companion** . You will not need to use that book to use chariots in combat, though it may help to refer to it if you use a chariot to travel a long distance. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L13"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L14"></a>**Cost:** This is the basic price a cartwright might ask for if asked to build a chariot. Consider that many cultures and species regard chariots as foreign or outdated curiosities. A cartwright in the Empire may well take money to build a chariot, but the resulting creation will likely be flawed. Orcs, Goblins, and Beastmen are exuberant builders of chariots, though their creations are rickety and ramshackle. The Elves of Ulthuan and Naggaroth do construct chariots, and take the commission to build one seriously, using fine materials, and the resulting creations are often much more expensive. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L15"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L16"></a>These rules can also be used in conjunction with carts, wagons, coaches, carriages, and other vehicles in combat. Chariots, which are designed to be manoeuvrable in battle, are treated as the default for these rules. If other vehicles are used to do the job of a chariot, the GM should consider moderating these rules or imposing significant additional penalties to Tests.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L32"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L33"></a>**Speed of a Chariot**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L34"></a>The speed of a chariot dictates not just how far it moves, but also the degree to which it inflicts damage on those struck by it. However, the faster a chariot moves, the harder it is to perform Manoeuvre Tests.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L46"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L47"></a>**Attacks on Chariots**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L48"></a>Given that a chariot cannot dodge or move of its own accord, melee attacks made against a stationary chariot automatically hit. If the chariot is moving, or a creature wishes to attack a specific, difficult-to-hit spot, count the attack as a Called Shot at –20. If the driver of a moving chariot is attacked in melee, resolve this as an **Opposed Melee** Test, but the driver may use their _Drive_ Skill if they wish. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L49"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L50"></a>Ranged attacks against chariots are resolved as normal. Chariots are generally _Large_ targets, so the Difficulty of hitting them is Average (+20). However, bullets, arrows, and bolts typically do no appreciable damage to the wood and canvas construction. At the GM’s discretion, Called Shots against fragile components such as the vehicle’s harness may still trigger one of the special effects, described below. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L51"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L52"></a>Vehicles have a _Toughness_ Characteristic, which determines their Toughness Bonus, and a number of Wounds, which function in exactly the same way as any other target in combat. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L53"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L54"></a>Vehicles cannot suffer Critical Wounds, but if a Called Shot to one of the locations below scores a Critical, then the described effect occurs. Furthermore, if the vehicle is in motion when a Critical Hit is suffered, the driver must make a **Hard (–20) Manoeuvre** Test to retain control. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L55"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L56"></a>**Fighting Platform**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L57"></a>The attack hits the part of the vehicle where passengers stand, smashing a sizeable hole in the vehicle. Half the attack’s Damage is also inflicted on a random passenger inside the chariot. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L58"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L59"></a>**Harness**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L60"></a>The shafts are shattered, the traces cut, or the team is separated from the vehicle in some other way. Drive Tests suffer from –4 SL from now on. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L61"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L62"></a>**Wheels**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L63"></a>A wheel breaks or falls off, requiring repairs before the vehicle can proceed any further. If the chariot was moving when this occurred, it is treated as a _Crash_ result on the **Manoeuvre Test Results** table. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L64"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L65"></a>**Chariots Striking**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L66"></a>In battle, chariots are sometimes used as weapons in and of themselves, as the vehicle is driven into (or over) the enemy. This is most effective when faced with massed ranks of troops who have little room to take evasive action, but even in smaller skirmishes, a chariot may be used to smash into an opponent who fails to get out of the way. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L67"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L68"></a>When a chariot strikes an enemy, it inflicts Damage as listed in the Strike column of the vehicle profile. Instead of using SB to determine the Damage, the chariot’s M is used. This is determined by the distance the chariot moves in the Turn it strikes — not the maximum it could move if it was unimpeded. By default, 1 M is equal to a rate of 2 yards per Turn. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L69"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L70"></a>The mass of a chariot also plays a large part in determining how much Damage it inflicts, with smaller vehicles having a Strike value of half their M (rounding down). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L71"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L72"></a>Chariots also possess the _Undamaging_ Flaw when used to strike an enemy. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L73"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L74"></a>**Wheel Scythes**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L75"></a>Some charioteers are so determined to inflict maximum carnage that they fit blades or spikes to the axles of their chariot. A chariot fitted with wheel scythes receives the following benefits. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L76"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L77"></a>Strikes made by the chariot lose the _Undamaging_ Flaw. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L78"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L79"></a>_Impact_ Strikes made by the chariot benefit from the Quality. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L80"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L81"></a>Add +2 SL to successful Trample and Crush manoeuvres (see page 79).

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L94"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L95"></a>**Drivers**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L96"></a>Most chariots require a driver, though exceptions may exist in the cases of intelligent bearers or draft animals who voluntarily carry or pull the fighting platform. The driver directs the draft animals, and there may only be one driver at a time. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L97"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L98"></a>At the start of any Round, the chariot is assumed to be travelling in the same direction and at the same speed as it did the previous Turn. In the driver’s Turn, they may choose one of the following courses of action. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L99"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L100"></a>**Maintain Heading:** The chariot continues in the same direction and at the same speed. The driver does not need to focus on driving alone and may take an Action like other characters. No Manoeuvre Test is required. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L101"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L102"></a>**Simple Manoeuvre:** The chariot continues in the same direction and at the same speed. After it has moved, the driver can use their Action to turn up to 90 degrees, bring the chariot to a halt, or increase the speed of the chariot. No Manoeuvre Test is required. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L103"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L104"></a>**Haul on the Reins:** The driver can use their Move and Action to bring the chariot to a sudden halt. Other occupants of the chariot must pass a **Challenging (+0) Athletics** Test or gain the _Prone_ Condition. No Manoeuvre Test is required. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L105"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L106"></a>**Quick Manoeuvre:** The driver can use their Move and Action to make an **Average (+20) Manoeuvre** Test to turn up to 90 degrees or alter the speed of the chariot before the chariot moves. If the Test succeeds, the chariot performs the manoeuvre and moves to the new heading and at the desired rate. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L107"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L108"></a>**Complex Manoeuvre:** The driver can use their Move and Action to make a **Difficult (–10) Manoeuvre** Test to have the chariot perform a U-turn, or jink past oncoming obstacles. If the Test succeeds, the chariot performs the manoeuvre and moves to the new heading and at the desired rate. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L109"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L110"></a>**Combat Manoeuvres**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L111"></a>As chariots are designed to be employed in battle, drivers can use them to inflict harm on opponents. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L112"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L113"></a>Because a chariot’s driver makes these actions during their Turn, any characters on board the chariot who can make an attack because of the manoeuvre may do so outside of their normal Combat Initiative. This represents them either waiting for their chance (if they are normally quicker) or benefitting from the momentum of the chariot (if they are normally slower). 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L114"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L115"></a>Note that these exceptions only occur in cases where the driver pulls off a combat manoeuvre. If the chariot is engaged in combat in any other circumstances, the normal Initiative order applies.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L118"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L119"></a>**Trample and Crush**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L120"></a>The driver can use their Move and Action to bring the chariot to bear on an enemy and drive right at them, hoping to knock them down and crush them underneath the hooves of the horses and wheels of the chariot. This is worked out in the following way: 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L121"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L122"></a>The driver makes a **Challenging (+0) Manoeuvre** Test to guide the chariot directly towards an enemy. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L123"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L124"></a>If this Manoeuvre Test is successful, retain the SL, adding a further +2 SL if the chariot is equipped with wheel scythes. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L125"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L126"></a>Use the total SL to work out the result of an **Opposed Drive/Dodge** Test against the target. If the target cannot move for some reason, treat their score on the Test as if they had rolled 00.

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L155"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L156"></a>**Hit and Run**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L157"></a>A benefit of fighting from the back of a chariot is that the bulk and the momentum of the vehicle means it may engage and then disengage with an opponent without incurring the usual risks. Using this manoeuvre, a driver can use their Move and Action to bring the chariot close to an opponent, allowing passengers to strike, and then drive away rather than getting bogged down in combat. This is worked out in the following way: 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L158"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L159"></a>The driver makes a **Challenging (+0) Manoeuvre** Test to guide the chariot directly past an enemy. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L160"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L161"></a>If this Manoeuvre Test is successful, any chariot crew who have not made an Action this Round may make a melee attack against the target as if they had Charged into combat. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L162"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L163"></a>If the target has a Combat Initiative that is equal to or higher than that of the driver, they may strike back against their choice of draft animals, chariot structure, or chariot crew. If it is lower, they may not. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L164"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L165"></a>The chariot may continue to move. This counts as Disengaging rather than Fleeing. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L166"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L167"></a>Skycutters and Tidecutters
<a class="line-anchor" id="L168"></a>The Asur employ a form of chariot known as a Skycutter, which is enchanted to hover in the air. The chariot is usually pulled by a swiftfeather roc, a gigantic bird of prey. The merchant adventurers of Cothique also often take to the seas in Tidecutters, similar craft that are pulled by giant fish that skip and skim over the surface of the water. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L169"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L170"></a>Both craft count as Light Chariots, but they can only be flown through the air or pulled across the water, respectively. Despite this, Drive Tests are still necessary to control or manoeuvre the vehicles. Neither of these vehicles are particularly useful for fighting in melee the way chariots can be. Tidecutters are more often used to scout the seas and carry small quantities of cargo, whilst Skycutters often mount a small bolt thrower to assist in skirmishing actions. Any crash that results from damage or mishap on board these vehicles carries the additional risk of falling from the sky or being lost at sea. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L171"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L172"></a>A Tidecutter’s asking price is 40 CG. Skycutters are rare and magically enchanted military assets, and to commission one would mean tracking down a specialist artificer and parting with no less than 500 GC. And this is before factoring in the cost of acquiring, training, and keeping a swiftfeather roc or flying fish. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L173"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L174"></a>**Fighting Platforms**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L175"></a>A fighting platform refers to a flat and stable surface upon which a rider of a chariot may stand and fight. Using a fighting platform confers many of the benefits that are conferred from mounted combat. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L176"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L177"></a>Whatever the nature of the platform, it confers the following advantages: 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L178"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L179"></a>For the purposes of their Move, riders count as having the _Movement_ Attribute of the creatures carrying or drawing the platform. Drivers take Drive Tests for any Tests to Sprint and use the draft animals’ Movement Attribute. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L180"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L181"></a>Any melee attack from a rider on a target smaller than their platform gains a bonus of +2 SL. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L182"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L183"></a>If you are rolling to hit a mounted character, you choose whether to hit the rider or the mount. If you are in melee, you also suffer a penalty of –10 to your Melee Test if you target the rider and are smaller than the mount (see **Size** in **Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay** , page 162). 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L184"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L185"></a>Draft animals with the _Trained (War)_ Trait are effectively extra combatants and may use their own Action to attack opponents. The Size rules of the mounts are used when calculating Damage _Fear_ or _Terror_ , 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L186"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L187"></a>of these attacks. They may cause and gain other combat advantages as explained in **Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay** , page 341. 

- <a class="line-anchor" id="L188"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L189"></a>When riding in a chariot, you suffer a –20 penalty to use the _Dodge_ Skill unless you have the _Trick Rider_ Talent. 

## <a class="line-anchor" id="L190"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L191"></a>**Death of a Draft Animal**
<a class="line-anchor" id="L192"></a>Should one of the creatures pulling the chariot be killed or incapacitated, the chariot may not move. Any surviving creatures are too encumbered by the corpse, and even if it is cut free, the chariot is not designed to be pulled by an under-strength team. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L193"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L194"></a>Should an animal be killed whilst the chariot is in motion, the driver of the chariot should make a **Challenging (+0) Drive** Test to bring the vehicle to a safe stop. If the creature is killed during a Turn before the driver of a chariot has a chance to declare an Action, it counts as moving as fast as it did the previous Turn. 

<a class="line-anchor" id="L195"></a><a class="line-anchor" id="L196"></a>The results of the Test are outlined in the table below. The term occupants is used to refer to any creature riding the chariot, pulling the chariot, or riding creatures pulling the chariot. Any creatures who gain the _Prone_ Condition no longer benefit from any protection or bonus associated with being on a fighting platform.
